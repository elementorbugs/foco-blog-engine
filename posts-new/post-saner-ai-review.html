It is strongest on a computer, has a real free plan, and costs $8 or $16 a month on its website.

  • Try Saner.AI if your problem is information scattered across Gmail, Slack, Drive and notes, and you work mostly in a browser.

What does Saner.AI actually do?

Saner.AI is a second brain with an assistant attached: one place where your notes, tasks and messages live, so you don't have to remember which app something is in. We did not test it hands-on; the images below are Saner.AI's own, and the details come from its site, store listings and pricing page.

On a computer, you write or paste a note on one side and talk to Skai on the other. Ask it to set something up, and it turns the request into a task with a time and a reminder, and points out related notes you already have.

Its inbox gathers to-dos from Gmail, Slack and your own notes, ranks them by priority, and offers one-tap actions like "Set a reminder" and "Break it down." The app store listings say tasks can also be pulled out of emails and documents automatically.

The feature Saner leads with in its screenshots is recall. You can ask "what did I promise Alex and forget?" and Skai answers from your meeting notes, with links to the notes it used.

You capture by typing, voice-to-text, the Chrome extension, or uploading files and PDFs, and its pricing page lists OCR for text inside images. Notes get suggested tags and folders, so you don't have to file them yourself.

Is Saner.AI built for ADHD?

Yes, by its own positioning. Its homepage calls it an "AI Personal Assistant for ADHDers," its store listings describe it as built for "ADHDers" who are entrepreneurs and knowledge workers, and its Google Play listing says the founders tried "countless" note and task apps before building it.

Its focus is narrower than most ADHD apps, though. It targets information overload at work, not routines, emotions or self-care, so it fits a professional juggling inboxes better than a student or parent planning a day.

What does Saner.AI get right for ADHD brains?

It offloads working memory, the mental notepad that drops things so easily with ADHD. Instead of remembering where a detail lives, you ask for it (our guide to working memory and ADHD explains why that load matters).

It cuts app-switching. Saner's pitch is "no more switching between five apps," and each switch is a chance to get pulled into something else; that is a common pain point we cover in ADHD and remote work.

It accepts messy input. You can dump half-formed thoughts and let the AI sort them, which is the core of a good ADHD brain dump.

And it catches dropped threads. An inbox that flags "you haven't replied to this email for 3 days" is an outside reminder for things that otherwise vanish from view.

What do users say about Saner.AI?

Reviews are split by platform. Its Chrome extension holds 4.5 stars, and early users on launch sites praise the AI search and chatting with their notes.

The mobile apps get much harsher reviews: 3.0 on the US App Store and 1.9 on Google Play. Recent Google Play reviews complain that the assistant can be slow to respond, that tasks sometimes land in notes instead of the task list, and that onboarding on mobile is unclear (Saner.AI on Google Play).

A December 2025 thread on Reddit's r/PKMS, a community for note-taking systems, started with a user calling Saner "pretty slow at joining the dots" and unable to edit notes it had created. These are individual reports, not a test, but they line up with the gap between its web and mobile ratings.

Where does Saner.AI fall short?

The mobile experience lags the web. With 14 App Store ratings and 82 on Google Play, the sample is small, but the low scores and specific complaints suggest it is best treated as a browser-first tool.

The free plan is tight. Thirty AI messages a month runs out quickly if Skai is the reason you signed up, so plan to test the paid tier during the trial.

Organizing is not starting. Saner can tell you exactly which task matters most and even break it down, but a ranked list can still sit untouched; that gap is task initiation, explained in our guide to task initiation deficit.

It also leans on the tools you already use. If your work doesn't run through Gmail, Google Calendar, Drive or Slack, much of its value disappears.

Is Saner.AI safe for private notes and email?

Saner.AI says it does not train on, sell or share your data, encrypts data in transit and at rest, limits staff access, and keeps daily backups with an export option. That is a reasonable baseline for a tool that reads your inbox.

Connecting work email and Slack to any third-party AI tool may still break your employer's policies, so check before linking a work account. We did not audit Saner's security ourselves.

FAQ

Is Saner.AI good for ADHD?

It can be, especially for knowledge workers whose information is scattered across email, Slack and notes. It is weaker on mobile and doesn't help you start a task once it's planned.

Is Saner.AI free?

Yes, Saner.AI has a free plan with 30 AI messages a month, 100 notes and 100MB of storage. Paid plans start at $8 a month on its website.

How much does Saner.AI cost?

Starter is $8 a month and Standard $16 a month on its website, with 20% off yearly. In the US App Store they cost $12 and $20 a month.

Is Saner.AI on Android and iPhone?

Yes. Saner.AI has iPhone, iPad and Android apps plus a web app and Chrome extension, though its mobile apps are rated much lower than its extension.

What is Skai in Saner.AI?

Skai is Saner.AI's built-in assistant. It searches and summarizes your notes, answers questions about them, plans tasks and can break them into steps.

Does Saner.AI train AI on my data?

Saner.AI says it does not train on, sell or share your data, and that data is encrypted in transit and at rest. Check your employer's rules before connecting a work account.
