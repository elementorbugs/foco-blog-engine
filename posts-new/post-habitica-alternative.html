Why do people look for a Habitica alternative?

Usually because the same game loop that rewards you also punishes you. Habitica turns tasks into an RPG: you earn XP and gold for Habits, Dailies and To-Dos, and missed Dailies or negative Habits cost your character health points (HP).

When HP hits zero, your character "dies." According to the Habitica wiki, you come back with full HP but lose a level, all of your gold and one random piece of equipment.

For some people that is just a mechanic. For many ADHD adults, a missed task already comes with enough self-criticism, and a falling health bar can turn one bad day into a second failure, especially with rejection sensitivity in the mix.

There is also a scope gap: Habitica rewards tasks you have already decided on, but it has no tool for the moment before that, when a task feels too big or vague to begin. That is task initiation, a different problem from staying consistent.

To be fair, Habitica is genuinely generous. Its App Store listing says it "can be fully enjoyed for free," and its optional subscription runs $4.99 a month or $47.99 a year in the US (prices may vary by location).

What are the best Habitica alternatives for ADHD?

1. FOCO, best for starting a task you're stuck on

We make FOCO, and it sits first only because its job is the one Habitica skips: getting from frozen to step one. AI breaks a stuck task into small steps, each with a time estimate, and a focus timer with calm music and an on-screen body-doubling companion keeps you company while you work.

There is no HP and no character to lose. In FOCO's built-in calendar, missed tasks move on instead of piling into a red overdue wall.

The honest trade-off: FOCO does not gamify long-term habits the way Habitica does. If streak motivation is what you need, pair it with one of the trackers below.

Price: 7-day free trial, then $9 a month or $59 a year. iPhone and Android, no web app (checked Oct 9, 2026).

2. Finch, best for reward without punishment

Finch pairs small self-care goals with a virtual pet bird that grows as you complete them. Its store listing frames everything as rewards and celebrating wins, with goal tracking, a mood tracker and guided journaling.

It leans toward wellbeing tasks like drinking water, resting or journaling rather than work projects. That makes it narrower than Habitica, but a lot softer on a bad day.

Price: free to download and use, with an optional Finch Plus subscription. The US App Store lists several Finch Plus price points, so check the price shown in the app (checked Oct 9, 2026).

3. Streaks, best for a simple chain with no game

Streaks tracks up to 24 tasks you want to do each day and shows how many days in a row you've kept each one going. It can also mark tasks done automatically from Apple Health, like steps or water.

A missed day resets the streak, but nothing else happens: no HP, no lost gear. It is Apple-only, so it won't help on Android.

Price: $5.99 one-time on the US App Store, no subscription. iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch and Mac (checked Oct 9, 2026).

4. TickTick, best for habits and tasks in one plain app

TickTick puts a habit tracker next to its to-do list, calendar and Pomodoro-style focus timer, with no avatar or game layer. You check a habit off or you don't.

If you used Habitica mainly to keep habits and tasks in one place, TickTick does that job without the RPG. The trade-off is depth: lots of settings can become their own form of avoidance, which our TickTick alternative guide covers.

Price: free plan available; Premium is $4.99 a month or $49.99 a year on the US App Store, and TickTick's upgrade page also lists $49.99 a year (checked Oct 9, 2026).

5. Todoist, best for the plainest possible recurring list

Todoist has no dedicated habit tracker, but a recurring task does the same job for people who don't want streaks, avatars or charts. The task just comes back on schedule.

For some ADHD adults, dropping the tracking layer is a relief rather than a loss. There is less to look at and nothing visibly falling behind.

Price: free Beginner plan; Pro is $7 a month or $60 a year since a December 2025 price change (Todoist help center) (checked Oct 9, 2026).

Who should stay with Habitica?

  • You genuinely enjoy the RPG loop, and leveling up gets you moving.
  • A party or boss quest makes you show up for other people, and that accountability works for you.
  • The penalty only bothers you during rough stretches. Habitica's Pause Damage setting (formerly "Rest in the Inn") stops missed Dailies from costing HP, though the wiki notes party members' missed Dailies can still hurt you during a boss quest.

FAQ

Is there a free alternative to Habitica?

Yes. Finch, TickTick and Todoist all have free versions, and Habitica itself is free to play.

What is the best Habitica alternative for ADHD?

It depends on what went wrong. Finch or Streaks if the penalty stressed you out, TickTick or Todoist for plain habit tracking, and FOCO if you can't start the task at all.

What happens when you die in Habitica?

Your HP refills, but you lose a level, all of your gold and one random piece of equipment. You can buy the lost item back with gold.

Can you turn off damage in Habitica?

Yes. The Pause Damage setting stops missed Dailies from costing HP, although party boss quests can still damage you.

Is Habitica free?

Yes, Habitica says it can be fully enjoyed for free. Its optional subscription costs $4.99 a month or $47.99 a year on the US App Store.

Does Habitica help with task paralysis?

Not directly. It rewards tasks you have already defined, but it has no feature for breaking down or starting a task you're frozen on.
