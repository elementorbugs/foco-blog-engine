one sec App Review: Does a Pause Beat an App Blocker?

one sec is a screen time app that adds a short pause, like a deep breath, before a distracting app or website opens, so you choose whether to continue. It is free for one app, with one sec pro for more, and runs on iPhone, Android and as a browser extension on Windows, Mac and Linux.

  • Try one sec if you open Instagram or TikTok on autopilot and still need those apps sometimes.
  • Skip it if you want a hard lock you cannot click past, though one sec has strict blocks too.
  • Pick FOCO, the app we make, if the phone is down and you still cannot start the task, and you want it split into small timed steps.

What does one sec look like in use?

We did not test one sec hands-on. The screens below are its official App Store screenshots, so they show the app as the developer presents it.

Open Instagram, and one sec shows "It's time to take a deep breath" first, then counts how many times you tried to open it today. It can also ask why you are opening the app, with choices like "Search for info" or "Distraction."

For harder limits, strict blocks run on a schedule, such as a phone-free morning or a "work tunnel." It also connects to other apps, like blocking distractions while a task in Structured is unfinished.

Does one sec actually work?

There is more research behind it than behind most screen time apps. one sec lists a 2023 study in Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences by Grüning, Riedel and Lorenz-Spreen, and says app usage "drops by 57% on average."

Frederik Riedel, one sec's developer, is a co-author of that study, so it is not fully independent. one sec also lists a 2024 study at the CHI conference and a 2025 field experiment by Denmark's Competition and Consumer Authority.

The idea behind it is simple: a few seconds of friction interrupts a habit loop. Procrastination often works as quick mood repair, as Sirois and Pychyl argue, and the pause gives you a moment to notice you are escaping.

Is one sec good for ADHD?

It can be. ADHD is linked to differences in executive function, including stopping an impulse, as Russell Barkley's fact sheet explains.

A pause puts a small gap between the urge and the scroll.

Its developer writes that "especially for people with ADHD one sec is a great tool," but that is the maker's view, not a study. Because it delays rather than locks, it also suits people who need social apps for work or family.

It does not help you start a task once the phone is down. That is task initiation, which our guide to task initiation deficit explains.

Where might one sec fall short?

Free covers one app: Using one sec with more than one app needs one sec pro.

Setup on iPhone: one sec uses Shortcuts automations to show its pause, so setup takes a few extra steps compared with a simple blocker.

A pause can be skipped: If you are very determined, a breath is easy to wait out. For a lock you cannot click past, use its strict blocks or a physical tool like Brick.

Computers use the browser: On Windows, Mac and Linux, one sec works through a browser extension, so it covers websites rather than desktop apps.

Is one sec free?

Yes, for one app. "Using one sec with one app of your choice is completely free," its App Store listing says, and the Safari extension is free too.

For more apps, one sec pro comes as an annual, monthly or lifetime purchase, and its website says you "pay once, use on all your devices." The App Store listing does not show pro prices, so check them in the app.

Is one sec safe?

Its App Store privacy label lists only usage data, marked as not linked to you. one sec says its intervention logic "runs locally on your device," with "no profiling, no data selling."

FAQ

Is one sec free?

Yes, for one app of your choice, plus a free Safari extension. More apps need one sec pro, which comes as an annual, monthly or lifetime purchase.

How does one sec work?

When you open an app you chose, one sec shows a short pause, like a deep breath or a question about why you are opening it. You can then continue or close the app.

Is the one sec app safe?

Its App Store privacy label lists only usage data not linked to you, and one sec says its logic runs locally with no data selling.

Does one sec really reduce screen time?

one sec cites a 2023 study in PNAS and says app use drops by 57% on average. Its developer co-wrote the study, so it is promising but not fully independent.

Does one sec work on Android and computers?

Yes. one sec runs on iPhone, iPad and Android, and as a browser extension on Windows, Mac and Linux, with one purchase covering all your devices.

Is one sec better than ScreenZen?

They are similar: both pause you before an app opens and offer strict blocks. ScreenZen is free with optional tips, while one sec is free for one app with a paid pro tier.
