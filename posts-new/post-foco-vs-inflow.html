Short answer: Inflow is the better choice if you want to learn how ADHD works for you, through a structured CBT-based program with a community, live focus rooms and coaching. FOCO is the better choice if you already know what to do and need help starting one task today, with timed steps and a focus companion, for less money.

  • Pick Inflow if you are newly diagnosed, want lessons and skills practice, or want people (peers, experts, a coach) in the loop.
  • Pick Inflow if CBT-style learning and a community matter more to you than a planner.
  • Pick FOCO if your problem is starting, not understanding, and you want each step timed and planned into your day.

What do the two apps look like in use?

Inflow describes itself as a program you can follow in about 5 minutes a day, built on cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) principles and developed with clinical psychologists (Inflow on Google Play). Its modules cover focus, time management, emotional regulation, impulsivity, phone distraction and rejection sensitivity.

Tap into a step and FOCO opens a focus session: one step on screen, a time to start, a soundscape and the companion sitting with you.

Which is better for understanding your ADHD?

Inflow, clearly. FOCO has no lessons, no community and no coaching, so on this layer it is not competing.

Inflow's CBT framing treats ADHD as a set of patterns you can learn to work with, not a character flaw. The National Institute of Mental Health lists psychotherapy, including cognitive behavioral therapy, among the treatment options for adults with ADHD.

Inflow is not therapy, and it says so: it calls itself a support tool and educational program, not a medical device. For a newly diagnosed adult, though, a structured way to learn about emotional regulation, time and avoidance can be worth a lot (see our guide to emotional regulation and ADHD).

Which is better when you can't start a task right now?

FOCO, for most people, because starting is the one job it is built around. Inflow can help here too, so this is closer than it used to be.

Inflow's AI companion, Quinn, can break a big project into steps on request, and its live focus rooms offer body doubling with other people. Those are real tools for task initiation, and if working next to humans helps you, that is a point for Inflow.

FOCO puts the task first: say or type it, get small steps with minutes on each, then press start on step one. Psychologist Russell Barkley describes ADHD as a problem of using what you know at the moment it matters, more than a knowledge gap (Barkley fact sheet), which is the moment FOCO targets.

If you freeze even when the steps are clear, our guide to task initiation deficit explains why that happens.

Which gives you more human support?

Inflow, by a wide margin. FOCO's companion is an AI on your screen, not a person.

Inflow lists an in-app community, live events with ADHD experts, and a personal coach from its coaching team (Inflow, how it works). Inflow does not publish a separate price for coaching on its site, so ask before you sign up if that is the part you want.

If you mainly want a human coach, our list of ADHD coaches compares more options.

What does each one cost?

Inflow: a 7-day free trial, then $47.99 a month or $199 a year for the plan with coaching, according to Inflow's FAQ (getinflow.io FAQs). Inflow also offers an affordability program and a student discount, and the price shown in the app at checkout is the one that counts.

FOCO: a 7-day free trial, then $9 a month or $59 a year. Neither app has a permanent free plan.

Inflow costs more, and it also includes more: lessons, community, live rooms and coaching access. If you will use those every week, the gap makes sense, and if you only want help starting tasks, you would be paying for parts you skip.

Who should pick which?

Choose Inflow if:

  • You are newly or late diagnosed and want to understand your patterns.
  • You like learning in short daily lessons built on CBT ideas.
  • You want a community, live body doubling rooms or a coach.
  • You will engage most days, so the subscription earns its price.

Choose FOCO if:

  • You already understand your ADHD and the problem is starting step one.
  • You want every step timed, planned into your day, and a focus timer with a companion on screen.
  • You are fine with a paid app after a 7-day free trial ($9 a month or $59 a year), on iPhone or Android.

FAQ

How much does Inflow cost?

Inflow's FAQ lists $47.99 a month or $199 a year after a 7-day free trial. It also offers an affordability program and a student discount.

Is Inflow free?

No. The app is free to download, but the program needs a subscription after the 7-day trial.

Does Inflow include coaching?

Inflow's site describes a personal coach from its coaching team, plus live events with ADHD experts. It does not publish a separate coaching price, so check in the app.

Is Inflow the same kind of app as FOCO?

No. Inflow is a CBT-based program for learning to manage ADHD, while FOCO is a planner built for starting and finishing specific tasks.

Can I use FOCO and Inflow together?

Yes. Some people use Inflow to learn skills and FOCO to start the day's tasks, and the two do not conflict.

Is Inflow a replacement for therapy?

No. Inflow calls itself a support tool and educational program, not a medical treatment, and the same goes for FOCO.
