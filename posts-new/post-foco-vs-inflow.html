What is Inflow?

Inflow is a CBT-based ADHD coaching app built with clinical psychologists, closer to "therapy in your pocket" than a productivity tool, though it isn't therapy and shouldn't replace one.

The core format is short daily lessons, around 5 minutes, covering topics like task avoidance, managing emotions, time management, and chunking tasks. Inflow's own marketing cites development input from researchers at the University of Richmond and Syracuse University.

Beyond the lessons, Inflow includes a community forum and optional 1:1 coaching add-ons. For adults newly diagnosed or working in isolation, hearing other adults describe the same patterns, perfectionism, masking, exhaustion, does psychological work no productivity app replicates.

It runs roughly $15-20/month with a free trial. It is explicitly not a task management app.

What is FOCO?

FOCO doesn't teach lessons or host a community. It's built for one moment: you know what to do and cannot start it, what clinicians call task initiation deficit.

You enter one stuck task, FOCO breaks it into small first steps with time estimates, and stays with you through them with a timer and soft check-ins.

There's no lesson to complete before it works, and no forum. It's a narrow tool for a narrow, mechanical problem.

Which is better for starting a task right now?

FOCO, and it's not close, because Inflow isn't built for this at all.

If you have 30 minutes and a report you know you need to write, Inflow doesn't have the right tools. Its lessons are educational, meant to build understanding over weeks, not to unstick a specific task in the next ten minutes.

FOCO is built for exactly that moment. You open it, enter the task, and the breakdown starts immediately with no lesson to sit through first. The Russell Barkley executive function model frames ADHD as weaker self-regulation in the moment of doing, not a knowledge gap, which is exactly the layer FOCO targets and Inflow's lesson format doesn't.

Which is better for understanding your ADHD?

Inflow, decisively. This is the one dimension where FOCO isn't trying to compete.

The CBT framing treats ADHD as a pattern to work with, not a character flaw to overcome with willpower. For adults who carried decades of internalized "I'm just bad at this," that framing alone can be worth the subscription. The community adds a layer no solo productivity app can, hearing other adults describe the same struggles does real psychological work.

According to the National Institute of Mental Health, cognitive behavioral therapy is part of evidence-based ADHD treatment for adults. Inflow's format is built around that same approach, delivered in small daily doses.

FOCO does not offer this. It has no lessons, no community, and no coaching layer.

How does the pricing compare?

FOCO's manual task tracking is free; the AI breakdown that does the actual step-splitting is $9/month or $59/year.

Inflow has no permanent free tier, only a free trial before its $15-20/month subscription. That's a real ongoing cost, and it requires consistent engagement (lessons, community, coaching) to be worth it.

If you'll engage with Inflow's lessons twice a month, you're paying for an unused subscription. If you'll do the daily 5 minutes, the price buys something FOCO doesn't offer at any price: structured coaching content and community.

When should you pick Inflow?

Pick Inflow when any of these are true.

You're newly diagnosed and still processing what ADHD means for you. The lessons and community are built for exactly this stage.

You want to understand your patterns, not just get through today's task list. Inflow works on the "why," not the "how do I do this right now."

You'll commit to daily engagement. The format rewards consistency; sporadic use wastes the subscription.

When should you pick FOCO?

Pick FOCO when any of these are true.

You already understand your ADHD and just need to do the thing. No lesson will get today's report written.

Your problem is starting, not understanding. FOCO is calibrated for the specific mechanical moment of task initiation.

You want to try manual task tracking at no cost first. FOCO's AI breakdown is $9/month or $59/year if you decide you need it.

FAQ

Is Inflow the same kind of app as FOCO?

No. Inflow is CBT-based coaching content with lessons and community. FOCO is a task-breakdown and initiation tool. They address different layers of adult ADHD and aren't really direct competitors.

Does Inflow have a free plan?

No permanent free tier, only a free trial before the $15-20/month subscription. Verify current pricing on Inflow's own site before subscribing.

Can Inflow help me finish a task today?

Not directly. Inflow's lessons build understanding over time rather than unsticking a specific task right now. For that, a task-initiation tool like FOCO is built for the immediate moment.

Is FOCO a replacement for therapy or coaching?

No, and neither is Inflow. Both apps describe themselves as scaffolding alongside, not a replacement for, clinical care. If ADHD significantly affects your daily life, a clinician experienced in adult ADHD is the right first step.

Can I use FOCO and Inflow together?

Yes. Inflow for the educational and community layer, FOCO for the specific task you're stuck on. They don't integrate, but they don't conflict either.

The bottom line

Inflow and FOCO aren't really fighting for the same use case. Inflow helps you understand and process ADHD over weeks. FOCO helps you start a specific task in the next ten minutes.

If you're newly diagnosed and still making sense of what ADHD means for your life, Inflow's lessons and community are worth the subscription.

If you already know your ADHD and just need today's task done, that's what FOCO is built for, and you can start free.
