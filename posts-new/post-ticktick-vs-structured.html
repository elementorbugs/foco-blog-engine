What do the two apps actually look like?

We did not test TickTick or Structured hands-on for this comparison. The screenshots below are each company's official App Store images, and the feature details come from their listings, pricing pages, help centers and release notes.

Structured puts the whole day on one vertical line. Every task is a colored block with an icon, a start time and a length, so a busy morning looks busy.

TickTick opens on your lists and builds outward: calendar views, a priority matrix, reminders, habits and widgets, all in one app.

Which one is better for ADHD?

It depends on what breaks down for you. If your days fall apart because you can't feel time passing, Structured fits better; if they fall apart because things live in ten places, TickTick does.

Structured's App Store listing says it is built "for ADHD minds, busy professionals, and students," and its screen shows very little besides the day. TickTick is a general productivity app that many ADHD users rely on, with far more on every screen.

Both try to take load off executive function, the brain skills behind planning, prioritizing and holding steps in mind (our executive function guide explains more). What counts is whether you still open the app on day 10.

Which helps more with time blindness?

Structured, for most people. Time blindness, trouble sensing how long things take or how much time has passed, is exactly what a timeline makes visible (our time blindness guide goes deeper).

In Structured, a 30-minute task is a taller block than a 15-minute one, and you can see the gap before your next meeting. Its Energy Monitor also lets you score tasks by how draining they are and warns you when a day is overloaded (Structured help: Energy Monitor).

If you want time blocks beside your real calendar instead, see our Ellie Planner review.

TickTick can show tasks as blocks in time too, but on the free plan the calendar is basic. Unlimited calendar views and the Timeline view are listed on its Premium page (TickTick Premium).

Can either app break a big task into steps?

Yes, both can, in different ways. You can add subtasks by hand in both apps for free.

Structured's smart subtasks use Apple Intelligence to suggest steps for a task, which needs an iPhone 15 or newer on iOS 26 or later (Structured help: smart subtasks). Its Pro plan adds Structured AI, which drafts or reshuffles your day from a typed or spoken request.

TickTick added an AI Assistant in October 2026 that can create tasks and break down projects from a single instruction, according to its App Store release notes. Its pricing page lists AI features but does not say which plan includes them, so check in the app before you count on it.

Which is better for routines and habits?

TickTick. Its habit tracker is built in, with 5 habits on the free plan and more on Premium.

Structured shows routines beautifully on the timeline, but recurring tasks are a Pro feature (What is Structured Pro?). On the free plan, a morning routine means adding the same blocks again each day.

Which is better for staying focused once you start?

It is close. Both have a free Pomodoro-style timer, so neither forces you into a second app.

TickTick's Pomo timer comes with white noise and can sync focus time to Apple Health. Structured keeps the current task in Live Activities and widgets, so the countdown sits on your lock screen.

Which is cheaper?

It depends on what you need. Structured's free version covers more of its core, the timeline, while TickTick's free plan is broader but capped at 9 lists and 5 habits.

TickTick Premium is US$49.99 a year on its pricing page. Structured Pro is monthly, yearly or a one-time lifetime purchase, and Structured says the price depends on your region; on the US App Store its Pro purchases are listed from $2.99 to $99.99.

Does either app help you start a task you're avoiding?

Partly. Both help you plan, and a timeline or a short list takes some weight off the first step.

But both start from organizing. If the plan is already fine and you still freeze, that is task initiation, the gap between knowing what to do and doing it.

What are the downsides of each app?

TickTick: its range can turn into a hobby. Tags, smart lists and matrix quadrants can feel like progress while the actual task waits, and its richer calendar views sit on Premium.

Structured: it is built around one day, so long-term projects and big backlogs are not its strength. Repeating routines need Pro, and Structured says some iOS features are still missing on Android and web (Structured help: platform availability).

FAQ

Is Structured or TickTick better for ADHD?

Structured is better if you need to see time; TickTick is better if you need everything in one system. Neither is built specifically around starting a task you're avoiding.

Is Structured free?

Mostly. Structured says almost every feature is free, and Pro adds Structured AI, recurring tasks, calendar import and custom notifications.

Is TickTick free?

Yes, TickTick has a permanent free plan with 9 lists, 99 tasks per list and 5 habits. Premium is US$49.99 a year.

Does TickTick have a timeline like Structured?

TickTick has calendar and Timeline views that show tasks as blocks over time, with the fuller views on Premium. It does not center the whole app on one day's timeline the way Structured does.

Can Structured sync with TickTick?

Structured does not list a TickTick integration. Structured Pro can import your calendars and Apple Reminders, so the usual workaround is a shared calendar, which adds a step that is easy to let slip.

Does Structured work on Windows?

Not as a Windows app. Structured runs on Apple devices, Android and the web, so on a Windows PC you would use the web version; TickTick has a native Windows app.
