Short answer: Numo is an ADHD app built around community: a gamified to-do list with points, short lessons with memes, and small groups of other ADHD adults who cheer each other on. It suits people who get going when others are watching, and it is a paid subscription after a free trial.

  • Try Numo if you are motivated by people, points and a bit of humor, and you want ADHD content that doesn't feel clinical.
  • Skip it if you want a quiet, private planner, or social feeds pull you away from the task.
  • Pick FOCO if you already know the task and the hard part is starting it, and you want each step timed with a focus companion on screen.

What does Numo look like in use?

The screenshots below are Numo's official App Store screenshots, not our own. We did not test Numo hands-on, so we describe what the listing shows and what Numo says the app does.

Your Today list is a plain to-do list with a twist: every finished task earns Karma points that raise your level. Tasks can be private or shared with your group.

The Team tab is where Numo is different. Your Squad, a small group of ADHD adults, sees what you finished and how you feel, and can react or comment.

A Discover tab collects community hacks and questions, and a Learn section offers short, meme-heavy lessons on topics like procrastination and time management. Numo's site adds that brain dumps and a focus timer also live in the app (Numo's site).

Is Numo built for ADHD?

Yes. Numo calls itself an app made by ADHD adults for ADHD adults, its CEO describes herself as an "ADHDer," and every part of the app, from lessons to groups, is framed around ADHD.

Its approach leans on accountability: other people seeing your progress. That is a real lever for many people with ADHD, the same idea behind body doubling, where someone else's presence makes it easier to stay on task.

What does Numo get right for ADHD brains?

It makes progress visible and social. A point for every task and a group that notices can supply the quick reward that a far-off deadline doesn't, which ties into how executive function struggles to run on distant goals (see Russell Barkley's fact sheet).

It also cuts shame. Seeing others post the same stuck moments, from unwashed dishes to late diagnoses, tells you this is ADHD, not a character flaw.

And it is unusually open about evidence. Numo publishes a 2024 study by psychologist Olha Orel and lists its limits itself: no control group, no random assignment, users grouped after the fact, and a gift certificate at the end (Orel, 2024).

In that study, the 53 adults who used Numo nearly every day for 90 days reported fewer ADHD symptoms on a standard rating scale. Because of the design, it shows an association in daily users, not proof that the app caused the change.

Where does Numo fall short?

A social feed is also a distraction. For some people, checking the Squad turns into scrolling, and a busy feed is one more thing to manage on a hard day.

Points and lists don't solve the first step. If a task is too big or too vague to begin, ticking off smaller ones for Karma can feel productive while the real one waits; that gap is task initiation, explained in our guide to task initiation deficit.

Its own study points the same way: the benefit showed up in near-daily users, and the 10 occasional users saw little change in quality of life. If you tend to drop apps after two weeks, plan for that.

Finally, ratings are split. Numo holds 4.4 on the US App Store but 3.2 on Google Play, so Android users may want to read recent reviews first (Numo on Google Play).

How much does Numo cost?

Numo is free to download and sold as a subscription. Its App Store description lists $7.99 a month with a free 30-day trial for new users in the US.

The App Store also lists other plans, from $9.99 and $15.99 a month to $29.99 a quarter and $59.99 for six months or a year, including a yearly plan with a 14-day trial. The offer you see may depend on when and where you sign up, so check the paywall before you start a trial.

FAQ

Is the Numo app good for ADHD?

It can be, especially if community and points keep you going. It works best with near-daily use, and its social feed can distract some people.

Is Numo free?

Numo is free to download, but full access is a subscription. New US users get a free trial, 30 days according to the App Store description.

How much does Numo cost?

The App Store description lists $7.99 a month in the US, and other listed plans run up to $59.99 for six months or a year. Check the paywall for your current offer.

Is Numo on Android?

Yes. Numo is on Google Play as well as the App Store, where it is rated 3.2 compared with 4.4 on the US App Store.

Who makes the Numo app?

Numo is made by Mindist, Inc., a small team from Ukraine whose CEO, Julia, has ADHD herself.

Is there research on Numo?

Numo publishes a 2024 study in which daily users reported fewer ADHD symptoms after 90 days. It had no control group or random assignment, so it is early evidence, not proof.
