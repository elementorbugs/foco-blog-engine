What is Brain.fm and how does it work?

Brain.fm is a music app built for focus, relaxation, meditation and sleep. You pick a mental state and an activity, press play, and it streams instrumental tracks with no lyrics and no ads.

STEP 1

Pick a mental state

Focus, relax, meditate or sleep.

STEP 2

Choose the task

Deep work, creative flow, or study and reading, plus a genre like lofi or classical.

STEP 3

Set the strength

Low, Medium or High "neural effect," and an infinite, timer or interval session.

What makes it different from a Spotify focus playlist is amplitude modulation: fast, regular changes in volume layered into the music. Brain.fm says these pulses nudge brain activity toward a focused rhythm, and that its human-composed tracks are processed with patented technology.

It is not binaural beats, which play a slightly different tone in each ear. Brain.fm's pricing FAQ answers that question with a plain "No."

Does Brain.fm actually work? What the science says

There is one peer-reviewed study behind it, and it found real effects on attention in a lab. It is also small, and two of its authors worked for Brain.fm, so treat it as a promising start rather than proof.

The study, published in Communications Biology in October 2024, ran four experiments with music supplied by Brain.fm. Here is what it did and didn't show:

What it found

  • 83 online listeners did better on an attention task with modulated music, when it came early in the session
  • In 34 people in an fMRI scanner, it raised activity in attention networks
  • Beta-range pulses helped more for people with more ADHD symptoms

What it didn't show

  • ADHD was measured by a self-report scale (ASRS), not diagnosis
  • No test of real work over weeks
  • One author is a Brain.fm employee with equity options; another was an employee

The authors disclose those ties in the paper's competing interests section. Brain.fm also says its research was funded by a National Science Foundation grant (STTR #1720698).

Bigger numbers on its marketing, like "increase focus by up to 10x" and a 119% boost in beta brainwaves, come from the company. We couldn't match them to independent research, so read them as claims.

Is Brain.fm good for ADHD?

It might help, especially if silence feels too empty and songs with lyrics pull you away. Brain.fm calls itself "the first music app designed for ADHD," and its High setting is labelled for people who "need extra stimulation, or have attentional challenges (ADHD or similar)."

The idea fits what many people with ADHD describe: a little steady background stimulation makes it easier to stay on one thing. ADHD involves differences in executive function, the brain skills that hold attention on a task and filter out distractions (NIMH).

Two cautions. Some users find High too intense; one Trustpilot reviewer described it as "like a horror movie," and the app itself suggests Low for people sensitive to sound.

Music also helps you keep going once you've started, not start in the first place. If your problem is the blank moment before a task, read about ADHD task paralysis, and see our guide to music for ADHD for what to play.

Is Brain.fm legit?

Yes, it's a real product that has been around since 2015, with a 4.5 App Store rating and over a million Android downloads. Its Trustpilot score tells a different story, at 2.6 out of 5 from 120 reviews.

Most of those low reviews are about billing, not the music: being charged after cancelling or after the trial, and slow refunds. The company replies to many of them, and some reviewers updated their rating once support fixed it.

FAQ

Is Brain.fm free?

No. Brain.fm has a free trial with full access, then costs $14.99 a month or $99.99 a year; there is no free plan.

How much does Brain.fm cost per month?

$14.99 billed monthly, or about $8.33 a month on the $99.99 yearly plan, per its pricing page on October 29, 2026. Students can ask for 20% off.

Is Brain.fm binaural beats?

No. Brain.fm adds fast volume pulses (amplitude modulation) to the music, and its FAQ says this works differently from binaural beats.

Does Brain.fm work for ADHD?

A 2024 study found its modulated music helped attention more for people with more self-reported ADHD symptoms, but it was small, in a lab and co-written by Brain.fm staff. It isn't an ADHD treatment, so use it alongside the care a clinician recommends.

Is Brain.fm legit?

Yes, it's a real app with a 4.5 App Store rating and over a million Android downloads. Its Trustpilot score is 2.6, mostly from billing and cancellation complaints, so note when your trial ends.

How do I cancel Brain.fm?

On the web, cancel from Account Overview or email support@brain.fm. If you subscribed in the App Store or Google Play, cancel in your phone's subscription settings.

What is the difference between Brain.fm and FOCO?
