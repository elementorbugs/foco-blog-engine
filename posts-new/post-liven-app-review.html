What is the Liven app?

Liven is a self-improvement app built around an AI coach called Livie. Its App Store listing calls it "your AI life coach for stress and anxiety relief," with 10-minute daily sessions on emotions, boundaries and self-image.

Most people meet it through a short-video ad and a personality-style quiz on theliven.com, not through the App Store. That quiz-first funnel is why "is Liven legit" gets searched thousands of times a month.

What does Liven look like in use?

The screens below come from Liven's official App Store screenshots, not our own. We did not test Liven hands-on, so we describe what the listing shows and what Liven says the app does.

The home screen is a path of short sessions, grouped by theme, such as "Your Body Speaks First" and "Anger or Hurt?" Later sessions unlock as you go, with a daily streak to bring you back.

Role Play lets you rehearse a conversation before you have it: saying no, protecting a boundary, asking for a raise or repairing after a fight. You can also type one line and Livie builds a custom scene.

A journal turns what you write into a "map of your patterns," and a Phrasebook collects the exact words to use next time. Breathing exercises and calming sounds round it out.

Livie is the center of the app. You can talk or type about anything, from a fight to a deadline, or follow a guided 5-minute session.

What do Liven users say?

The star average is high: 75.5% of Liven's US App Store ratings are 5 stars and 7.4% are 1 star. Recent written reviews are far more split.

We read the 500 most recent US App Store reviews returned by Apple's public review feed, posted June 6 to October 30, 2026. Of those, 234 gave 4 or 5 stars, 45 gave 3 stars and 221 gave 1 or 2 stars.

Is Liven legit or a scam?

Liven is legit in the sense that matters most: it is a real, working app from a registered company, with tens of thousands of satisfied ratings. We found no regulator action or fraud finding against it.

The "scam" reviews come from billing, not from a fake product. Liven sells mainly through its website, and web subscriptions are billed and renewed outside the App Store, so cancelling in one place can leave the other running.

Liven's own support replies on Trustpilot confirm this: subscriptions "renew automatically unless canceled," web subscriptions "are managed separately from the App Store," and "deleting the app itself does not stop recurring payments." One App Store reviewer cancelled in the app, got a confirmation email, and was still billed for nearly a year on a second, web subscription.

Brainway, another procrastination app sold through a quiz, draws the same kind of billing complaints; see our Brainway app review.

How much does Liven cost?

It depends on where you buy. The US App Store lists in-app options of $7.99 a week, a yearly plan at $49.99, $59.99 or $89.99 (the $89.99 one comes with a trial), a $99.99 lifetime plan and a $19.99 workbook bundle (Liven on the App Store).

Web prices from the quiz are not published and seem to change by offer. Reviewers report about $38 to $40 per 4-week period after a cheap first period, and one said Liven offered to drop it to $8 when they tried to cancel.

Before you pay on the web, screenshot the checkout page with the renewal price and period. If a 4-week plan renews at full price, that is roughly $500 a year.

Is Liven free?

No, not in any useful way. The app is free to download, but the session path, the coach and the rest of the program sit behind a paid plan.

How do you cancel Liven and get a refund?

Cancel where you paid, and if you are not sure, check both places. Deleting the app cancels nothing.

  1. Bought in the App Store: iPhone Settings, tap your name, then Subscriptions, then Liven, then Cancel Subscription.
  2. Bought on Google Play: Play Store, your profile, Payments and subscriptions, Subscriptions, Liven, Cancel.
  3. Bought on theliven.com: in the app go to Settings, then Help and Support, then Membership, per Liven's support replies, or email support@theliven.com and keep the reply.
  4. Check your bank for a second recurring charge the next month, and set a calendar reminder a few days before any renewal.

On refunds, Liven's support replies say its Money-Back Policy applies to the initial billing period, and renewal charges are generally not refunded. For App Store purchases, request a refund from Apple at reportaproblem.apple.com.

Several reviewers say Liven did refund them after they wrote in, so it is worth asking, with dates and screenshots. In the US, recurring online charges are covered by the FTC's negative option rules and ROSCA (FTC negative option rule), and your card issuer can open a dispute if a merchant won't stop billing.

Is the Liven quiz an ADHD test?

No. The quiz personalizes Liven's sales pitch and plan; it is not a diagnosis, and Liven's own listing says the app is "not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any health conditions."

ADHD is diagnosed by a clinician, and the CDC notes there is "no single test to diagnose ADHD" (CDC on ADHD diagnosis). If an ad or quiz made you wonder about ADHD, that question is worth taking to a doctor, not a checkout page.

Does Liven work for ADHD?

Liven isn't designed for ADHD. Its listing never mentions it, even though some of its short-video ads lean on ADHD and procrastination framing (Aftermath).

Where it can help is the emotional side. ADHD often comes with intense, fast-moving feelings, and practicing what to say before a hard conversation fits what we cover in ADHD emotional regulation and rejection sensitive dysphoria.

Where it falls short is follow-through. One reviewer with ADHD expected "prompts, reminders, and direction" and found "ZERO," and a local newspaper review of Liven's procrastination plan said the lack of reminders "appears to be by design" (Citrus Heights Sentinel).

The evidence base is also thin for this whole category. A 2025 systematic review found ADHD apps "may be useful as adjunctive tools" but need longer-term validation (Frontiers in Public Health), and we found no published trial of Liven itself.
