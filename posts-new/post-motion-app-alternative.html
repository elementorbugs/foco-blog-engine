Where Motion's automation breaks down for ADHD

Motion analyzes your tasks, deadlines, meetings, and availability, then auto-slots tasks into your calendar and reshuffles them in real time when something changes. For ADHD adults who freeze at the decision of "what do I do first," that removal of choice is genuinely valuable.

The catch is what the automation depends on: reasonably accurate time estimates for each task. Time blindness, the difficulty ADHD brains have sensing how long something actually takes, is exactly the input Motion needs most and the one ADHD adults are worst at supplying.

Underestimate consistently, and the schedule Motion builds stops matching your real day, which erodes trust in the tool faster than a plain list ever would.

There's a second issue that's newer: Motion has expanded from a focused auto-scheduler into a broader "AI SuperApp," adding AI Chat, Docs, Wiki, and Sheets. Some users who chose Motion specifically for its narrow, focused job now find themselves managing a bigger surface than they signed up for.

There's also no permanent free tier, only a 7-day trial that requires a card upfront, which makes it a harder tool to simply try without commitment.

5 Motion alternatives worth trying

1. FOCO, when the problem is starting, not scheduling

Motion decides when you'll do a task. It doesn't help if, when that slot arrives, you still can't make yourself begin. That's a different problem, and it's task initiation deficit, not a scheduling gap.

FOCO breaks one stuck task into a small first step and adds body doubling, so starting happens with a present companion instead of alone. There's no calendar to manage and no time estimates to get wrong.

Best for: the moment a perfectly scheduled task still doesn't get started when its block arrives.

Pricing: free to start, with a paid Pro tier.

Honest take: FOCO won't auto-arrange your week the way Motion does. If you genuinely need that, pair it with a scheduler and use FOCO purely for starting.

2. Reclaim.ai, for auto-scheduling at a lower price

Reclaim works on the same core idea as Motion: it books focus time, habits, and breaks automatically around your existing calendar, and reshuffles when meetings shift.

It keeps a normal calendar at the center rather than a separate scheduling layer, and it costs meaningfully less than Motion, with an actual free Lite tier.

Best for: people who want Motion's core auto-scheduling idea without the price or the expanding feature set.

Pricing: free Lite plan; paid plans start around $10/month.

3. Sunsama, for a guided ritual instead of full automation

Sunsama doesn't fully automate scheduling the way Motion does. Instead it walks you through a daily planning ritual and pulls tasks in from your other tools, keeping a human in the loop rather than handing the whole day to an algorithm.

For people who don't fully trust an AI to arrange their day, or who found Motion's automation opaque, that guided middle ground can feel steadier.

Best for: people who want structure but still want to see and approve the plan, not have it fully decided for them.

Pricing: no free tier; around $22/month, less billed annually, after a trial.

4. TickTick, if AI scheduling was never actually the point

If what you liked about Motion was just having tasks, a calendar, and a timer in one place, TickTick does that without an AI layer deciding your day for you.

It has its own risk, tagging and configuring can become a substitute for doing the task, covered in our TickTick alternative guide, but it avoids Motion's dependency on accurate time estimates entirely.

Best for: people who wanted consolidation, not automation.

Pricing: strong free plan; Premium around $36/year.

5. Todoist, for the simplest possible list

If Motion's automation, or any scheduling logic at all, feels like more machinery than you need, Todoist strips everything back to a fast, plain task list.

No auto-arranging, no time estimates to feed an algorithm, just capture and a "Today" view.

Best for: people whose real problem is forgetting tasks, not arranging or automating them.

Pricing: a genuinely usable free plan; Pro around $4/month billed annually.

How to pick the right one

Don't start from the app. Start from what actually stopped Motion from working for you.

You get the task scheduled but still can't start it? That's initiation. Go with FOCO.

You liked the auto-scheduling but not the price or scope creep? Reclaim.ai.

You want structure but not full automation? Sunsama.

The AI layer was never the appeal? TickTick if you want habits and a timer too, Todoist if you want just a list.

Many people combine two: a scheduler or plain list to organize, FOCO for the tasks that don't get started even once they're on the calendar. See our how to focus with ADHD guide for the underlying techniques.

FAQ

Is Motion worth $19/month for ADHD?

If your bottleneck is deciding when to do things and you can give it reasonably accurate time estimates, yes, the automation is genuinely useful. If your estimates are consistently off or you don't start tasks even once they're scheduled, you're paying for a problem it doesn't solve.

What is the best Motion alternative for ADHD?

It depends on what you needed. For starting a task, FOCO. For similar auto-scheduling at a lower cost, Reclaim.ai. For guided but human-approved planning, Sunsama. For no AI layer at all, TickTick or Todoist.

Is there a free alternative to Motion?

Yes. FOCO, Reclaim.ai (Lite), TickTick, and Todoist all have usable free tiers. Motion itself only offers a 7-day trial requiring a card upfront.

Does Motion help with task initiation, not just scheduling?

No. It decides when a task happens on your calendar, but has no feature for breaking down a task or helping you begin one you're avoiding once its time slot arrives.

Why did Motion get more complicated recently?

It expanded from a focused auto-scheduler into a broader "AI SuperApp" with AI Chat, Docs, Wiki, and Sheets. Some users who wanted the original narrow tool now manage a larger feature set than before.

The bottom line

Motion solves a real problem, deciding when to do things, but it depends on time estimates ADHD brains struggle to give accurately, and its scope has grown beyond that original job.

Pick by your actual bottleneck. If it's starting a task once it's scheduled, FOCO addresses that specifically. If it's the automation you want at a better price, Reclaim.ai. If it's wanting a human-guided plan, Sunsama. If AI scheduling was never the point, TickTick or Todoist.
