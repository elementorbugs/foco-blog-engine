    Why does this specific order break task paralysis?

    Most advice to "just start" fails because it skips straight to action while the brain is still stuck on a decision. Task initiation deficit lives in that gap between deciding and doing, not in a lack of motivation.

    This checklist works because each step removes exactly one barrier before asking for action: calming the nervous system, narrowing the choice, shrinking the size, and finally making starting itself low-stakes with a short timer.

    Research published in JAMA (Volkow et al., 2009) points to differences in dopamine reward circuitry in ADHD brains, part of why a distant goal motivates less than an immediate, tiny, achievable one.

    What are the 7 steps?

    StepWhat it does
    1. Stop & BreatheCalms the panic response that makes everything feel urgent and impossible at once
    2. Pick ONE TaskRemoves the decision paralysis of choosing among competing priorities
    3. Make It SmallerShrinks the task to a 2-10 minute action, not the whole project
    4. Choose the First StepNames the exact physical motion, not a vague intention
    5. Set a 10-Minute TimerMakes the commitment feel small and reversible, not open-ended
    6. Start Before You Feel ReadyAction creates the motivation, not the other way around
    7. Celebrate StartingReinforces that showing up is the win, not just finishing

    FAQ: ADHD Task Paralysis Reset Checklist

    Is this checklist free?

    Yes, both the interactive version on this page and the PDF are free.

    Do I need to do all 7 steps every time?

    No. Some people only need steps 1-3 to get moving. Use whichever steps actually break the stall for you.

    What if I finish the 10 minutes and still don't want to continue?

    That's fine - stopping after 10 minutes is a real success. You can always restart the checklist for the next task.

    How is this different from the FOCO Start Method?

    They overlap. This checklist is a fast, one-page version you can use anywhere. The full FOCO Start Method goes deeper on each stage.

    Can I use this for tasks that aren't work-related?

    Yes. The steps apply to anything you're stuck starting - chores, emails, phone calls, or personal projects.
