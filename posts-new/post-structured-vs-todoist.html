What do the two apps actually look like?

We did not test Structured or Todoist hands-on for this comparison. The screens below come from each company's official App Store images, and the details come from their listings and help centers.

Structured draws your day as one vertical line. Each task is a block, and a 30-minute task is taller than a 15-minute one, so gaps and overload are visible at a glance.

Todoist is a list first. You type or say a task in plain words, and it sorts it into a project with a date, a priority and a repeat.

Which is better for ADHD, Structured or Todoist?

Neither is an ADHD app, and neither wins outright. The answer depends on which part of your day breaks down.

If the problem is time, Structured fits better. If the problem is remembering and sorting everything you have to do, Todoist fits better.

Both take load off executive function, the brain skills behind planning, sequencing and holding steps in mind (our executive function guide explains more). They just carry different parts of it.

Which helps more with time blindness?

Structured, clearly. Time blindness, trouble sensing how long things take or how much time has passed, is what its timeline is built to make visible (our time blindness guide goes deeper).

Because blocks are sized by duration, you can see that three one-hour tasks will not fit before a 2 PM meeting. Its Energy Monitor goes a step further, letting you score how draining each task is and warning you when a day is overloaded (Structured help).

Todoist has a calendar layout and task durations, but both are Pro features. Its strength is syncing two ways with Google or Outlook Calendar on every plan, so timed tasks appear next to your meetings (Todoist calendar integration).

Which is faster for getting tasks out of your head?

Todoist. Its Quick Add reads a sentence like "Submit report every Friday at 4pm" and sets the date and the repeat, and Ramble lets you talk instead of type.

Fast capture matters with ADHD because working memory, the mental notepad, drops things quickly (more on working memory and ADHD). The gap between thinking of a task and saving it is where tasks vanish.

Structured has an inbox for ideas you will schedule later. It works, but it asks you to place each task on the timeline, which takes more steps than dropping it in a list.

Which handles big projects better?

Todoist. Projects, sections, sub-tasks, four priority levels and saved filters let it hold a whole job, a house move and a side project at once.

Structured is built around today. It shows one day beautifully, but it is not where you would track a three-month project with dozens of steps.

Many people use the two together for that reason, with the list in Todoist and only today's blocks in Structured. Keeping two apps in sync is extra work, though, and that is often the first habit to slip.

Which one helps you focus once you start?

Structured, because it has a focus timer built in and Todoist does not. Its widgets and Live Activities keep the current task and countdown on your lock screen.

Todoist users usually add a separate timer. If timed blocks help you, our guide to the Pomodoro method with ADHD covers how to make them fit.

Which is cheaper?

Structured, for most people. Its help center says you can use "almost every feature of Structured for free", with Pro adding Structured AI, recurring tasks, calendar import and custom notifications (What is Structured Pro?).

On the US App Store, Structured Pro purchases range from $2.99 to $99.99, including a one-time lifetime option. Todoist Pro is $6.99 a month or $59.99 a year there, and its free plan caps you at 5 projects (Todoist pricing FAQ).

One catch on Structured's free plan: recurring tasks are Pro, so a free morning routine means adding the same blocks again each day.

Does either app help you start a task you're avoiding?

Partly. Structured gives the task a time slot and Todoist gives it a place on the list, and both take some weight off the first step.

But if the plan is already fine and you still freeze, that is task initiation, the gap between knowing what to do and doing it. Our guide to task initiation deficit explains why a better plan alone often doesn't fix it.

FAQ

Is Structured better than Todoist?

Not overall. Structured is better for seeing your day on a timeline; Todoist is better for capturing tasks fast and managing many projects.

Is Structured or Todoist better for ADHD?

Structured suits people whose main struggle is time blindness. Todoist suits people who forget tasks or feel buried under too many projects.

Is Structured free?

Mostly. Structured says almost every feature is free, and Pro adds Structured AI, recurring tasks, calendar import and custom notifications.

Is Todoist free?

Yes, Todoist has a permanent free plan with 5 projects, reminders and 3 filter views. Pro is $6.99 a month or $59.99 a year on the US App Store.

Can I use Structured and Todoist together?

You can keep the full list in Todoist and plan only today's blocks in Structured. If you do, pick one app as the source of truth so nothing gets lost between them.

Does Todoist have a timeline like Structured?

Not the same one. Todoist has a calendar layout on Pro, but not Structured's single line with blocks sized by duration.
