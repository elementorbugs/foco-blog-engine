Best Todoist Alternatives for ADHD (2026)

Why are people looking for Todoist alternatives right now?

On December 10, 2025, Todoist raised its Pro plan price.

Monthly billing went from $5 to $7. Annual billing went from $48/year ($4/month) to $60/year ($5/month).

Todoist said the increase funds continued development, including newer features like calendar layouts, deadlines, and an upcoming voice-capture tool called Ramble.

That's a legitimate reason to look around, separate from whether the app actually fits an ADHD brain in the first place.

Why doesn't Todoist work well for ADHD?

Todoist is a clean, capable list app.

It assumes the hard part is remembering what to do and keeping it organized.

For ADHD, that's rarely the actual problem.

You can have a perfectly labeled, perfectly due-dated list and still sit frozen in front of item one.

That gap has a name: task initiation deficit, the space between knowing and starting.

A long visible backlog can make it worse, not better, by putting the whole weight of everything undone in view at once.

1. FOCO - best for the moment you're stuck, not the planning before it

FOCO breaks one stuck task into a small first step and stays with you through it using an AI focus companion.

It's built for the starting moment itself, not for replacing your calendar.

Key features:

  • AI task breakdown that turns one overwhelming task into a tiny first step
  • AI body-doubling companion that stays present while you work
  • Free web tools: focus timers, brain dump, and task breakdown
  • No calendar or project-management layer to set up first

Pros:

  • Zero planning required - type the task, get a first step back immediately
  • Works cold, no onboarding or setup before it's useful
  • Core breakdown and focus tools are free forever, not a time-limited trial

Cons:

  • Not a full task manager or calendar replacement
  • Mobile app subscription costs more than a pure list app like Todoist

Pricing. Web tools (breakdown, focus timers, body doubling) are free forever. The mobile app adds a subscription at $19.99/month or $99/year, with a 7-day free trial.

2. Goblin Tools - best free task breakdown

Goblin Tools is a free web tool, no signup required, that breaks any task into smaller concrete steps.

If the barrier is purely "I don't know how to start this," it solves that specific problem at no cost.

Key features:

  • "Magic ADHD To-Do List" that splits any task into smaller steps
  • Tone slider to adjust how the steps are phrased
  • No account, no data saved server-side by default

Pros:

  • Completely free on the web, no account or card required
  • Fastest possible path from stuck to a concrete list of steps

Cons:

  • No memory between sessions - the breakdown disappears when you close the tab
  • No companion or presence feature for staying with you through the task

Pricing. Free on the web. The mobile app is a one-time purchase (around $2.99-$3.99), with an optional $39.99/year Pro tier for extra features.

3. TickTick - best like-for-like swap with a built-in timer

TickTick is the closest direct replacement for Todoist's list format, with a built-in Pomodoro timer and habit tracker added on top.

If you like the list format and just want more built into one app, this is the smallest jump to make.

Key features:

  • List and Kanban views on the free plan
  • Built-in Pomodoro timer and habit tracker
  • Two-way calendar sync on the paid plan

Pros:

  • Familiar list-based workflow for anyone leaving Todoist
  • Focus timer and habit tracking without a second app
  • One of the cheapest paid tiers in this list

Cons:

  • Still fundamentally a list - doesn't break a task into smaller steps
  • Free plan caps out at 9 lists and 99 tasks per list

Pricing. Free plan available. Premium is listed at $49.99/year, with the site frequently showing a discounted $35.99/year offer (about $3/month).

4. Tiimo - best visual planner built for ADHD

Tiimo is a visual planner built specifically for ADHD and autism, using timelines and icons instead of a plain list.

It's closer to a planning tool than a starting tool, so pair it with a breakdown app for the actual freeze moment.

Key features:

  • Visual timeline instead of a plain task list
  • Icon-based cues for each task
  • Built-in focus timers and routine templates

Pros:

  • Built specifically for neurodivergent users, not adapted after the fact
  • Strong for building repeatable routines with visual cues

Cons:

  • Doesn't shrink a specific stuck task into a smaller first step
  • No free trial on monthly billing - the 7-day trial requires the annual plan

Pricing. Free plan with limited features. Premium is $7.99/month or $79.99/year, with a family plan at $119.99/year for up to 5 people.

5. Structured - best visual daily timeline

Structured lays the day out as a visual timeline instead of a list, which helps some ADHD brains see "now" more concretely.

Key features:

  • Timeline view of the whole day, updated in real time
  • Templates for recurring days
  • Widgets showing the current and next task

Pros:

  • The visual timeline makes "what should I be doing right now" obvious at a glance
  • One of the cheapest annual plans in this list

Cons:

  • iOS only
  • Still fundamentally a planner - won't shrink an intimidating task into a smaller first step

Pricing. Free tier available. Structured Pro is $6.49/month or $19.99/year, with a $99.99 lifetime option.

6. Sunsama - best for capping an unrealistic backlog

Sunsama forces a daily planning ritual that caps how much you commit to, which reduces the overwhelm that comes from an endless visible list.

Key features:

  • Daily planning ritual that caps how much you schedule
  • Calendar integration built into the daily view
  • Weekly review built into the workflow

Pros:

  • The daily ritual prevents the kind of backlog overwhelm a long Todoist list can create
  • 14-day trial with no credit card required

Cons:

  • No permanent free plan
  • The daily planning ritual is itself a task some ADHD users skip, which defeats the purpose
  • The most expensive list-style option in this roundup

Pricing. No free tier. $22/user/month billed monthly, or $17/user/month ($204/year) billed annually, after a 14-day free trial.

7. Inflow - best for the pattern underneath getting stuck

Inflow is a CBT-based coaching app with daily lessons and a moderated community, aimed at the thinking patterns behind getting stuck repeatedly.

Key features:

  • Daily CBT-based lessons built around ADHD-specific patterns
  • Moderated community for accountability
  • Progress tracking across lessons

Pros:

  • Addresses why the freeze keeps happening, not just today's instance of it
  • Community adds accountability a solo list app can't

Cons:

  • Daily lessons take real time - not a quick fix for a task you're stuck on right now
  • Subscription-only, and the most expensive app in this roundup

Pricing. No permanent free tier. $199/year (about $16.58/month) or $47.99/month, after a 7-day free trial.

8. Habitica - best gamified routine builder

Habitica turns recurring tasks into an RPG with experience points and rewards.

It works best for repeating routines rather than the one-off stuck task a plain list leaves sitting at the top.

Key features:

  • RPG-style avatar, party, and quest system built around your habits
  • Fully functional free version - subscription only unlocks cosmetics
  • Group accountability through parties and guilds

Pros:

  • Genuinely fun for people who respond to game mechanics
  • Free version is fully functional, not a limited demo

Cons:

  • Setting up your avatar, party, and habits can become its own procrastination target
  • Not built for "one task, right now"

Pricing. Free version is fully functional. Optional subscription from $4.99/month ($47.99/year) buys cosmetic gems only - it doesn't unlock core functionality.

FAQ: Todoist Alternatives for ADHD

Why did Todoist get more expensive?

Todoist raised Pro pricing on December 10, 2025, from $5 to $7/month ($48 to $60/year), citing continued investment in the product and new features like Ramble, a voice-capture tool.

What's the single best Todoist alternative to try first?

Start with a free breakdown tool like Goblin Tools or FOCO's free tier if starting is the actual problem. If you just want a smarter list, TickTick is the closest swap.

Is Todoist actually bad for ADHD?

Not bad, just built for a different problem. It organizes tasks well but doesn't address the gap between knowing a task and starting it, which is what most ADHD users get stuck on.

Should I delete Todoist entirely?

Not necessarily. Many people keep it for pure reminders and pair it with a breakdown tool like FOCO for the tasks they actually freeze on.

Do visual planners like Tiimo really work better than a list?

For some ADHD brains, yes - seeing time as a timeline instead of reading a list reduces the translation step between "what's next" and "when is now."

Are free alternatives as good as paid ones?

For the core breakdown job specifically, yes - Goblin Tools and Habitica's free tiers are fully functional. Paid apps mostly add extras like coaching content or calendar sync rather than a fundamentally better breakdown.

Can I use more than one of these together?

Yes, and many people do - a breakdown tool for the starting moment plus a visual planner like Tiimo or Structured for the rest of the day covers two different problems a single list app rarely solves alone.
