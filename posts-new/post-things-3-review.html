Things 3 Review: Is the One-Time To-Do App Good for ADHD?

Things 3 is a to-do app for Apple devices only, bought once per device type with no subscription: US$9.99 for iPhone and Apple Watch, US$19.99 for iPad and US$49.99 for Mac. It is calm and fast to use, but it has no Android, Windows or web version and no built-in focus timer.

  • Try Things 3 if you live on Apple devices, hate subscriptions, and want a clean list that stays out of your way.
  • Skip it if you use Android or a Windows computer, or need to share lists with other people.
  • Pick FOCO, the app we make, if your list is fine but you cannot start the task, and you want it split into small timed steps.

What does Things 3 look like in use?

We did not test Things 3 hands-on. The screens below are its official App Store screenshots for Mac, so they show the app as Cultured Code presents it.

Your day lives in Today, with calendar events on top and a separate This Evening section below. Every to-do sits in a project, and every project sits in an area such as Work or Family.

Upcoming shows the days ahead, and you drag a to-do to move it. Inside a project, headings split the work into stages, and each to-do can hold its own checklist.

Is Things 3 good for ADHD?

It can be, mostly because it stays quiet. ADHD is linked to differences in executive function, the skills behind planning and getting started, as Russell Barkley's fact sheet explains.

A few parts of Things fit that well:

  • Someday: Ideas you are not doing now leave your main lists, so Today shows only today.
  • Checklists inside a to-do: You can split a task into small steps without making a whole project.
  • Quick capture: Siri, the share sheet, widgets and Mail to Things all drop new items into the Inbox.
  • Nothing to maintain: One purchase and free sync means no plan to manage or renew.

What it does not do is help you start: its feature list has no timer, no first-step prompt and no body doubling, so a task can sit in Today for days. That gap is task initiation, which our guide to task initiation deficit explains.

For questions about ADHD itself, the National Institute of Mental Health is a better place to start.

Where might Things 3 fall short?

Apple only: Cultured Code says Things does not work on Windows, Android, Linux or in a web browser. A Windows work laptop is a dealbreaker.

Paying per device type: iPhone and Mac together cost US$59.98, and adding iPad brings it to US$79.97. It is still one payment, not a yearly bill.

Trial on Mac only: The iPhone listing points to a free trial for Mac and mentions none for iPhone. Refunds go through Apple, not Cultured Code.

No sharing: You cannot share a list with a partner or coworker, so it is a personal app only.

No discounts: Cultured Code says it cannot offer student or business discounts itself, and points to Apple's education and volume programs.

How much does Things 3 cost?

Things 3 costs US$9.99 for iPhone and Apple Watch, US$19.99 for iPad, US$49.99 for Mac and US$29.99 for Vision Pro. Each is a one-time purchase, sold only through Apple's App Store.

You do not pay again for a second device of the same kind, and Things Cloud sync is free. Family Sharing lets family members use the app at no extra cost.

Prices differ by country, so check the App Store in your region before you buy.

It can be if you only use Apple devices and want a calm list you pay for once. It has no timer or start-up help, so pair it with another tool if starting tasks is the hard part.
