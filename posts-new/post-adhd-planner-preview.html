What makes a printable planner work for ADHD?

It asks for fewer decisions and gives you a clear first move. Most planners do the opposite: lots of empty boxes and nothing that helps you begin.

Planning, prioritizing and starting all lean on executive function, and differences in executive function are central to ADHD, as described in Russell Barkley's model of ADHD.

The kit is built on five rules:

  • Undated. A blank week in a dated planner can feel like a failure. Undated pages just wait for you.
  • One page, one job. No setup and no system to maintain.
  • A first step, not just a task. The gap between knowing and starting is what FOCO calls a task initiation deficit, explained in task initiation deficit explained.
  • Time you can see. Rough blocks and minute guesses help with time blindness, the trouble sensing how much time is passing. More in time blindness and ADHD.
  • No streaks. The habit tracker and weekly page count good days, so one missed day doesn't erase the rest.

How do you use the ADHD planner kit in 5 minutes a day?

Keep it short enough to do on a bad day. Five minutes is the whole job.

  1. Pick one page. Use the table above and print a few copies of just that page.
  2. Put it where you already look. Next to the coffee maker, on your keyboard, on the fridge.
  3. Fill it in at the same moment each day. Tie it to something you already do, like your first coffee or plugging in your phone at night.
  4. Finish with the fill-in line. Write when you'll start, or the one thing you'll do first. That line is the part that gets you moving.

Missed a day? Don't catch up: take a fresh page, write today's date and start there.

How do you keep using a paper planner after the first week?

Make it hard to forget and easy to restart. Most paper planners die in a drawer, not from bad design.

Keep it visible, and keep one spare copy where you spend most of your time. If a page sits blank for a week, that's information, not failure: try a different page from the kit.

If you plan well but freeze at the first task, the planner isn't the problem. A timer can give the task a clear end, like the 10-minute timer method.

FAQ

Is there a free printable ADHD planner?

Yes, FOCO's ADHD planner kit is free to download with no email or signup. It has seven undated pages in US Letter and A4, in a dark version and an ink-saver version.

Should I use a dated or undated planner?

Undated is usually easier with ADHD, because skipped days don't leave blank pages behind. Dated planners help if seeing the day of the week is what keeps you oriented.

How do I stop abandoning planners after a few days?

Keep it where you already look, fill it in at the same moment each day, and use only one page at first. If you miss a day, start again from today instead of catching up.

Can I use the planner on an iPad or tablet?

Yes, open the dark PDF in a note-taking app like GoodNotes or Notability and write on it with a stylus. You can duplicate a page as often as you like.

Will the planner print on A4 paper?

Do bullet journals work for ADHD?

The simple version can, because it is fast and flexible. Elaborate spreads tend to need more upkeep, so if you try one, keep the layouts plain.
