Why do people look for a Sunsama alternative?

Usually for one of two reasons: the price, or the planning routine. Sunsama is a well-made digital daily planner that pulls tasks from your other tools onto one calendar, with focus mode, timeboxing and end-of-day reviews (sunsama.com).

It has no free plan, and its pricing page says it "doesn't plan to" add one. The plan card shows $17 a month billed yearly or $22 billed monthly after a 14-day free trial with no card, while the FAQ on the same page says $20 and $25, so check the price at checkout (Sunsama pricing).

The routine is the other part. Sunsama works best when you sit down each morning and plan, and for many people with ADHD, planning itself leans on the same executive function skills that are hardest to summon on demand.

What are the best Sunsama alternatives for ADHD?

1. FOCO, best for starting a task you keep avoiding

We make FOCO, so weigh this entry with that in mind. It is first here because it targets the step Sunsama assumes you can already do: task initiation, the gap between knowing what to do and starting it.

You add a stuck task by typing or speaking, and AI breaks it into small steps, each with a time estimate. Then a focus timer with calm music and an on-screen AI companion stays with you, a form of body doubling.

The trade-off: FOCO is not a work calendar hub. It has a built-in day view, but it does not pull in Jira or Slack the way Sunsama does.

Price: 7-day free trial, then $9/month or $59/year. iPhone and Android, no web app (checked Oct 9, 2026)

2. Motion, best for letting AI build your schedule

Motion takes your tasks, deadlines and durations and places them on your calendar for you, moving them when the day changes.

The catch is that it still needs time estimates, and those are hard to get right when time blindness is part of your ADHD.

Price: no free plan, free trial. For one person, Pro AI is $29 a month billed yearly or $49 billed monthly; the $19 often quoted is the per-seat team price (Motion pricing, checked Oct 9, 2026).

3. Reclaim.ai, best for protecting focus time automatically

Reclaim.ai fills your existing calendar with focus blocks, habits and breaks around your meetings. It does the time-blocking that Sunsama asks you to do by hand.

The trade-off is mobile: Reclaim is primarily a web app, and its help center suggests adding it to your phone's home screen or using the Google Calendar app (Reclaim help).

Price: free Lite plan with a one-week scheduling range; Starter $10 and Business $15 per seat per month billed yearly, or $12 and $18 monthly (Reclaim pricing, checked Oct 9, 2026)

4. Structured, best for a simple visual timeline

Structured shows one day as one line of colored blocks, sized to how long each thing takes. There is no morning review to get through, which makes it much lighter to open than Sunsama.

It is no longer Apple-only: Structured runs on Android and the web too, though it says some iOS features are still missing there (Structured help).

Price: "almost every feature" is free; Pro is monthly, yearly or lifetime, with prices that vary by region (checked Oct 9, 2026)

5. Tiimo, best for a visual planner built for neurodivergent brains

Tiimo is designed for ADHD and autistic users first, with a color-coded timeline, icons, routines, reminders and a focus timer.

Most of the depth sits in Pro, and its Android app was rebuilt in 2026 and is still catching up to iOS.

Price: free version with some tools; Pro monthly or yearly, price shown at checkout and varies by country, 7-day trial on the yearly plan (Tiimo pricing, checked Oct 9, 2026)

6. Todoist, best for fast capture

Todoist is a quick, plain task list that understands natural language, so "call dentist tomorrow 3pm" becomes a dated task. For ADHD, its strength is getting a thought out of your head before it slips away.

It will not plan your day or help you start, and it can grow into a wall of overdue tasks if you add every idea with a date.

Price: free Beginner plan with five personal projects; Pro $7/month or $60/year, with a 7-day trial (Todoist pricing FAQ, checked Oct 9, 2026)

7. TickTick, best for tasks, habits and a timer in one app

TickTick combines a task list, calendar, habit tracker and a Pomodoro timer.

The trade-off: lots of settings can mean choice overload.

Price: free plan with 9 lists and 99 tasks per list; Premium US$49.99/year (TickTick Premium, checked Oct 9, 2026)

Who should stay with Sunsama?

  • You actually enjoy the morning planning routine, and it calms you rather than drains you.
  • Your work lives across Slack, Jira, Asana or Notion, and one unified list saves you real time.
  • You need desktop apps on Windows or Linux as well as your phone.

FAQ

Is there a free alternative to Sunsama?

Yes. Structured, Todoist, TickTick and Reclaim.ai all have free plans you can keep using, and Tiimo has a free version with some tools.

What is the best Sunsama alternative for ADHD?

It depends on where your day breaks down. FOCO fits if you can't start, Motion or Reclaim.ai if planning is the hurdle, and Structured or Tiimo if you need to see your day.

Does Sunsama have a free plan?

No. Sunsama offers a 14-day free trial with no credit card, and its pricing page says it does not plan to add a free plan.

How much does Sunsama cost?

Its pricing page showed $17 a month billed yearly or $22 monthly on October 9, 2026, while its FAQ listed $20 and $25. Check at checkout.

What is the best AI alternative to Sunsama?

Motion and Reclaim.ai both schedule your tasks onto your calendar automatically. Reclaim.ai has a free plan; Motion is paid after a trial.

Is Sunsama good for ADHD?

It can be, if a daily planning routine helps you. If you keep skipping that routine, a tool that removes it may suit you better.
