How does the Brick app work?

Brick works in three moves: pick the apps to block, tap your phone on the Brick to lock them, then leave the Brick somewhere else. The apps stay locked until you walk back and tap again.

STEP 1

Choose what to block

Pick apps, whole categories like Social, and Safari sites, saved as a mode.

STEP 2

Tap to "Brick" your phone

Hold the phone to the puck. The blocked apps and their notifications disappear.

STEP 3

Walk away from it

Stick it on the fridge. To unlock, you have to go back and tap again.

The trick is where the lock lives. Screen Time limits and blocker apps sit on the same phone you're trying to put down, so turning them off takes a swipe; Brick's own listing calls it "a physical barrier you can't swipe out of."

The app adds schedules that block apps at set times, a focus timer that counts how long you've stayed Bricked, and a usage history. Calls always work, since Apple doesn't allow blocking the dialer.

What is the difference between Brick and Brick Night?

Brick is the small grey puck you tap whenever you want to lock your phone. Brick Night is a larger, $129 Bluetooth device for the bedroom that blocks your chosen apps automatically when you walk in, with no tap needed.

You set how far Brick Night's "Bricked space" reaches, so it can cover just the bed or the whole room. The app then shows nights, average hours and "reclaimed hours" next to your daytime Brick time.

If your scrolling peaks at night, Brick Night is the more targeted buy. For a bedtime plan that doesn't depend on a gadget, see our ADHD bedtime routine.

Is Brick good for ADHD?

For a lot of ADHD adults, yes, because it moves the decision away from the moment of impulse. Brick comes up again and again in r/ADHD and r/adhdwomen threads about doomscrolling, usually from people whose software blockers didn't survive a bored evening.

Three things about ADHD explain why a physical lock can work better than a setting:

1. Friction beats willpower

ADHD involves differences in executive function, the brain skills that pause an urge and weigh it against a goal (NIMH). Brick doesn't ask you to resist; it makes the feed a walk away.

2. Feeds reward fast

Endless new posts deliver quick hits of novelty, the kind of instant reward many people with ADHD find hard to step away from (more in ADHD and dopamine).

3. "One minute" becomes an hour

With time blindness, a quick check can quietly stretch out. A locked phone stops the slide before it starts.

Brick isn't an ADHD treatment, and it doesn't replace care from a clinician. CHADD is a good place to start for support.

What are the downsides of Brick?

The biggest one is that a lock only works if you leave it locked, and Brick gives you 5 emergency unbricks for when you can't reach the puck. Reviewers at Cybernews say getting more means emailing support, which some find stressful if the Brick is misplaced.

What people like

  • No subscription, one payment
  • Can't be switched off with a swipe
  • Modes and schedules for different parts of the day
  • One puck can lock several phones, per ABC News

What to watch for

  • Only 5 emergency unbricks
  • Doesn't block a laptop or desktop
  • Easy to lock away an app you need, like banking
  • Needs working NFC on your phone

Reviewers also mention gaps around the edges, like links opened from other apps reaching a blocked site. If your phone's NFC is broken, the tap won't register at all.

And Brick stops one habit without giving you a next step. A locked phone in the middle of the afternoon still leaves you facing the task you were avoiding, which is often why you picked up the phone in the first place.

Brick vs Opal: which is better?

Pick Brick if you need a lock you physically can't reach, and Opal if you want software with rules, schedules and stats and no device to buy. Opal has a free plan limited to one rule, and Opal Pro costs $99.99 a year (more in our Opal app review).

The trade-off is cost over time against strictness. Brick is $59 once; Opal's yearly plan costs more than that every year, but it can't be left at work by accident.

FAQ

Is Brick worth $59?

For people who keep switching off software blockers, many reviewers say yes, because a one-time $59 costs less than a year of most blocker subscriptions. If a free pause app like ScreenZen already works for you, you may not need it.

Does Brick have a subscription?

No. You buy the device once, and the Brick app is free with no in-app purchases listed on the App Store.

Does Brick work on Android?

Yes, the Brick app is on Google Play as well as the App Store, where it needs iOS 18 or later. Either way, your phone needs working NFC for the tap.

What happens if you lose your Brick?

You can end a session with one of 5 emergency unbricks, and reviewers say support can add more. Buying a second Brick is the backup if it's gone for good.

Can you still make calls when your phone is Bricked?

Yes. Apple doesn't allow the phone dialer to be blocked, so calls always work, and you choose whether messaging apps stay open.

Is Brick good for ADHD?

Many ADHD users say it helps because it adds physical distance instead of relying on willpower in the moment. It doesn't treat ADHD or help you start tasks, so it works best alongside other strategies.

What is the difference between Brick and FOCO?

Brick blocks distracting apps until you tap the device again. FOCO is an ADHD planner: it breaks a stuck task into timed steps, puts it in your calendar and keeps a companion with you while you focus, so the two work well together.
