What does Inflow actually look like?

We did not test Inflow hands-on for this update. The screens below are from the app and from Inflow's own website, and the feature details come from Inflow's site and App Store listing.

Inflow describes itself as a digital program rooted in CBT principles, narrated by co-founder and clinical director Dr. George Sachs with guest experts. Lessons cover topics like procrastination, anxiety, impulsivity and avoidance, and you work through them in small daily doses.

Around the lessons sit a peer community, live events with ADHD experts, and live or on-demand coworking rooms for body doubling. Inflow's website invites new users to start with a short quiz.

Is Inflow legit and backed by research?

Yes, Inflow is a real company with clinical founders, but its published evidence is early. The peer-reviewed study we found is a 2022 open feasibility study in PLOS Digital Health, with no control group (Knouse et al., 2022).

In that study, adults rated the app as easy to use, and most of the 93 people with matched data at week 7 self-reported fewer ADHD symptoms. The authors say a randomized controlled trial is needed to show whether the app itself caused the change.

Two of the four authors co-founded Inflow, which the paper discloses.

Inflow's own FAQ is clear that it "is not a diagnostic tool and does not offer clinical services or medical treatment" and is not meant to replace medication or therapy (Inflow FAQ). CBT itself is a recognized approach for adult ADHD, and NIMH lists psychotherapy alongside medication among treatment options.

How much does Inflow cost, and is it free?

Inflow is not free. The app is free to download, but the program sits behind a subscription with a 7-day free trial.

Inflow's FAQ lists $47.99 a month or $199 a year after the trial. The US App Store shows $47.99 monthly and $199.99 yearly for plans that include coaching, plus cheaper plans without coaching at $22.49 a month and $95.99 a year (Inflow on the App Store).

The store lists several other tiers with unclear labels, so which plans you see may depend on your offer or country. Inflow also mentions an affordability program and a student discount.

Cancel at least 24 hours before renewal, and note that deleting the app does not cancel it. Inflow says it gives refunds if you forget to cancel.

What is Inflow coaching, and is it therapy?

Inflow coaching is accountability support, not therapy. On coaching plans you get access to Inflow's coaching team, who check in on your goals and help you stay on track.

It does not replace an ADHD-trained therapist or prescriber, and Inflow does not claim it does. If you want a person working with you one-on-one, our guide to ADHD coaches compares the options.

Does Inflow help when you can't start a task?

Partly. Inflow teaches why task initiation is hard with ADHD and gives you strategies, and the coworking rooms can help you get going.

What it does not do is turn today's stuck task into steps on your screen. Psychologist Russell Barkley describes ADHD as a problem of doing what you know rather than knowing what to do (Barkley fact sheet), which is why a lesson alone may not get you moving in the moment.

That in-the-moment gap is what our guide to task initiation deficit explains, and what FOCO is built for. FOCO breaks the task into timed steps and opens a focus session with a companion on screen.

Who should try Inflow?

Try Inflow if:

  • You were diagnosed recently and want to understand how ADHD shows up in your life.
  • You like learning in a course format and want a community of ADHD adults.
  • You want an accountability coach and the coaching plan fits your budget.

Skip Inflow if:

  • You need a daily planner or task list, which Inflow is not.
  • You want clinical treatment, therapy or a diagnosis.
  • You use an iPad as your main device.

Pick FOCO if:
