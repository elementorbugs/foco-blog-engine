Which is better when you can't start a task?

FOCO, for most people whose main problem is starting. Task initiation, the gap between knowing what to do and doing it, is the moment FOCO is built around.

Psychologist Russell Barkley describes ADHD as a problem of self-regulation, of doing what you know rather than knowing what to do (Barkley, executive function fact sheet). That is why every decision before step one can cost more than it looks.

Marvin does take starting seriously. Its Procrastination Wizard, Task Jar and Super Focus Mode all target that stuck feeling, and some people find them excellent once their setup is in place.

The difference is the order: in Marvin you first pick a workflow and the features you want, while in FOCO you type or say the task and the AI hands you timed steps.

Which is better for running your whole system?

Amazing Marvin, clearly. It is one of the deepest personal task managers aimed at ADHD users, and its homepage lists 300+ customizable settings.

You can plan by the day with a capacity estimator, keep simple lists, work spontaneously with its Orbit and Spotlight features, or run full Getting Things Done. It also offers calendar and Kanban layouts, habits, goals, time tracking and imports from Todoist, TickTick and other apps.

It runs on the web, Mac, Windows, iPhone and Android, so the same system follows you from your desk to your phone. FOCO has no web or desktop app, so if you plan your week at a computer, Marvin fits that habit and FOCO does not.

FOCO does include a simple day plan: tasks sit in morning, afternoon or anytime, with reminders, and missed tasks move forward without a red overdue pile. It is meant for today, not for managing projects.

Is all that customization a plus or a risk with ADHD?

It depends on how you react to setup. If building a system feels satisfying, Marvin's depth is a real advantage, because you can shape it around how your brain works instead of forcing yourself into someone else's method.

If setting up planners is where you usually drop off, depth can work against you. Many people with ADHD know this pattern well, which we cover in why you keep abandoning ADHD planner apps.

Marvin's own answer is that you can switch features on gradually and keep the rest hidden. That works well if you go slowly, and its free trial can be extended on request, according to its pricing page.

What does each one cost?

Amazing Marvin: one plan, Marvin Pro, at $12 a month billed monthly or $96 a year, which works out to $8 a month (Amazing Marvin pricing, checked October 9, 2026). The trial is 14 days with no credit card, students get 50% off by email, and a pay-what-you-can option exists for anyone who cannot afford it.

That pay-what-you-can policy is rare among paid productivity apps and deserves credit. Marvin says it does not offer refunds, but will extend the trial if you need more time.

FOCO: a 7-day free trial, then $9 a month or $59 a year. There is no free tier.

Prices change, so check the official page or store before you pay. We date-stamp every price and recheck it when we update this page.

Who should pick which?

Choose Amazing Marvin if:

  • You want one deep app for tasks, projects, habits, goals and time tracking.
  • You plan at a computer and want the same system on Mac, Windows, web and phone.
  • Tweaking settings until a system fits you feels rewarding, not draining.
  • Cost is a barrier and the student discount or pay-what-you-can option would help.

Choose FOCO if:

  • You already know what to do and freeze before step one.
  • You want the AI to break a task into steps with a time on each, without building a system first.
  • You work mostly from your phone and are fine with a paid app after a 7-day free trial ($9 a month or $59 a year), on iPhone or Android.

FAQ

Does Amazing Marvin have a free plan?

No. It offers a 14-day free trial with no credit card, then Marvin Pro at $12 a month or $96 a year, with student and pay-what-you-can discounts by email.

Is Amazing Marvin good for ADHD?

Many people with ADHD like it, and its site describes it as built for ADHD minds and chronic procrastinators. It suits people who enjoy customizing; the depth can feel like a lot if setup is where you stall.

Is Amazing Marvin on Android and Windows?

Yes. Amazing Marvin lists web, Mac, Windows, iOS and Android apps on its pricing page.

Is FOCO a replacement for Amazing Marvin?

Not for project management. FOCO focuses on breaking one task into timed steps and helping you start, with a simple day plan rather than a full system.

Can I use FOCO and Amazing Marvin together?

Yes. Some people keep their week and projects in Marvin and open FOCO for the one task they cannot get started on.

Does FOCO have a free version?

No. FOCO has a 7-day free trial, then costs $9 a month or $59 a year.
