What is Amazing Marvin?

Amazing Marvin is one of the only task managers that markets to ADHD directly, not as an afterthought bolted onto generic productivity copy.

The feature list reads like it was written by someone who has lived the problem: Super Focus Mode, an Accountability Pledge, a Task Jar for picking a task at random when choosing feels impossible, timed Spotlight work sessions, a Procrastination Wizard, built-in time tracking.

All of it sits behind 300+ settings you can turn on or ignore, spread across four planning methodologies (GTD, List-Based, Spontaneous, Day Planning). It also runs a genuine "pay what you can" program for people who can't afford the subscription, which is rare among paid productivity apps and worth real credit.

Marvin's own marketing material accidentally makes the point about depth: the app's own team has a to-do list category called "Marvin," a task inside it named "Make screenshots," and 126 items waiting in the Master List below.

That is not a criticism of clutter. It is what a genuinely deep, flexible system looks like once you have lived in it for years.

Reviewers on G2 and Capterra consistently flag the learning curve as the main drawback, and a common reason people leave is that newer apps offer AI scheduling that Marvin doesn't. There is also no permanent free tier: after the 14-day trial, it is $8 a month billed annually, or you email support and ask for a discount.

What is FOCO?

FOCO is built for one narrow moment: you know what to do and cannot start it, what clinicians call task initiation deficit.

You enter one stuck task. FOCO breaks it into small first steps with time estimates, and stays with you while you work through them. There is no methodology to choose and no setting to configure first.

Compare that to Marvin's Today view above: folders, counters, a category system. FOCO's screen has one thing on it.

The trade-off is real. FOCO does not track projects, does not have a master list, and will not replace Marvin's planning depth if that is what you actually need. It is a narrow tool built for a narrow, specific failure point.

Which is better for actually starting a task?

For pure task initiation, FOCO wins, and the reason is structural, not a matter of taste.

The Russell Barkley executive function model frames ADHD as weaker self-regulation, not a lack of intelligence. Asking that same regulation system to choose a methodology and configure settings before it can help you spends the exact resource the app is supposed to save.

Marvin's Procrastination Wizard and Task Jar are genuinely useful once you are inside the system. Getting inside the system in the first place, choosing between GTD, List-Based, Spontaneous, and Day Planning, then deciding which of 300+ settings to turn on, is itself a task that can trigger the exact paralysis Marvin is trying to solve.

FOCO skips that step entirely. There is nothing to decide before you can start.

How do the free tiers compare?

Neither app is free forever, but the comparison isn't quite even. FOCO's website tools (brain dump, focus timers, body doubling room) are free forever with no account, something Marvin has no equivalent of.

For the app itself, both are trial-then-subscribe: FOCO runs $9/month or $59/year after a 7-day trial. Marvin runs $8/month billed annually after a 14-day trial, with student and pay-what-you-can discounts available by emailing support.

If you want to test-drive before paying anything, Marvin's trial is longer. If you want genuinely free tools to fall back on between trials, FOCO's website covers that.

Which has more features overall?

Amazing Marvin, by a wide margin. Project tracking, time analytics, multiple methodologies, gamification, a Procrastination Wizard, and 300+ settings to shape all of it.

FOCO is deliberately narrow: one stuck task, a breakdown, a timer. It will not replace a full planning system.

The honest framing is not "which app has more" but "which gap are you actually trying to close." Marvin closes the planning-and-structure gap. FOCO closes the starting gap. Different tools for different failure points.

When should you pick Amazing Marvin?

Pick Marvin when any of these are true.

You want to build a system and keep it long-term. Project tracking, custom workflows, and analytics reward the setup time if you will actually use them for months.

Configuring things feels satisfying, not draining. Some ADHD brains do better with a system they built themselves than with a fixed, narrow tool.

Your problem is structural, not initiation-specific. If your week falls apart because nothing is tracked anywhere, Marvin's depth is the point.

When should you pick FOCO?

Pick FOCO when any of these are true.

You read Marvin's feature list and felt tired before opening the app. That reaction is common, and it is exactly the failure mode FOCO is built around.

You already know what to do and just cannot start it. No amount of system depth fixes that specific moment. A smaller first step does.

You want to try something today with no methodology to choose. FOCO's free website tools (brain dump, timers, body doubling) need no setup, and the app itself is a 7-day free trial away.

FAQ

Is Amazing Marvin actually built for ADHD?

Yes. Unlike many productivity apps that add "ADHD-friendly" as marketing copy, Amazing Marvin's own site positions it explicitly as a task manager for ADHD, with features named around procrastination and executive dysfunction.

Does Amazing Marvin have a free plan?

No. It offers a 14-day free trial with no credit card required, but no permanent free tier. After the trial it's $8/month billed annually, with student and pay-what-you-can discounts available by contacting support.

Is FOCO a replacement for Amazing Marvin?

No, and it isn't trying to be. FOCO doesn't track projects or offer methodology choices. It solves one problem, starting a stuck task, that Marvin's depth doesn't specifically target.

Is Amazing Marvin worth the learning curve?

It depends on whether you enjoy building your own system. If configuring workflows feels satisfying, Marvin's depth pays off. If configuration itself is where your ADHD stalls, a simpler tool will get more actual use out of you.

Can I use FOCO and Amazing Marvin together?

Yes, and it's a reasonable combination: Marvin for the week-level system, FOCO for the specific task you're stuck on right now. They solve different layers of the same day.

The bottom line

Amazing Marvin is one of the most honestly ADHD-branded tools on the market, and its depth is real, not padding. If you want a system and will put in the setup time, it delivers.

But depth has a cost: 300 settings to get through before the app starts helping you. If that setup is where you'd stall, the fix isn't a better configuration screen.

It's a tool with nothing to configure at all. That's what FOCO is built to be, and you can try it free for 7 days.
