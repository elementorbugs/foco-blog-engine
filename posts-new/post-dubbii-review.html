Dubbii Review: Body Doubling App for ADHD Chores

Dubbii is a body doubling app from ADHD Love for iPhone, iPad and Android. It offers live sessions with hosts every three hours, a 24/7 quiet focus room, follow-along cleaning videos and reminders.

It is a subscription with a 3-day free trial for new subscribers, and the price is shown inside the app.

  • Try Dubbii if housework is where you get stuck, and seeing real people clean alongside you helps you start.
  • Skip it if you need help with desk work more than chores, or you would rather not be seen on camera in a group room.
  • Pick FOCO, the app we make, if you want any task split into small timed steps, with a body-doubling companion and no live room.

What does Dubbii look like in use?

We did not test Dubbii hands-on. The screens below are its official App Store screenshots, so they show the app as the developer presents it.

The focus room shows a grid of members working on camera, each tile labeled with a name, while a timer runs at the top. The Lives tab lists the day's hosted sessions, and the Videos tab sorts cleaning videos by room.

Each video splits a chore into short sections, such as "bin rubbish" then "remove clutter," that you can skip between. Nudges are simple reminders you tick off, like "Take meds" or "Morning routine."

Is Dubbii good for ADHD?

It can be, because it is built on body doubling: doing a task while someone else is present. In a survey of 220 neurodivergent adults by Eagle and colleagues, 85% said they were more likely to finish a task with someone else there.

Dubbii applies that to housework, where a lot of ADHD stress lives. A 2-to-5-minute "making the bed" video turns a vague chore into one step you can press play on.

Dubbii's own listing says 84% of users report it helps them finish tasks or stay focused, and 96% say it reduced ADHD-related shame. Those figures come from the company's self-reported user survey, not an independent study.

Where might Dubbii fall short?

Price is hidden until you open it: The App Store listing shows a subscription but not its price, so you only see the cost inside the app.

A short trial: The free trial is 3 days, which is enough for a couple of sessions but not a full week of routines.

Built for chores: The videos and many sessions center on cleaning. For emails, studying or work projects, the guided content fits less well.

The camera: Its screenshots show members on camera in the focus room. If being seen makes you freeze, that room may not suit you.

Steps come from its videos: Its website promises micro-steps for everyday tasks, and the store screenshots show those steps inside its own chore videos. We found no feature in the listings that splits a task you type in.

How much does Dubbii cost?

Dubbii is free to download, with one "dubbii Subscription" for full access, according to its store listings. Its latest update added a 3-day free trial for new eligible subscribers.

The App Store did not show the subscription price on November 14, 2026, so check the price in the app before the trial ends. The App Store says some subscriptions can be shared through Family Sharing.

How is Dubbii different from Flow Club and Focusmate?

Flow Club runs hosted video sessions for desk work from a computer, at US$40 a month when we reviewed it. Focusmate pairs you one-on-one with another person.

Dubbii is the only one of the three built mainly for the phone and for housework. Its follow-along videos also work when no live session is on.
