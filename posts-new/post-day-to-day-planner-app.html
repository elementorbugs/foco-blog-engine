What makes a good day to day planner app?

A good day planner app shows today first, lets you add a task in a few seconds, and survives a day that goes off plan. Everything else is a bonus.

That last part matters more than most feature lists admit. People reliably underestimate how long tasks take, a bias psychologists call the planning fallacy (Buehler, Griffin and Ross, 1994), so a day packed edge to edge breaks by lunch.

Look for three things: a view of today you'll actually open, a way to move unfinished tasks without guilt, and reminders that reach you when you're not in the app.

Which day to day planner app fits how you plan?

Start from how your head already organizes a day. The best app is the one that matches it, not the one with the longest feature list.

The 8 best day to day planner apps in 2026

1. FOCO, best for actually starting the day you planned

FOCO is the planner we make, built for days where the list is clear but nothing gets started. You type a task, say it out loud or snap a photo of a handwritten list, and it lands in your day under morning, afternoon or anytime, with a reminder.

When a task feels too big, one tap and AI breaks it into small steps with a time for each. Step one opens a focus timer with calm sounds and an AI companion on screen, and missed tasks move on to the next day instead of piling up.

Good to know: FOCO has its own calendar and doesn't sync with Google or Outlook yet, so it works best next to your work calendar.

Price: $59 a year with a 7-day free trial, or $9 a month, on iPhone and Android (checked Oct 20, 2026).

2. Structured, best for one visual timeline

Structured turns your day into a single vertical timeline, with every task and event as a colored block sized to its length. It's the closest thing to seeing your day on paper, and it's one of the most popular planner apps on the App Store.

The core timeline is free. Pro adds AI planning and Replan, which moves the tasks you missed into the rest of your day.

Price: free to start; Pro is a paid upgrade whose price isn't listed on Structured's site, so check it in the app (checked Oct 20, 2026).

3. Tiimo, best for a calm, visual day

Tiimo shows your day as soft, color-coded blocks with icons, and it was designed with neurodivergent people in mind. It feels less like a productivity tool and more like a gentle guide through the day.

A co-planner can turn a brain dump into a plan, and a visual timer shows how much time is left in each block.

Price: free version on iPhone and Android; Pro is monthly or yearly, the price varies by location, and the 7-day trial is on yearly only (Tiimo pricing, checked Oct 20, 2026).

4. TickTick, best all-in-one planner on a budget

TickTick packs a to-do list, a calendar, a habit tracker and a Pomodoro focus timer into one app. If you want one place for everything and don't want to pay much, it's hard to beat.

The free plan covers 9 lists, 99 tasks per list, 5 habits and a basic calendar view. Premium unlocks the full calendar views and more reminders per task.

Price: free plan; Premium $49.99 a year (TickTick, checked Oct 20, 2026).

5. Todoist, best for a to-do list that becomes a day

Todoist is a task list first. You type "call the dentist Friday 3pm" and it picks up the date, then the Today and Upcoming views turn your list into a plan for each day.

It's fast and works everywhere, but it shows tasks, not time. If you need to see how the hours fit, pair it with a calendar.

Price: free plan with 5 projects and reminders; Pro $6.99 a month or $59.99 a year on the US App Store (checked Oct 19, 2026).

6. Google Calendar, best free time blocking

Plenty of people don't need a new app at all. Google Calendar is free, already holds your meetings, and works as a day planner if you block time for tasks, not just events.

The trade-off is that a calendar doesn't hold a to-do list well. Tasks you don't finish stay where they were, so moving them is on you.

Price: free for personal use (Google Calendar, checked Oct 20, 2026).

7. Any.do, best simple daily list with reminders

Any.do keeps planning simple: Today, Tomorrow and Upcoming, with a reminder on anything that needs one. Its daily planner walks you through your tasks each morning.

The free personal plan includes tasks, lists, reminders, a calendar and the daily planner, synced across devices.

Price: free personal plan; Premium $4.99 a month billed yearly or $7.99 billed monthly (Any.do, checked Oct 20, 2026).

8. Ellie, best for brain dump then timeboxing

Ellie copies the paper method many planners swear by: dump every task on a list, then drag each one into an hour of the day. It's clean, quiet and quick to learn.

The free plan covers unlimited tasks and the brain dump. Timebox mode, calendar sync, recurring tasks and reminders are Pro.

Price: free plan; Pro $9.99 a month or $99.99 a year on the US App Store (US App Store, checked Oct 19, 2026; more in our Ellie review).

Are free day to day planner apps good enough?

For many people, yes. Google Calendar is fully free, and Structured, TickTick, Todoist and Any.do all have free plans that cover a normal day.

FAQ

What is the best day to day planner app?

It depends on how you plan. Structured is the best visual timeline, TickTick the best all-in-one on a budget, Google Calendar the best free option, and FOCO the best if you struggle to start what you planned.

Is there a free day planner app?

Yes. Google Calendar is completely free, and Structured, Tiimo, TickTick, Todoist, Any.do and Ellie all have free plans with paid upgrades.

What is the best day by day planner app for iPhone?

Structured and Tiimo are the most popular visual day planners on iPhone. TickTick and Todoist are the strongest list-based options, and all four have free versions.

Is a planner app better than a paper planner?

An app wins on reminders and moving tasks you didn't finish. Paper wins if you plan better by writing, and many people use both.

What is the difference between a day planner and a to-do list app?

A to-do list app holds tasks, while a day planner places them into the hours of a specific day. Apps like TickTick and Todoist sit in between, with a list that sorts into days.

Which day planner app helps you actually get things done?

One that matches how you work and makes the first step small. FOCO breaks each task into tiny timed steps and starts a focus session with you, which helps when the plan is fine but starting isn't.
