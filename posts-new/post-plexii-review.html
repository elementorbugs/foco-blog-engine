Plexii Review: A Desktop Workspace With ADHD Focus Tools

Short answer: Plexii (also called PlexiiDesk) is a Mac and Windows app that gives every task its own "desk", with docs, sheets, a browser, notes, timers and an AI assistant on one canvas. It adds ADHD-minded tools like a five-minute start and live body doubling, free for up to three desks or $12 a month for Pro.

  • Try Plexii if you work at a computer, lose the thread every time you switch apps, and want each project's pieces in one place.
  • Skip it if you want something on your phone, or a big all-in-one app tends to become a project of its own for you.
  • Pick FOCO, the app we make, if you need help starting one task from your phone, with the task split into timed steps and a focus companion on screen.

What does Plexii look like in use?

We did not test Plexii hands-on. The screens below are official screenshots from Plexii's own ADHD page, so they show the app as the developer presents it.

A desk holds one piece of work. In this example, a grant report sits next to its brief, a re-entry note for where to pick up, a table of sections, a 25-minute timer and a sticky note for stray thoughts.

When you open an important desk you haven't started, a card asks what would make it 10% easier. The calendar can then book time for open tasks, and on Pro a "Plan my day" button lays the day out for you to review.

Is Plexii built for ADHD?

Partly. Plexii is a general work app sold to founders, designers, students and others, and it also has a dedicated ADHD focus page describing tools for lost context, app switching and hard starts.

That page says plainly that Plexii is a work tool, not a treatment for ADHD. ADHD often affects executive function, the skills behind planning and follow-through, and the National Institute of Mental Health is a good place to start on the condition itself.

What does Plexii get right for ADHD brains?

One task, one place: The doc, the numbers, the web pages and the notes for a piece of work sit side by side. If every app switch costs you the thread, keeping it all in view is the core idea.

A gentle start: On a high-stakes desk still marked to do, Plexii offers "Just 5 minutes", a quiet AI check-in, or an AI-suggested first step. The help centre says the card steps aside after 15 seconds if you ignore it, and it works on every plan.

A way back in: Resume writes a short note on where a desk stands, its open questions and the next three actions. That helps with the slow restart after a day away, though it needs AI credits or your own key.

Short focus sessions: Focus sessions default to five minutes, and the help centre says the timer counts only time you spend on the work. They are on every plan and need no AI.

Live body doubling: On Pro and up, Plexii pairs you at random with another member who is also working, on camera, in one of four modes from Silent to Happy to talk. Our guide to body doubling for ADHD explains why working next to someone can help.

Privacy defaults: Plexii describes itself as local-first, with no third-party analytics in the app. AI runs on its credits, on your own Anthropic or OpenAI key, or on a model that runs on your computer.

Where might Plexii fall short?

A lot of app: Beyond desks, Plexii includes a full office suite, mail, chat, calls, e-signatures and a password vault. If big tools tend to turn into a setup project for you, that breadth is a real risk.

Computer only: There is no iPhone or Android app, so it can't help when the stuck moment happens away from your desk. On a phone, its site offers to email you the download link instead.

Tight free plan: Free allows three desks and 100 AI credits a month on Claude Haiku. Body doubling and Plan my day, two of the most ADHD-relevant features, need Pro.

Camera on for body doubling: Every body doubling mode starts with your camera on, though either person can turn it off. That suits some people and puts others off.

Little outside evidence: Plexii isn't in an app store, so there are no public ratings, and we found no independent reviews. The ADHD Products Directory lists it as a new tool, and the app ships updates often (version 4.3.48 when we checked).

How much does Plexii cost?

Plexii is free for personal use, with up to three desks. Pro is US$12 a month or $120 a year, Pro+ is $25 a month or $250 a year, and Team is $15 per seat a month or $150 a year, per its pricing page.

Every new account starts with a 14-day trial of Team features and 300 AI credits, with no credit card. When the trial ends, the account carries on as Free unless you upgrade.

Is Plexii worth it?

It could be if you do long, messy work at a computer and keep losing the thread between apps. The free plan and the trial make it cheap to find out on one real project.

FAQ

Is Plexii free?

Yes, for personal use. The free plan includes up to three desks, 100 AI credits a month and sync on up to three devices.

How much is Plexii Pro?

Pro is US$12 a month or $120 a year. Pro+ is $25 a month or $250 a year.

Is Plexii the same as PlexiiDesk?

Yes. Plexii says the name PlexiiDesk refers to the same product, and its help centre still uses that name.

Does Plexii have a phone app?

No. Plexii runs on Mac and Windows, and its site says Linux is planned.

Does Plexii have body doubling?

Yes, on Pro, Pro+ and Team. It pairs you at random with another member on camera, in one of four modes from Silent to Happy to talk.

Is Plexii good for ADHD?

It may suit adults with ADHD who lose context between apps at a computer. It is new, with no public ratings or independent reviews yet, so try it on one real project first.
