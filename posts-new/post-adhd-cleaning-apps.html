Why is cleaning so hard with ADHD?

Because cleaning is mostly decisions, not scrubbing. Where do you start, what counts as done, and what comes next all lean on executive function, the brain's system for planning and starting, which Russell Barkley's model of ADHD puts at the center of the condition.

So the hard part is usually the gap between "I should clean" and actually starting. FOCO calls that gap a task initiation deficit, and our guide to it explains why it is not laziness.

What makes a cleaning app ADHD-friendly?

Three things, and each app below leans on at least one of them.

Specific first steps: A meta-analysis by Gollwitzer and Sheeran found that planning when and how you will act had a "medium-to-large" effect on reaching goals (d = .65) across 94 tests.

What are the best ADHD cleaning apps?

These are the cleaning apps we recommend, grouped by where they help. We did not test them hands-on; every fact comes from their App Store and Google Play listings, their websites and our own reviews.

1. FOCO: best when you know the chore but cannot start

FOCO, the app we make, is not only for cleaning, but it is built for the moment you know what to do and still cannot begin. Type "clean the bathroom," and AI splits it into small steps, each with a time estimate.

A focus timer then runs with a body-doubling companion on screen. It costs $59 a year with a 7-day free trial, or $9 a month billed right away, on iPhone and Android.

2. Clean Your Room: best when you look at the mess and freeze

You take one photo of the room, and its AI writes specific steps like "Pick up the backpack and hang it up," each marked on your photo. You choose 5 items, 10 items or the whole room.

Its listing says the first 3 room scans are free, and unlimited scans need a subscription. It launched in January 2026, is on iPhone only, and sends your photos to Google Gemini to generate the steps.

3. Nudge: best when you only have energy for one thing

Nudge hands you one tiny task, a short timer and a clear place to stop, with modes like "Start Rescue" and "Panic Clean" for before guests arrive. A cartoon cat called Mochi keeps you quiet company, with no camera or live partner.

Your first task comes before any subscription. After that it needs a weekly, monthly or yearly plan; the monthly and yearly plans have a 7-day free trial.

It launched in May 2026 and has 27 ratings so far.

4. ADHD Cleaning Planner Schedule: best when rewards keep you going

This iPhone app works room by room, with small tasks, a timer and 10 levels from "Soggy Sponge" to "Vibey Vacuum." A "Help, I can't start!" button gives you a countdown when starting is the problem.

You can also take before and after photos and record timelapse videos. It has 4.9 stars from 121 ratings and has been out since December 2025.

5. Goblin Tools: best free way to shrink a big job

Goblin Tools' Magic ToDo turns "clean the kitchen" into a checklist of steps in seconds. The website is free with no account, and the iPhone app costs $0.99 once on the US App Store.

It gives you the steps but has no timer, reminders or schedule. See our FOCO vs Goblin Tools comparison.

6. Sweepy: best for sharing chores with a household

Sweepy tracks how clean each room is and builds a daily schedule for each person in your home, with points and a leaderboard. The core app is free, and Premium has a monthly or yearly plan with a 3-day trial.

Read our full Sweepy app review for what Premium adds.

7. Tody: best when fixed schedules make you quit

Tody tracks chores by "indicators of actual need rather than arbitrary dates," with bars that fill as a room gets dirtier. Deadlines, alarms and notifications are optional.

That matters if a list of overdue chores makes you give up. Tody has been on the App Store since 2013 and has 4.8 stars from 10,910 US ratings.

8. FlyLady: best when you want a ready-made routine

The FlyLady app is built on Marla Cilley's FlyLady Method, with preloaded morning, afternoon and evening routines and cleaning lists organized by zone. Its listing says you can start free with no login, and a subscription unlocks everything.

It is the most-rated app here, with 4.9 stars from 23,470 US ratings. It suits people who want someone else to decide what to clean today.

9. Dubbii: best when you clean better with someone

Dubbii runs live body doubling sessions with real people, plus follow-along cleaning videos and gentle reminders. It comes from ADHD Love, and has 4.8 stars from 778 US ratings.

See our Dubbii review. For a free option with no booking, try our focus room.

How do you make a cleaning app actually work?

  1. Pick one app. Two cleaning apps means two places to forget.
  2. Start with one room. Set up the room that bothers you most, not the whole house.
  3. Make the first step tiny. "Put the cups in the sink" is a step; "clean the kitchen" is a project.
  4. Set a short timer. Ten minutes is enough to count as a win.
  5. Use a "bare minimum" list for bad days. Our free ADHD cleaning planner has one you can print.
  6. Skip the guilt. If the app shows a pile of overdue tasks, clear them and start from today.

Are cleaning apps good for ADHD?

They can help, but none of these apps has published research of its own showing it improves cleaning for people with ADHD. They work by borrowing methods with some evidence, like specific plans and body doubling.

If ADHD makes daily life hard well beyond cleaning, talk to a clinician. The National Institute of Mental Health explains how ADHD is diagnosed and treated.

FAQ

What is the best cleaning app for ADHD?

It depends on where you get stuck. FOCO, Clean Your Room or Nudge help you start, Goblin Tools shrinks a big job, Sweepy, Tody or FlyLady give you a plan, and Dubbii gives you company.

Is there a free ADHD cleaning app?

Goblin Tools' website is free with no account. Sweepy's core app and FlyLady's starter version are free, and Clean Your Room gives you 3 free room scans.

Is there an app that tells you what to clean from a photo?

Yes. Clean Your Room turns one photo of your room into specific steps marked on the picture, and Nudge has a "Scan My Mess" mode that turns a room photo into a starting point.

What is the best cleaning app for a family?

Sweepy and Tody both let several people in a home share chores across devices. Sweepy adds points and a leaderboard.

Does body doubling help with cleaning?

In one survey of 220 neurodivergent adults, 85% said they were more likely to finish a task with someone there. Dubbii runs live cleaning sessions with real people, and FOCO and Nudge put a companion on screen while you work.

Is Tody or Sweepy better for ADHD?

Tody suits you if deadlines make you quit, since it tracks need, not dates. Sweepy suits a household that wants a schedule for each person and a game to share it.
