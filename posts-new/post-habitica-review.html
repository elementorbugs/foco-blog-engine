What is Habitica?

Habitica is a habit tracker and to-do app built like a pixel-art role-playing game. It began in 2013 as HabitRPG, a Kickstarter project by Tyler Renelle, and is run by HabitRPG, Inc., with its code open source on GitHub (Habitica on GitHub).

You create an avatar, add your tasks, and earn gold and experience when you check them off in real life. Its App Store listing names ADHD first in the list of things it can help with.

How does Habitica work?

Habitica splits your list into three task types, plus a shop for rewards.

  • Habits: things you do any number of times a day, with a plus or minus button. Good habits earn gold and experience; bad ones cost health.
  • Dailies: tasks on a schedule, like "take meds" every morning. They build a streak, and missing one costs health when the day resets.
  • To-Dos: one-off tasks. They reward you when done and never cost health.
  • Rewards: spend your gold on gear, health potions, or custom real-life treats you set yourself, like "buy a coffee."

At level 10 you choose a class, and with friends you can form a Party to fight bosses. On a boss quest your finished tasks deal damage to the boss, but your missed Dailies hurt everyone in the party.

Habitica closed its public Tavern chat and Guilds on August 8, 2023, citing low use and new online safety laws, though its App Store listing still mentions Guilds. Parties, private messages and public Challenges remain.

What happens when you die in Habitica?

If your health reaches zero, you lose one level, that level's stat point, all your gold and one piece of equipment, which you can buy back later. Your tasks stay, so you can pick up where you left off.

You can avoid it by drinking a health potion (15 HP for 25 gold) or turning on Pause Damage in Settings. Paused players still take damage from their party's missed Dailies during a boss quest, per Habitica's own FAQ.

Is Habitica free?

Yes. Habitica says it "can be fully enjoyed for free," and every task feature, quest and pet works without paying.

The optional subscription costs $4.99 a month, $14.99 for three months, $29.99 for six months or $47.99 a year on the US App Store. It adds cosmetic extras: a monthly avatar item, a Mystic Hourglass, double item drops, two gems a month and the ability to buy gems with gold.

Gems are Habitica's premium currency, used for quests, backgrounds and some pets. You can buy them in packs from $0.99 for 4 to $19.99 for 84, win them in Challenges, or, as a subscriber, buy up to 50 a month with gold.

Is Habitica good for ADHD?

It can be, especially if you're motivated by games and quick rewards. Research suggests ADHD involves differences in the brain's reward system, and people with ADHD tend to respond more strongly to immediate rewards than delayed ones (Tripp and Wickens, 2009).

Gold after every checked-off task is exactly that kind of immediate reward. Party quests add outside accountability, the same idea behind body doubling.

The risk is the punishment. In a study of 45 Habitica users, every participant experienced some counterproductive effects, including "being punished by Habitica in especially productive times," and feeling the system was unfair predicted falling motivation (Diefenbach and Müssig, 2019).

Gamification's boost also tends to be modest and fade. A 2022 meta-analysis of 16 trials found a small-to-medium effect that shrank at follow-up (Mazeas and colleagues, JMIR), and Habitica is not an ADHD treatment; see the NIMH ADHD overview.

How do you set up Habitica for an ADHD brain?

A few settings make the difference between a fun game and a pile of red tasks. These all come from Habitica's own features.

  1. Keep Dailies to three or four. Put everything else in To-Dos, which never cost health.
  2. Set Custom Day Start to when you actually go to bed, so a late night doesn't count as a missed day.
  3. Use Pause Damage in Settings for sick days, travel or burnout, instead of quitting.
  4. Add checklists to big tasks; finished checklist items reduce the damage if the Daily stays undone.
  5. Make real rewards cheap and specific, like "10 minutes of a game for 15 gold," so the gold means something.

If missed days still make you want to delete it, that's useful information, not a failure. Our Habitica alternatives list covers gentler options.

Habitica vs Finch: which is better?

Both are free and gamified, but they feel opposite. Habitica rewards and punishes like an RPG, while Finch is a gentle self-care pet that never makes you lose anything.

Pick Habitica if challenge and friends motivate you, and Finch if guilt is your main enemy. Our Finch vs Habitica comparison and Finch app review go deeper.

How do you delete a Habitica account?

Go to Settings and find the Danger Zone, then choose to delete your account. Habitica says this is permanent, gems are not refunded, and deletion can take up to 24 hours.

If you pay through the App Store or Google Play, cancel the subscription there first. Habitica's text doesn't say that deleting the account cancels a store subscription.

FAQ

Is Habitica free?

Yes. Habitica works fully for free, and its subscription, from $4.99 a month to $47.99 a year, only adds cosmetic and gem extras.

What is the difference between Habits and Dailies in Habitica?

Habits can be tapped any number of times a day and have no schedule. Dailies run on a schedule, build a streak and cost health if you miss them.

What happens when you die in Habitica?

You lose one level, that level's stat point, all your gold and one piece of equipment, which you can buy back. Your tasks stay.

How do you get gems in Habitica?

Buy them in packs, win them in Challenges, or, with a subscription, buy up to 50 a month with gold and get two free each month.

Can you pause damage in Habitica?

Yes. Turn on Pause Damage in Settings, though you can still take damage from your party's missed Dailies during a boss quest.

Is Habitica good for ADHD?

Many people with ADHD like its instant rewards and party accountability. Its penalties for missed Dailies can backfire, so keep Dailies few and use Pause Damage on hard weeks.

Is Habitica open source?

Yes. Its code is open source on GitHub, and volunteers contribute art, translations and fixes.
