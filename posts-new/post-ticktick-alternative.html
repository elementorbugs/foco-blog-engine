Why do people look for a TickTick alternative?

Usually not because TickTick is bad. TickTick combines a to-do list, calendar views, habits, a Pomo focus timer, Kanban and an Eisenhower matrix in one app, and its free plan is generous.

The catch is what all that range invites. Tagging, filtering and rearranging columns can feel like progress while the task you opened the app for stays where it was.

That is not laziness. It is often task initiation, the gap between knowing what to do and beginning, which is one of the executive function skills ADHD affects.

Some people also hit the free plan's limits: TickTick's pricing page lists 9 lists, 99 tasks per list and 5 habits on Free (TickTick Premium page). If those limits are your only issue, Premium may be cheaper than switching.

What are the best TickTick alternatives for ADHD?

1. FOCO, best for starting a task you keep avoiding

If you can build a perfectly tagged list and still not touch the task, the organizer was never the bottleneck. That is task paralysis, and FOCO, the app we make, is built for that moment.

You pick the stuck task and AI breaks it into small steps, each with a time estimate. Then a focus timer with calm music and an on-screen body doubling companion stays with you through step one.

The honest trade-off: FOCO has a built-in calendar and reminders, but no Kanban board, Eisenhower matrix or habit tracker. It runs on iPhone and Android only, with no web app.

Price: 7-day free trial, then $9/month or $59/year. There is no free tier (checked Oct 9, 2026).

2. Habitica, best for turning habits into a game

TickTick's habit tracker is useful but quiet. Habitica turns habits, dailies and to-dos into a retro RPG: finish one and you earn gold and experience; miss dailies and your character loses health.

For brains that respond to immediate, visible reward, that loop can help. The flip side is the penalty, which can add guilt on top of a task you were already avoiding, so notice how it feels after a rough week.

Price: free to use; optional subscription $4.99/month up to $47.99/year on the US App Store, and Habitica notes prices vary by location (checked Oct 9, 2026). More options in our Habitica alternatives guide.

3. Tiimo, best for a visual day built for neurodivergent brains

If TickTick's lists don't stick but you want structure, Tiimo replaces the list with a color-coded timeline designed for ADHD and autistic users. It adds routines, reminders and a focus timer around the plan.

Its Android app was rebuilt in 2026, and Tiimo says it is still adding features to match iOS. The full app, including the web version, needs Pro.

Price: free version with some planning tools; Pro monthly or yearly at a price that varies by country, with a 7-day trial on the yearly plan only (Tiimo pricing, checked Oct 9, 2026).

4. Structured, best for one clean timeline

Structured shows a single timeline of your day, with each task as a block sized to how long it takes. There is less around it than in TickTick or Tiimo, which can make it easier to keep opening.

It is no longer Apple-only: Structured launched on Android in November 2023 and on the web in August 2024, though some iOS features are still missing there (Structured help).

Price: almost every feature is free; Pro is monthly, yearly or lifetime, priced by region (checked Oct 9, 2026). Compare it head to head in Tiimo vs Structured.

5. Todoist, best when TickTick's extra layers are the problem

If you never open Kanban or the Eisenhower matrix, Todoist keeps things to a fast, plain list with good quick capture.

The ADHD trap here is building nested projects all over again. One "Today" view, capture as things come up, and skip due dates that aren't real.

Price: free Beginner plan with 5 personal projects; Pro $7/month or $60/year, with a 7-day Pro trial (Todoist pricing FAQ, checked Oct 9, 2026). See also Todoist alternatives.

6. Sunsama, best for a guided daily planning ritual

Sunsama is the opposite of TickTick's self-serve toolbox. It walks you through planning each day, pulls in tasks and calendar events, and keeps the plan realistic.

It is built around the desktop app; the phone app is a companion that works after you plan your first day on desktop. If you tend to skip long setup steps, a daily ritual carries the same risk as TickTick's settings.

Price: no free plan, 14-day free trial, and the Pro card on Sunsama's pricing page shows $22/month monthly or $17/month billed yearly, while its FAQ quotes $25 and $20, so confirm at checkout (checked Oct 9, 2026). More in our Sunsama alternatives guide.

Who should stay with TickTick?

  • You use several of its features every week, so one app replaces three or four.
  • You open it and do the task, rather than reorganizing first.
  • You only outgrew the free limits; Premium at US$49.99 a year costs less than most apps here.

FAQ

What is the best TickTick alternative for ADHD?

It depends on what breaks. FOCO for starting a stuck task, Habitica for motivation, Tiimo or Structured for a visual day, Todoist for a simpler list.

Is there a free alternative to TickTick?

Yes. Habitica is free to play, Structured offers almost every feature free, and Todoist and Tiimo have free versions; FOCO has a 7-day free trial, then a subscription.

How much is TickTick Premium?

TickTick's pricing page lists Premium at US$49.99 a year, which it describes as less than US$4.17 a month. The free plan stays available.

Does TickTick help with task initiation?

Not directly. It organizes and times tasks you have already defined, but it does not break a task you are avoiding into small, timed steps.

Is Structured only on iPhone?

No. Structured launched on Android in November 2023 and on the web in August 2024, though some iOS features are still missing there.

Can FOCO replace TickTick completely?

Partly. FOCO has a calendar and reminders but no habit tracker, Kanban or web app, so some people keep TickTick for organizing and use FOCO to start.
