Why do people look for a Goblin Tools alternative?

Usually not because Goblin Tools is bad. Its makers describe a free website with no account needed, nine small single-purpose tools, and a promise that existing features will never move behind a paywall (goblin.tools About).

We gave Magic ToDo a real task on October 9, 2026. "Clean the kitchen" became six sensible steps in a few seconds, and the "spiciness" control sets how small the steps get.

The reasons people move on are about what comes after the list. Goblin Tools has no day planner and no countdown timer, so the steps don't land on a schedule and nothing keeps time while you work.

The bigger gap is task initiation, the jump from knowing the steps to doing the first one. A tidy list can still sit there untouched, and that is the gap our guide to task initiation deficit explains.

What are the best Goblin Tools alternatives for ADHD?

1. FOCO, best for starting a stuck task

FOCO comes first because its use case is the one Goblin Tools leaves open: starting. We make it, so weigh this entry with that in mind.

FOCO's AI breaks a stuck task into small steps and gives each one a time estimate. Then focus mode puts step one on screen with a timer, optional calm music and an AI body doubling companion that stays with you while you work.

It also takes voice capture and a photo of a handwritten list, and puts tasks into a built-in calendar where missed tasks move on instead of piling up as overdue. The trade-off is cost: Goblin Tools is free on the web, and FOCO is not.

Price: 7-day free trial, then $9/month or $59/year, on iPhone and Android (checked Oct 9, 2026).

2. Tiimo, best for a visual plan of your whole day

Tiimo is built for neurodivergent users and puts tasks on a color-coded timeline with icons, so you can see the shape of the day.

It adds routines, reminders and a focus timer, which can help if time blindness is part of your story.

The trade-off is setup and price: you plan the day before it helps, and most features need Pro. We compare the two directly in Goblin Tools vs Tiimo.

Price: free version with a selection of tools; Pro monthly or yearly, priced by country, with a 7-day trial on the yearly plan only, per Tiimo pricing (checked Oct 9, 2026).

3. Structured, best free day planner

Structured shows one day as a single timeline of colored blocks, each with a start and end time. Structured says almost every feature is free, which makes it the closest match to Goblin's price for people who want a planner (What is Structured Pro?).

The trade-off is that it plans tasks you already know how to do. It has AI planning on Pro, but it won't sit with you through a step you are avoiding.

Price: free version with almost every feature; Pro monthly, yearly or lifetime, priced by region (checked Oct 9, 2026).

4. Llama Life, best for a countdown on every task

Llama Life gives each task its own countdown timer and shows when your list will be finished, so one step can't quietly eat the afternoon.

The trade-off is that you bring the steps yourself, since it times tasks rather than breaking them down.

Price: 7-day free trial, then $6/month or $39/year on the US App Store, which also lists other plans; the iOS app is iPhone only (checked Oct 9, 2026).

5. Saner.AI, best for dumping a messy brain

Saner.AI calls itself an AI personal assistant for ADHD. You throw in notes, tasks and emails, and the AI organizes them, pulls out tasks and can break them into steps, according to its App Store listing.

The trade-off is breadth: it is a whole AI workspace, which is more than some people want. Its US App Store rating is low so far, 3.0 from 13 ratings.

Price: free plan with 30 AI messages a month; Starter $8/month and Standard $16/month on Saner.AI pricing, with higher in-app prices on the App Store (checked Oct 9, 2026).

6. Todoist, best for one dependable list

If you mostly need to stop forgetting things, Todoist is a fast list that syncs everywhere. Its Ramble feature lets you speak tasks and have Todoist structure them.

The trade-off is that a list is still a list, and it is easy to over-build. More options are in our Todoist alternatives guide.

Price: free Beginner plan with 5 personal projects; Pro $7 a month or $60 a year since a December 2025 price change (Todoist help center) for web prices (checked Oct 9, 2026).

Who should stay with Goblin Tools?

  • You only need a quick breakdown when a task feels too big, and then you are fine.
  • You don't want another subscription or another app to set up.
  • You like the extra tools, like Formalizer or Judge, and use them often.

FAQ

Is there a free alternative to Goblin Tools?

Yes. Structured has a free version with almost every feature, Todoist has a free plan, and Tiimo and Saner.AI have limited free versions.

What is the best Goblin Tools alternative for ADHD?

It depends on why you are leaving. Pick FOCO to start a stuck task, Tiimo for a visual day plan, or Structured for a free, simple timeline.

Is Goblin Tools still free in 2026?

Yes, the website is free and needs no account. The mobile apps are a one-time purchase ($1.99 on the US App Store), and an optional Pro subscription covers only advanced features.

Does Goblin Tools save my lists?

Yes, if you create an optional account, your lists sync. The account is not required to use the tools.

Does Goblin Tools have a timer?

No, it has no countdown timer. Llama Life and FOCO both put a timer on the task you are working on.

Can I use Goblin Tools with another app?

Yes. You can break a task down in Goblin Tools, then place the steps in a planner like Tiimo or Structured, or start step one in a focus app.
