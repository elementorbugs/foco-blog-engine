Which one is built for ADHD?

Tiimo. It is designed for neurodivergent people first, and its App Store listing says it was co-designed with ADHD and autism experts.

In practice that means routines shown with icons, a calm look with few choices per screen, and a focus on what comes next. Todoist is a general task manager with more than 50 million users, built for speed and structure rather than calm.

Both try to take load off executive function, the brain skills behind planning, prioritizing and holding steps in mind (our executive function guide explains more). Psychologist Russell Barkley describes ADHD as a problem of self-regulation rather than knowledge (Barkley fact sheet), which is why outside structure like these apps can help.

Which is better at getting tasks out of your head?

Todoist, by a wide margin. Capture is what it does best, and with ADHD's working memory differences, a thought that is not written down right away often slips.

Its Ramble feature lets you talk instead of type, and Todoist turns what you say into tasks with dates and projects. Quick Add understands plain sentences too: type "Submit report every Friday at 4pm #Work" and it sets the date, the repeat and the project for you.

Tiimo's capture is different. You brain dump into its AI Co-planner, and it turns the list into timed tasks on your day, which is slower than a one-line Quick Add but does the scheduling at the same time.

On Todoist's free plan Ramble comes in limited sessions; Pro makes it unlimited (Todoist pricing).

Which helps more with time blindness?

Tiimo, clearly. Time blindness, trouble sensing how long things take or how much time has passed, is the exact problem its visual day is built for (our time blindness guide goes deeper; CHADD notes that many adults with ADHD have difficulties with day-to-day responsibilities).

Tiimo groups the day into morning, day and evening, gives each block a start and end time, and keeps the next thing in front of you. Todoist can show tasks on a calendar, but its calendar layout is a Pro feature, and its home screen is a list of what is due, not a picture of the day.

If your tasks are mostly "when" problems (getting out the door, switching between activities, losing an afternoon), Tiimo fits. If they are mostly "what" problems (too many things, nothing written down), Todoist fits.

Which one works with the calendar you already use?

It depends on your calendar. Todoist's Calendar integration connects Google Calendar or Outlook Calendar on every plan, including the free one, though only one provider at a time (Todoist calendar help).

Tiimo's listing names Apple Calendar and Apple Reminders. In recent App Store reviews, several people who live in Outlook or Google Calendar said they could not connect them, so check before you commit if your work calendar is there.

Which keeps bigger projects organized?

Todoist. It handles projects with sub-tasks, sections, labels, filters and priorities, and any project can switch between a list, a board and (on Pro) a calendar.

It also works for shared life admin: share a project, assign tasks, leave comments. Tiimo can share up to five profiles, which suits a family routine, but it is not built for a home renovation or a work launch.

The flip side is that Todoist's flexibility can become its own project. Labels, filters and perfectly named sections can feel like progress while the actual task waits.

Which helps you stay on task once you start?

Tiimo is the only one of the two with a built-in timer. Its visual countdown sits on the task in front of you, and its widgets keep it on your home or lock screen.

Todoist has no focus timer. It gives you due dates, reminders and priorities, and leaves the doing to you.

Can either app break a big task into steps?

Yes, both can, with different limits. Tiimo's AI Co-planner can split a larger task into steps with a time for each, on iOS and the web (Tiimo AI planning).

Todoist's Task Assist has a "Break task down" option that suggests sub-tasks you can review and add. Its help center lists it for Pro and Business plans and says it is not available in the iPhone and Android apps (Todoist Task Assist help).

So if you want AI breakdown on your iPhone, Tiimo has the edge today. If you plan at a desk, Todoist's version works there.

Which is cheaper?

Todoist, for most people. Its free plan covers more of the core app, with 5 personal projects, task reminders and calendar sync, and Pro is a clear $6.99 a month or $59.99 a year on the US App Store.

Tiimo's free version has a basic planner and limited AI chats, and the web app needs Pro. Tiimo does not publish one fixed price because it varies by country; its US App Store in-app purchases run from $7 to $54, and the 7-day free trial applies to the yearly plan only (Tiimo pricing).

What are the downsides of each app?

Tiimo: it is light on project structure, so long multi-step work and shared projects fit awkwardly. The full app, including the web version, needs Pro, and there is no Windows app.

Todoist: it shows you everything but doesn't pace any of it. With no timer and a list-first home screen, a long Today list can feel like a wall, and the calendar layout sits on Pro.

How do you set up Todoist so it doesn't become a wall?

If you pick Todoist, a little setup keeps the Today list from turning into a guilt screen. These habits come up again and again in ADHD planning advice:

  • One Inbox. Capture everything there first and sort later, so capturing never needs a decision.
  • Three things for today. Give only three tasks a date of today; everything else gets a later date or none.
  • Priority 1 means today. Keep p1 for what must happen today, and filter your view to it when the list feels loud.
  • Few projects. Start with Home, Work and Someday, and add more only when one gets crowded.

Tiimo needs less of this, because the day view already limits what you see. The trade-off is that you plan the day before you can lean on it.

Can you use Tiimo and Todoist together?

Yes, and it can work well if each app has exactly one job. Todoist holds everything you need to do; Tiimo holds only today.

A simple daily hand-off: every evening or morning, pick two or three tasks from Todoist and add them to Tiimo's day as timed blocks. During the day you live in Tiimo; new ideas still go straight into Todoist's Inbox.

The risk is upkeep. Two apps means two places to update, so if the hand-off stops happening, drop back to one app rather than letting both go stale.

What do App Store reviewers say about each app?

We read the 100 most recent US App Store reviews of each app on October 18, 2026 (July to October for Tiimo, June to October for Todoist). Recent reviews skew more critical than the overall ratings: they averaged 3.75 stars for Tiimo and 3.97 for Todoist.

Tiimo reviewers most often praise the calm, ADHD-friendly design and routines, with comments like "it's the reason I actually make my bed." The most common complaints were an AI assistant that misreads requests, bugs after updates, and charges around the end of the free trial.

Todoist reviewers most often praise fast capture, Ramble and reliability, and many have used it for years. The most common complaints were a crash after a recent update, reminders that are easy to miss, the subscription price, and no iCloud calendar sync.

What does a Monday morning look like in each app?

In Todoist, you open Today and see eleven tasks, two overdue, sorted by priority. You Ramble three new ones on the way to work ("call the dentist tomorrow at 9, renew passport this week, buy a birthday gift"), and they land in the right projects with dates.

In Tiimo, you open the app and see the morning as a few colored blocks with icons: shower, breakfast, leave at 8:30, deep work until 11. The current block is in focus, and a countdown shows how long is left before the next one.

Neither picture is wrong. The question is which one you would rather open at 8 AM.

Does either app help you start a task you're avoiding?

Partly. Both can now break work into smaller pieces, which takes some weight off the first step.

But both start from organizing: your tasks in Todoist, your day in Tiimo. If everything is captured and scheduled and you still freeze, that is task initiation, the gap between knowing what to do and doing it.

FAQ

Is Todoist good for ADHD?

It is good at the part of ADHD that forgets things: fast capture, reminders and one trusted list. It does less for time blindness or starting, since it has no timer and no visual day.

Is Tiimo better than Todoist?

For time and routines, yes. For capturing tasks and managing projects, Todoist is stronger.

Is Todoist free?

Yes, Todoist has a free plan with 5 personal projects and task reminders. Pro is $6.99 a month or $59.99 a year on the US App Store.

Is Tiimo free?

Tiimo has a free version with a basic planner and limited AI chats. The full app, including the web version, needs Pro, with a 7-day trial on the yearly plan.

Does Todoist have a focus timer?

No. Todoist has due dates, reminders and priorities, but no built-in focus or Pomodoro timer.

Can I use Tiimo and Todoist together?

Yes, and it can work well if each app has one job. See the workflow above: Todoist holds everything, Tiimo holds today.
