Pomofocus App Review: Is the Free Pomodoro Timer Good for ADHD?

Short answer: Pomofocus is a free Pomodoro timer that runs in your browser on desktop and phone, with a task list and a finish-time estimate, but no official iPhone or Android app. It suits people who can already start and just want a clean timer.

  • Try Pomofocus if you work at a computer, like the Pomodoro rhythm, and want something free that opens in one click.
  • Skip it if you need a real phone app with reminders, or the timer is not what stops you.
  • Pick FOCO, the app we make, if the hard part is starting, and you want a stuck task split into small timed steps on your phone.

What does Pomofocus look like?

The screens below are screenshots we took of the free web version on November 9, 2026. We only opened it and added three sample tasks, which is not a hands-on test.

You get one big 25:00 timer with Pomodoro, Short Break and Long Break tabs. On a phone, the same page shrinks to fit the screen.

Below the timer sits a task list. Each task gets an estimate in pomodoros, and the bottom bar adds them up into a finish time.

Is there a Pomofocus app for iPhone or Android?

No, not an official one. Pomofocus says it "works on desktop & mobile browser," and its site offers downloads only for Mac, Windows and Linux.

A Google Play app called "Pomofocus" exists, but it is listed by a different developer, Micra Solution, not by FocusLab. Check the developer name before you install anything with that name.

On a phone, you can open pomofocus.io and add it to your home screen. The site's web manifest is set up so it then opens in its own window, like an app, though without the reminders a native app can send.

Is Pomofocus good for ADHD?

It can help with one part of the problem: keeping going once you have started. A visible countdown gives time a shape, which many people with time blindness find useful (our guide to time blindness in ADHD explains why).

The Pomodoro Technique itself is a time management method from Francesco Cirillo, not an ADHD treatment. We found no study testing Pomofocus with ADHD, so treat it as a tool to try, and see CHADD for evidence-based support.

Where it does less is the moment before the timer. ADHD is linked to trouble with executive function, and the hardest step is often task initiation: you know the task and still cannot begin it.

A Start button does not change how big "Draft project outline" feels.

What does Pomofocus get right?

Zero setup: There is no sign-up, no onboarding and nothing to install. When starting anything feels heavy, that matters.

A finish-time estimate: Adding pomodoro estimates gives you a "Finish At" time for the day. That turns a vague list into a rough plan you can see.

Adjustable sessions: You can change the 25, 5 and 15 minute defaults, so a 10-minute first session is easy (our 10-minute timer is built on the same idea). Auto-start for breaks and pomodoros is off by default and can be switched on.

Sounds and reports: The free version includes alarm and background sounds, templates and daily, weekly and monthly reports, according to its site.

Where might Pomofocus fall short?

No real phone app: If your day runs on your phone, a browser tab is easy to lose. There are no push reminders for tasks.

You do the breaking down: Estimating pomodoros assumes you already know the steps. If a task is too foggy to estimate, the list will not help you find the first move.

A browser is full of distractions: The timer lives one tab away from everything else. Pomofocus does not block sites, so you may want a separate blocker.

Ads on the free version: "No Ads" is listed as a Premium feature, so the free version may show ads.

How much does Pomofocus cost?

The core timer is free. Premium adds projects, yearly reports, CSV download, unlimited templates (free allows 3), Todoist and webhook integrations, and no ads.

Pomofocus does not publish the Premium price on its site, and its legal notice only says prices are "listed on each product page." A third-party review from October 2026 lists $3 a month, $18 a year or $54 lifetime, but we could not confirm that on pomofocus.io, so check the upgrade screen before you pay.

FAQ

Is Pomofocus free?

Yes. The timer, task list, templates, reports and sounds are free, and Premium is an optional paid upgrade.

Is there a Pomofocus app for iPhone?

No. Pomofocus runs in mobile browsers and has desktop apps for Mac, Windows and Linux, but no official iPhone or Android app.

Is the Pomofocus app on Google Play official?

The Google Play app named "Pomofocus" is listed by Micra Solution, a different developer from FocusLab, which makes pomofocus.io.

Is Pomofocus safe?

It is run by FocusLab LLC, which publishes a legal notice, terms and privacy page. Its site warns that the Windows app is not signed, so Windows may show a security warning when you open it.

How much is Pomofocus Premium?

Pomofocus does not list the price on its site. A third-party review from October 2026 lists $3 a month, $18 a year or $54 lifetime, which we could not confirm.

Is Pomofocus good for ADHD?

It can help you keep going once you have started, thanks to a visible countdown and adjustable sessions. It does not help break a task down or get you started.
