Sunsama Review: Is the Daily Planner Worth It for ADHD?

Sunsama is a paid daily planner that walks you through planning each morning, pulls tasks from your other tools and puts them on a calendar. It costs $17 to $22 a month with no free plan, and it suits people who like the ritual and work mostly on a computer.

  • Try Sunsama if your tasks are scattered across work tools and a guided morning routine calms you down.
  • Skip it if you plan mainly on your phone, you tend to drop routines after a few days, or $204 a year is too much.
  • Pick FOCO, the app we make, if the plan is not the problem and starting is, and you want one stuck task split into timed steps.

What does Sunsama look like in use?

We did not test Sunsama hands-on. The images below come from its website and App Store listing, so they show the app as the company presents it.

The main view puts your days in columns, with tasks you can drag onto a calendar beside them. Each task carries a time estimate, so you can see when a day is already full.

The phone app is smaller on purpose. Its listing calls it a "companion to the desktop app," and says you can use it once you have planned your first day on desktop.

Is Sunsama good for ADHD?

It can be, if a set routine helps you, though it was not built for ADHD and its listing never mentions it. The fit depends on whether you will keep doing the daily ritual.

Some users say it works well for them. One App Store review from 2025 is titled "Perfect for ADHD and OCD," and a review written for ADHD and autistic readers credits the guided ritual and workload warnings.

That same review says the plan fell apart when the desktop ritual was skipped. Planning leans on executive function, the skills behind organizing and following through, and ADHD can make that kind of routine harder to sustain.

What does Sunsama get right for ADHD brains?

A guided start to the day: The planning ritual walks you through what to do today, which takes away some of the "where do I even begin" decisions. A similar shutdown ritual closes out the day.

A check on overplanning: Carly's review describes a workload check that warns when you plan too much. That can help if your plans tend to ignore how many hours a day has.

Time you can see: Time estimates and calendar blocks make the day visible, which may help with time blindness, trouble sensing how long things take (our time blindness guide goes deeper).

One list from many tools: Carly's review lists Asana, Jira, Notion, Slack, Todoist, Trello, Gmail and Outlook among its integrations. If remembering where a task lives is half the battle, this may be its biggest strength.

A forgiving trial: Sunsama's billing guide says you are not charged automatically when the 14 days end. That matters if you tend to forget to cancel things.

Where does Sunsama fall short?

The price: At $204 a year it is a real commitment, and there is no free plan to fall back on. Non-US users get no regional discount, according to its billing guide.

It depends on the routine: The ritual is the product. If you tend to drop routines after the first week, you may be paying for a step you skip (our piece on why ADHD planner apps get abandoned covers that pattern).

A limited phone app: The listing says it is not a standalone replacement for desktop. An App Store review from 2022 calls it underdeveloped for the price, and the 2025 ADHD review above says it "bugged out," though the app has had major updates since both.

It plans, but does not get you started: A neat plan can still leave you stuck on the first task. Sunsama's focus view helps once you are moving, but breaking down a frozen task is mostly left to you or to its assistant.

Data deletion after you cancel: The billing guide says workspace data is permanently deleted 30 days after a subscription ends. Export anything you want to keep before then.

How much does Sunsama cost?

Its help center lists $22 per user per month billed monthly, or $17 per month billed as $204 a year. Older sources still quote $20 and $25, so check the pricing page at checkout.

There is a 14-day trial and no free plan. Students with a college email and verified nonprofits can ask support for special pricing.

Does Sunsama have AI features?

Yes. Its help guide says an assistant called Sunny can create and move tasks, add subtasks, plan your day and organize your backlog, on desktop, iPhone and Android.

A feature called Autoplan can also place tasks on your calendar, and recent release notes show it being updated through the fall of 2026. Our AI calendar guide compares this kind of tool.

Is Sunsama worth it?

It can be if you use the ritual most days and your work is spread across several tools. For that person, one calm daily view may be worth the money.

Use the full 14 days on real workdays, including a bad one. If you skip the morning ritual twice in that time, take that as your answer, and see our Sunsama alternatives or Motion alternatives instead.

How is Sunsama different from FOCO?

Sunsama helps you decide what today should hold. FOCO, the app we make, helps with the moment you cannot start the thing you already chose.

That gap is task initiation, the step between knowing what to do and doing it (our guide to task initiation deficit explains it). In FOCO, AI splits the task into small steps, each with a time estimate, and a focus timer runs with an AI body-doubling companion on screen.

Sunsama is the stronger choice for desktop work across many tools, and it has web and computer apps while FOCO is phone only.
