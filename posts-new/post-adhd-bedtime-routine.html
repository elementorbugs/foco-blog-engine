Key Takeaways

Why is bedtime especially hard with ADHD?

The brain that struggled to start the day often struggles just as much to stop it.

There's no external deadline forcing a shutdown the way a morning alarm forces a start.

Time blindness plays a role here too.

"I'll stop in a minute" can quietly become another hour, especially during a hyperfocused task or a scrolling session.

Research published in JAMA (Volkow et al., 2009) points to differences in dopamine reward circuitry in ADHD brains.

A screen or a hyperfocus activity delivers an immediate reward that sleep simply can't compete with.

What belongs in the wind-down half?

The wind-down half calms your body and brain before you actually try to sleep.

It works the same way the morning foundations do: small, ordered, low-decision steps.

What should you set up for tomorrow?

The second half isn't about tonight at all.

It's a short gift to tomorrow-you, who will be tired and low on decision-making capacity.

How long should an ADHD bedtime routine take?

The full 14-step version usually runs 20 to 30 minutes.

Winding down alone takes about 10 to 15 minutes: alarm, devices away, dim lights, wash up.

Setting up tomorrow adds another 10 to 15 minutes, and it's the half most people skip when tired.

Skipping it occasionally is fine, but it's the half with the biggest payoff for tomorrow morning.

What if you're too wired to wind down?

Start with the smallest physical step, not the hardest mental one.

Dim the lights and put your phone in another room before trying to calm your thoughts.

A racing brain is harder to slow down while it's still getting bright light and new notifications.

If a brain dump doesn't work, try writing down just one sentence: what's the very next thing on your mind.

That's often enough to loosen the rest.

How do you actually stick with a bedtime routine?

Attach it to something you already do, like plugging in your phone or turning off the kitchen lights.

This is habit stacking, and it means you don't have to remember the routine exists.

Keep the printable somewhere you'll see it at night, like on your nightstand or bathroom mirror.

Let the routine flex.

Some nights you'll only manage the wind-down half, and that's still a routine that worked.

FAQ: ADHD Bedtime Routine

Is this checklist free?

Yes, both the interactive version on this page and the PDF are free.

Do I need to do all 14 steps every night?

No. Some nights, winding down alone is the whole win. Add the tomorrow-setup steps when you have the energy.

What if I fall asleep before finishing the routine?

That's a fine outcome. The point is calming down enough to sleep, not completing a checklist.

How is this different from general sleep hygiene advice?

Most sleep hygiene lists assume you'll remember and follow them without friction. This one is built around task initiation deficit, so it's a short, ordered checklist instead of a list of principles.

Can this replace FOCO?

They solve different moments. This checklist structures your evening; FOCO helps with the moment of actually starting a task, any time of day.
