Finch vs Habitica: how do they compare at a glance?

Both apps reward you for small real-life actions, but they are built for different jobs.

Finch is a self-care app with a pet. Habitica is a habit tracker and to-do list wrapped in a retro role-playing game.

What do the two apps actually look like?

We did not test Finch or Habitica hands-on for this comparison. The screens below come from each company's official App Store images, and the details come from their listings.

Finch opens on your bird and a short list of goals like "Get out of bed" and "Brush teeth". Each goal you tick gives your pet energy for its next adventure.

Habitica turns each task into part of a pixel-art game. You set a difficulty, earn gold and experience, and spend gold on gear or on rewards you choose yourself, like "Buy a coffee".

Is Finch or Habitica better for ADHD?

Neither is an ADHD app, and either can work. The deciding question is how you react to a missed day.

ADHD often makes consistency the hard part, because planning and follow-through lean on executive function, the brain skills behind starting, sequencing and remembering (our executive function guide explains more).

Finch is built around kindness. A low day costs you nothing, and even "Get out of bed" counts as a goal.

Habitica is built around stakes. Skip your Dailies and your avatar takes damage, which some people find motivating and others find crushing.

If shame after a slip tends to make you abandon apps, start with Finch. If you need a reason to care that a day was missed, Habitica gives you one.

What happens when you miss a day?

This is the biggest difference between the two apps.

In Habitica, unfinished Dailies cost your avatar health at the end of the day. If health hits zero, the community-run Habitica wiki says you lose a level, all your gold and one piece of equipment.

Habitica does have a pause button. Its Pause Damage setting, formerly called "Rest in the Inn", stops missed Dailies from costing health while you are sick or away (Habitica wiki).

In Finch, there is no damage to take. You set the next goal and your pet keeps going.

A hard week can feel very different in each app, so be honest about which one you would reopen afterwards.

Which one helps you build habits?

Habitica, if you mean a real system. It has Habits for things you do any number of times, Dailies on a daily, weekly or monthly schedule, and a regular To-do list.

Finch tracks goals and shows progress by area, such as movement, nutrition and self-kindness. It is closer to a gentle daily check-in than a habit system.

If you want streaks, schedules and checklists in one place, Habitica covers more ground. If you want two or three small goals a day and nothing more, Finch keeps it simple.

Which one supports your mood?

Finch, clearly. Its listing includes a mood tracker, journaling, breathing exercises, quizzes and morning and evening check-ins.

That makes Finch useful on days when the real task is getting through the day. If big feelings often derail your plans, our guide to emotional regulation with ADHD covers more ways to get through them.

Habitica does not try to do this. It cares about what you did, not how you felt.

Which is better with friends?

It depends on the kind of support you want. Finch has goal buddies: you share goals with a friend and send each other encouragement.

Habitica goes further with parties, shared challenges and guilds. In a party, you fight monsters together by finishing your tasks.

Habitica's party play also adds peer pressure, since your missed Dailies can hurt your party during a boss quest. For some people that accountability is the whole point.

Which is cheaper?

Both are free to start, and both free versions are usable long term.

Habitica's subscription is $4.99 a month or $47.99 a year on the US App Store, with 3- and 6-month options. It is optional, and you can also buy gems for in-game items.

Finch Plus is optional too. The US App Store lists several Finch Plus prices, from $5.99 to $69.99, so check the price your app shows.

Does either app help you start a task you're avoiding?

Not really. Both reward you after you act, which helps with habits but does not help much with one big task that feels impossible to begin.

That gap is task initiation, the step between knowing what to do and doing it. Our guide to task initiation deficit explains why rewards alone often don't close it.

FAQ

Is Finch or Habitica better?

Neither is better overall. Finch is better for gentle self-care with no penalties; Habitica is better as a full habit and to-do system that plays like a game.

Is Finch a good app for ADHD?

It can be. Finch is not made for ADHD, but its tiny goals and no-penalty design suit many ADHD adults, especially on low days.

Is Habitica good for ADHD?

For some people, yes. Its rewards and party accountability can help, but losing health for missed Dailies can feel discouraging during a hard week.

Is there a better app than Habitica?

It depends on what you need. If the penalties wear you down, Finch is gentler; our Habitica alternatives guide compares more options.

Is there a better app than Finch?

If you want a real task and habit system, Habitica covers more. If your trouble is starting one big task, a task-breakdown app like FOCO fits better.

Are Finch and Habitica free?

Yes, both are free to download and use. Finch Plus and the Habitica subscription are optional paid upgrades.
