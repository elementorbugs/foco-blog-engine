How does ScreenZen work?

You pick the apps that pull you in, and ScreenZen puts a pause and a question like "Is this important?" between you and the feed. The pause can grow every time you open the app, so the tenth open of the day takes longer than the first.

PAUSE

Wait before opening

A few seconds and a prompt, longer each time you open the app.

LIMIT

Cap opens and time

Like 3 opens of 10 minutes a day, then the app is blocked.

BLOCK

Strict schedules

Fully block apps by time of day, like 10 PM to 7 AM.

Version 4.0, released in September 2026, added a Minimal mode that keeps Instagram, YouTube, TikTok and Reddit usable without Reels, Shorts or endless feeds. It also added Practice, where you earn an open by breathing, journaling or learning something first.

To stop yourself switching it off in a weak moment, you can lock ScreenZen's own settings until a set time. You can also make an accountability partner the one who lets you back in.

Is ScreenZen really free?

Yes. Its website says "ScreenZen is completely free" and calls it the only donation-supported screen time app that blocks apps and websites.

The App Store's only in-app purchases are optional tips of $5, $10, $20 and $40. The one thing that costs money is the Halo device, which you don't need to use the app.

What is ScreenZen Halo?

Halo is a small $49 device you place in a room, usually the bedroom. When you walk in, ScreenZen automatically blocks the apps you chose, and to scroll you have to step out.

Its product page lists a no-bypass mode, US shipping only, $88.20 for two, and says Halo is HSA/FSA eligible. The September update added two more Halo modes: a Scroll zone, where apps only unlock in one spot, and a Habit mode that prompts a replacement activity when you arrive.

The difference from Brick, a $59 puck you tap to lock, is that Halo needs no tap. Being in the room is enough, which removes one more decision at bedtime.

Is ScreenZen safe?

By its App Store privacy label, yes: ScreenZen lists only "Data Not Linked to You," with nothing used to track you. That's cleaner than most blockers we've reviewed, including Opal and Brainrot.

On iPhone it blocks through Apple's Screen Time framework, which needs your permission to see and limit the apps you choose.

Does ScreenZen work on Mac and Windows?

Yes. ScreenZen's website offers downloads for Mac and Windows as well as iPhone and Android, and calls it "the only free screen time app for iOS, macOS, Windows and Android."

That matters if your laptop is the bigger distraction. Freedom also blocks across devices, but its full version is paid.

Why is ScreenZen not working?

The usual cause is a missing permission or a clash with another blocker. Check these before reinstalling:

CHECK 1

Screen Time access is still on for ScreenZen in your iPhone settings.

CHECK 2

Only one blocker is running; Screen Time apps can conflict with each other.

CHECK 3

The app is up to date; version 4.0 fixed blocking and unlock timers.

One Android user in ScreenZen's own testimonials says scheduled blocks could be skipped by changing the phone's clock. If that's your loophole, lock your settings or ask a partner to hold the unlock.

Is ScreenZen good for ADHD?

For impulsive scrolling, it's one of the best fits we've seen, and it's free. A reviewer on its website writes, "I have adhd and get really easily pulled into doomscrolling," and says the pause "annoyed me just enough" to stop opening apps on autopilot.

The pause works in the gap between urge and action, which is where ADHD makes things hardest. Pausing an urge is part of executive function, the brain skills for self-control and planning (NIMH).

Research on pause apps backs the idea. In a 2023 PNAS study of a similar app, one sec, people tried to open target apps 37% less often; ScreenZen itself hasn't been studied.

Where does ScreenZen fall short?

The pause is gentle by design, so on a bad day you can wait it out. Its strict modes and locked settings close most of that gap.

FAQ

Is ScreenZen free?

Yes. ScreenZen is completely free and supported by optional tips; only the Halo device costs money.

Is ScreenZen safe?

Its App Store privacy label lists only data not linked to you and nothing used to track you. On iPhone it blocks apps through Apple's Screen Time framework with your permission.

What is ScreenZen Halo?

Halo is a $49 device that automatically blocks your chosen apps while you're in the room it's in, usually the bedroom. It ships only within the US.

Does ScreenZen work on Mac?

Yes. ScreenZen offers downloads for Mac and Windows as well as iPhone and Android.

Is ScreenZen better than Opal?

ScreenZen is better if you want it free and private; Opal is better if you want polished schedules and a score and will pay for Pro. Both run on Apple's Screen Time framework on iPhone.

Why is ScreenZen not blocking my apps?

Check that Screen Time access is on, that no other blocker is running and that the app is updated. Conflicts between Screen Time apps are a common cause.

What is the difference between ScreenZen and FOCO?

ScreenZen blocks distracting apps. FOCO is an ADHD planner that breaks a stuck task into small steps, puts it in your calendar and stays with you while you focus, so the two work well together.
