How does the Opal app work?

You pick the apps and websites that pull you in, then set a rule for when Opal blocks them. When you try to open one, you get a block screen instead of the feed.

STEP 1

Pick what to block

Choose apps, games and sites, or flip it and allow only a few.

STEP 2

Set a rule

A schedule (like 9 to 5), a daily time limit, or a limit on how often you open an app.

STEP 3

Choose how strict

Easy lets you take a break; Hard Mode and Deep Focus (Pro) won't let you cancel.

On iPhone, Opal runs on Apple's Screen Time framework, which is why it can block apps system-wide. Its listing says it can also start sessions from iPhone Focus modes and Shortcuts.

Around the blocking sit the game parts: a daily Opal Score, gems you unlock as milestones, and leaderboards to compete with friends. The latest update, version 4.20 on October 19, 2026, added a gem for keeping apps blocked through the workday.

Is Opal free, and what does Opal Pro cost?

What is the difference between Opal and iPhone Screen Time?

Screen Time is free and built in, but its limits come with an "Ignore Limit" button, so the block is one tap away. Opal adds stricter modes, scheduled routines, a score and stats on top of the same Apple framework.

Is Opal good for ADHD?

It can be, especially for scrolling that eats the hours you meant for something else. Opal's own listing says it "supports focus challenges, including ADHD," and it comes up in r/ADHD threads about doomscrolling, though it isn't designed specifically for ADHD.

Three of its design choices line up with how ADHD tends to work:

1. It decides before the urge does

A schedule set in a calm moment does the stopping later. That helps with executive function, the brain skills that pause an urge and weigh it against a goal (NIMH).

2. Rewards land right away

The score and gems give an instant payoff, the kind many people with ADHD respond to better than distant goals (more in ADHD and dopamine).

3. It shows where time went

Insights like "you put your phone down around 1-2AM" make time blindness visible, in numbers instead of guilt.

One caution: a score can turn into one more thing to feel bad about on a hard day. Opal calls itself "guilt-free," so if the numbers start to sting, hide the widget and keep the rules.

Where does Opal fall short?

The price is the most common complaint, since $99.99 a year is a lot for blocking that iPhone partly does for free. Strictness also depends on paying: the free plan has no mode you can't cancel.

FAQ

Is the Opal app free?

Yes, Opal has a free plan that never expires, with one rule, app blocking, basic timers and today's score. Unlimited rules and Hard Mode need Opal Pro.

How much does Opal Pro cost?

Opal Pro is $99.99 a year (about $8.29 a month), $19.99 a month or $399 for lifetime access, per its pricing page on October 28, 2026. Students can apply for up to 50% off.

Is the Opal app safe?

Opal blocks apps through Apple's Screen Time framework and has a 4.7 rating from over 90,000 App Store reviews. Its privacy label lists identifiers used to track you, so read it if that matters to you.

Does Opal work on Android?

Yes. Opal is on Google Play with 500K+ downloads and a 4.6 rating, and its pricing page lists iPhone, Android and Mac.

Can you get around Opal?

On easier settings, yes, you can take a break or end a session. Hard Mode and Deep Focus in Pro are built so you can't cancel, though users have reported occasional bugs.

Is Opal good for ADHD?

Many ADHD users find scheduled blocks and instant rewards helpful for doomscrolling. It doesn't treat ADHD or help you start tasks, so it works best alongside other strategies and support from a clinician.

What is the difference between Opal and FOCO?

Opal blocks distracting apps on a schedule. FOCO is an ADHD planner: it breaks a stuck task into timed steps, puts it in your calendar and keeps a companion with you while you focus, so the two work well together.
