What is the Forest app?

Forest is a gamified focus timer. You set a session, a seed starts growing, and if you leave the app before the timer ends, the tree dies.

Finished sessions add trees to your forest, unlock new species and earn coins. Forest says its users have funded more than 2 million real trees through its partnership with Trees for the Future.

Recent versions add an Allow List that blocks distracting apps during a session, a scheduled blocker called Time Guard, breathing exercises, shared sessions with friends, and focus statistics by tag (Forest on the App Store).

Is the Forest app good for ADHD?

Forest can be good for ADHD at one specific point: keeping you on a task you have already started. Its strength is turning "do not touch your phone" into a visible, slightly emotional stake.

That matters because ADHD is linked to differences in attention and self-regulation, not to a lack of caring (NIMH). An outside cue that is concrete and immediate, like a tree that dies, can stand in for motivation that does not show up on its own.

ADHD is often described as a problem of doing what you know rather than knowing what to do, an idea most closely tied to researcher Russell Barkley. Making rewards visible in the moment is a common strategy built on that idea, and Forest does a small version of it inside a session.

Where Forest helps less: the tree is an external reward, and for some people novelty fades after a few weeks. A dead tree can also feel like a small failure, which is not useful if you already carry a lot of shame about unfinished work.

Does Forest help with task paralysis?

Not really, because Forest starts working after you have already begun. Task paralysis happens before that, in the gap between knowing what to do and being able to start it.

If you open Forest, set 25 minutes, and then stare at the task, the timer runs but nothing gets easier. The freeze is usually about the first step feeling too big or too vague, and a timer does not change the size of the step.

This is the part FOCO is built for. You type or say the task, and FOCO breaks it into small steps with a time on each, so the first one is something like "open the blank slides template, 3 minutes."

Is the Forest app free?

Yes. Forest is free to download on iPhone, iPad and Android, and the core tree timer works without paying.

The Android version shows ads, and both stores offer an optional Forest Plus subscription with extra coins, Plus-only trees and early seasonal trees, with a free trial. Forest does not publish one Plus price for every country, so check the price in your store before you subscribe.

Older articles say Forest costs $3.99 on iPhone. That was true of earlier versions, but the current US listing shows it as free.

Can you use Forest and FOCO together?

Yes, and for some ADHD brains the pair covers both ends of the problem. Use FOCO to get the first small step and start it, then run Forest for the longer stretch once you are moving.

Who should try Forest?

Try Forest if:

  • You start tasks fine but lose them to your phone.
  • Small visual rewards and streaks keep you going.
  • You want a free tool and do not mind ads on Android.

Skip Forest if:

  • Your freeze happens before you start, not during.
  • A dead tree would make you feel worse, not more motivated.

Pick FOCO if:

  • You know what the task is and still cannot begin it.

FAQ

Is the Forest app good for ADHD?

It can help you stay off your phone once a task is underway. It does not help you start a task you are frozen on.

Is the Forest app free?

Yes. Forest is free on iPhone, iPad and Android, with ads on Android and an optional Forest Plus subscription.

Does Forest really plant trees?

Forest says its community has planted more than 2 million real trees through its partnership with Trees for the Future.

What is a good Forest alternative for ADHD?

Flora is the closest free alternative with the same grow-a-plant timer. If the problem is starting rather than staying, FOCO breaks the task into a tiny first step instead.

Does Forest block apps?

Yes. On iOS 16 or later you can set an Allow List for a session and schedule blocks with Time Guard.

Can I use Forest for studying with ADHD?

Yes, many students use it for study sessions. Keep sessions short at first, so a missed session does not turn into a dead forest.
