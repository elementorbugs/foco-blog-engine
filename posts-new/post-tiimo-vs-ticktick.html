What do the two apps actually look like?

We did not test Tiimo or TickTick hands-on for this comparison. The screenshots below are each company's official App Store images, and the feature details come from their listings, pricing pages and release notes.

Tiimo lays the day out as a visual schedule, grouped into morning, day and evening, with soft icons instead of a wall of text.

TickTick starts from lists and builds outward: calendar views, a priority matrix, reminders, habits and widgets, all in one app.

Which one is built for ADHD?

Tiimo. It is designed and marketed for neurodivergent people first, and its listing says it was co-designed with ADHD and autism experts.

In practice that means visual routines with icons, a calm look with few choices per screen, and a focus on what comes next. TickTick is a general productivity app that many ADHD users rely on, with far more on every screen.

The label matters less than it sounds: what counts is whether you still open the app on day 10.

Both apps try to take load off executive function, the brain skills behind planning and holding steps in mind (our executive function guide explains more). They just do it differently.

For some people, Tiimo's calm, focused screens are easier to come back to. For others, TickTick's single place for everything is what makes it stick.

Which helps more with time blindness?

Tiimo, for most people. Time blindness, trouble sensing how long things take or how much time has passed, is the problem its visual schedule is built to make visible (our time blindness guide goes deeper).

Tiimo shows what you are doing now and what comes next, so the day reads like a path instead of a pile. TickTick opens on your lists, and its calendar shows tasks as blocks in time.

On TickTick's free plan the calendar is basic; unlimited calendar views, calendar subscriptions and a Timeline view are listed on its Premium page (TickTick Premium).

Which is better for staying focused once you start?

It is close. Both have a built-in focus timer, so neither forces you into a second app.

TickTick's Pomo timer comes with white noise sounds and can sync focus time to Apple Health. Tiimo's visual countdown shows time draining away, and its widgets keep the timer on your home or lock screen.

If a countdown you can see helps you more than a 25-minute block, lean Tiimo. If you already like the Pomodoro rhythm (here is how it works with ADHD), TickTick does it well.

Can either app break a big task into steps?

Yes, both can now. Tiimo's AI Co-planner turns a brain dump into timed tasks and can split a larger task into steps, on iOS and the web (Tiimo AI planning).

TickTick added an AI Assistant in its October 2026 update that can create tasks and break down projects from a single instruction, according to its App Store release notes. Its pricing page lists AI features but does not say clearly which plan includes them, so check in the app before you count on it.

Which is cheaper?

TickTick, for most people. Its free plan is permanent and covers a lot, and Premium is US$49.99 a year on its pricing page.

Tiimo has a free version with a basic planner and limited AI chats, and Pro unlocks the rest, including the web app. On the US App Store its Pro purchases run from $7 to $54 depending on the plan, prices vary by country, and the 7-day free trial applies to the yearly plan only (Tiimo pricing).

Does either app help you start a task you're avoiding?

Partly. Both now help you plan and break work down, which takes some weight off the first step.

Both also start from organizing: your day in Tiimo, your lists in TickTick. If the plan is already fine and you still freeze, that is task initiation, the gap between knowing what to do and doing it.

What are the downsides of each app?

Tiimo: the full app, including the web version, needs Pro, and the AI Co-planner works on iOS and the web. If you live on Windows or Linux, it will not follow you to your desk.

TickTick: its range can turn into a hobby. Tags, smart lists and matrix quadrants can feel like progress while the actual task waits, and its richer calendar views sit on Premium.

What does a morning look like in each app?

In Tiimo, you open the app and see the morning laid out as a few colored blocks with icons: shower, breakfast, write report. The current block is the one in focus, and a countdown shows how long is left before the next one.

In TickTick, you open your Today list: a report due at 3 PM, a gym session at 7, groceries, two habits to check off. You can drag tasks into the calendar, start a Pomo timer on the report, and tick habits as you go.

Same morning, two different feelings. Tiimo hands you the next thing; TickTick hands you the whole picture and trusts you to pick.

FAQ

Is TickTick good for ADHD?

It can be, especially if you like having everything in one place. Its range of features can also invite endless organizing, so keep the setup simple.

Is Tiimo better than TickTick?

Not overall. Tiimo is better for a visual, calm day; TickTick is better as an all-in-one task and habit system.

Is TickTick free?

Yes, TickTick has a permanent free plan with 9 lists, 99 tasks per list and 5 habits. Premium is US$49.99 a year.

Is Tiimo free?

Tiimo has a free version with a basic planner and limited AI chats. The full app, including the web version, needs Pro, with a 7-day trial on the yearly plan.

Does TickTick have a visual timeline like Tiimo?

TickTick has calendar and Timeline views that show tasks as blocks over time, but not Tiimo's calm, icon-based day. If a visual day is the main reason you want an app, Tiimo is the closer fit.

Can I use Tiimo and TickTick together?

You can, but keeping two planners in sync is extra work, which is often the first thing to slip with ADHD. If you do, keep one as the source of truth for tasks.
