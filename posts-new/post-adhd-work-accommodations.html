ADHD Accommodations at Work: What to Ask For and How

In the US, employers with 15 or more employees must provide reasonable accommodations for a disability, and ADHD can qualify when it substantially limits a major life activity. Common ones are a quiet workspace, written instructions, to-do lists, extra time, flexible hours and timers, and you can ask in plain words.

Task apps count as assistive technology too, including FOCO, the app we make.

Does ADHD count as a disability at work?

It can. The Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) has no list of qualifying conditions, but it covers any "physical or mental impairment that substantially limits one or more" major life activities, according to the Job Accommodation Network (JAN).

So the question is not whether you have an ADHD diagnosis. It is whether your symptoms substantially limit things like concentrating, thinking or working.

The rule applies to employers with 15 or more employees, according to the US Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC). State laws can differ, so check your state's rules if you work somewhere smaller.

You are far from alone. An estimated 15.5 million US adults, or 6.0%, had a current ADHD diagnosis in 2023, according to a CDC report.

What accommodations can you ask for with ADHD?

The best accommodation matches the specific thing that gets in your way. JAN, a free service funded by the US Department of Labor, lists these ideas for ADHD and for executive function difficulties.

If this gets in your wayAccommodations to ask for
Focus in a noisy officeA quiet workspace, noise cancellation or white noise, uninterrupted work time, or working from home
Time and deadlinesExtra time on projects, timers and alarms, calendars, and meetings to set clear expectations
Starting big tasksTo-do lists, help with prioritizing, and a mentor or regular check-ins
Remembering instructionsWritten instructions, checklists, reminders, and recorded directions
Staying organizedPlanners, calendars and color-coded files
RestlessnessStructured breaks, a private workspace, or a different style of supervision
Too many side tasksFewer marginal duties, so you can focus on the essential parts of your job

JAN also lists assistive technology such as "timers, apps, calendars," and an ADHD coach. Not everyone with ADHD needs accommodations, JAN notes, so ask only for what would help you.

How do you ask for ADHD accommodations?

You do not need special words. A person "need not mention the ADA," and requests "do not need to be in writing," according to the EEOC.

  1. Name the problem: Write down the one or two tasks where ADHD gets in your way most, such as long verbal briefings or an open-plan office.
  2. Pick specific requests: "A written recap after meetings" is easier to say yes to than "more support."
  3. Ask HR or your manager: A simple line works, such as "I have a medical condition, and I'd like to talk about some changes that would help me do my job."
  4. Keep a record: Even though writing is not required, a short email after the conversation gives you a dated record.
  5. Work it out together: The EEOC calls this the interactive process, where you and your employer find what works, and it "should proceed as quickly as possible."

Frame each request around getting the work done. "Written instructions help me deliver accurate work" focuses on results, not on what you struggle with.

What documentation can your employer ask for?

Only some. If your need is not obvious, your employer may ask for "reasonable documentation about his/her disability and functional limitations," according to the EEOC.

That usually means a note from your doctor or psychologist confirming the condition and how it affects your work. If you were diagnosed long ago, ask your current provider what they can write.

Do your coworkers have to know?

No. The ADA "specifically prohibits the disclosure of medical information except in certain limited situations," according to the EEOC, and those exceptions do not include telling coworkers.

Whether you tell your team is your choice. Some people share it to explain why they wear headphones or ask for written notes, and others keep it between themselves, HR and their manager.

What if your employer says no?

An employer can refuse an accommodation that would cause "undue hardship, meaning significant difficulty or expense," according to the EEOC. It can also choose a different option, as long as that option works.

If one request is turned down, suggest another that solves the same problem. A quiet room may be refused while headphones or a few remote days are approved.

If you believe your rights were violated, the EEOC is the federal agency that handles ADA complaints about employers. This article is general information, not legal advice, so talk to an employment lawyer about your situation.

What can you try before a formal request?

Many changes need no paperwork. You can ask your manager for written next steps after meetings, block focus time in your calendar, or wear headphones at your desk.

FAQ

Is ADHD covered by the ADA?

It can be. The ADA covers conditions that substantially limit a major life activity, and ADHD can meet that test, but it is decided case by case rather than by diagnosis alone.

Do I have to say I have ADHD to get accommodations?

You do not need to mention the ADA or use legal words. If your need is not obvious, your employer may ask for documentation about your condition and how it limits you.

Can I be fired for asking for ADHD accommodations?

The ADA prohibits retaliation for asking for a reasonable accommodation. If you think it happened to you, contact the EEOC or an employment lawyer.

Can working from home be an ADHD accommodation?

Yes. JAN lists working from home as an option, especially when no accommodation in the office environment works.

Can I ask for accommodations at a small company?

The federal ADA rule applies to employers with 15 or more employees. State laws may cover smaller employers, and any employer can agree to an informal change.

What are accommodations called outside the US?

Laws differ by country. In the UK, for example, they are called reasonable adjustments, so look up your own country's rules.

Key Takeaways

  • US employers with 15 or more employees must provide reasonable accommodations unless it causes undue hardship, and ADHD can qualify.
  • Match the accommodation to the problem: a quiet space for focus, written instructions for memory, to-do lists and check-ins for starting tasks.
  • You can ask in plain words, and your employer cannot share your medical information with coworkers.
  • Timers and task apps count as assistive technology, and FOCO splits big tasks into small timed steps.
