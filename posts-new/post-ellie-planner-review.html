Short answer: Ellie Planner is a simple, well-made daily planner built around one method: brain dump everything, pick what fits today, then drag it into time blocks next to your calendar. It is not an ADHD app by design, but many ADHD users like it, and its free plan covers unlimited tasks.

What does Ellie look like in use?

The screenshots below are Ellie's official App Store screenshots, not our own. We did not test Ellie hands-on, so we describe what the listing shows and what Ellie says the app does.

Everything starts in the Brain Dump, a single list where tasks go in with no date and no decision attached. You can add tasks by typing, by voice through Siri, by email, or from Slack.

Then you pick only what today can hold and drag those tasks into open gaps between your calendar events. Each task carries a time estimate, so the day fills up visibly.

Unfinished tasks roll over to tomorrow instead of piling up as overdue. Extras like a Pomodoro timer, focus mode, analytics and a daily shutdown stay hidden until you switch them on in Settings, so the default screen stays simple (Ellie's site).

Is Ellie Planner built for ADHD?

No, not by design. Ellie's own copy describes a planner for people who feel overloaded and does not mention ADHD; ADHD, AuDHD and time blindness come up only in user testimonials on its homepage.

That is not a flaw on its own. Apps built specifically around executive function, the brain's self-management system for planning, starting and switching tasks, make narrower choices; Ellie is a general planner that happens to match how many ADHD brains capture work.

For contrast, Inflow is built on ADHD-specific CBT lessons (see our Inflow review), and Tiimo is designed for neurodivergent users from the ground up.

What does Ellie get right for ADHD brains?

It separates capturing from deciding. Getting a thought out of your head before it vanishes is a different job from deciding when to do it, and Ellie gives each job its own place.

That is the core of a good ADHD brain dump, and it takes load off working memory, the mental notepad that drops things so easily with ADHD (more in our guide to working memory and ADHD).

Time blocking also makes time visible. When a 45-minute task has to fit between a 3:30 call and the gym, you see right away whether the day is realistic, which is the kind of outside cue that helps with time blindness (see our time blindness guide).

Rolling unfinished tasks forward is a small, kind choice. A red wall of overdue items is often what makes people abandon a planner, a pattern we cover in why you keep abandoning ADHD planner apps.

Where does Ellie fall short?

It asks for a daily planning habit. The method works when you sit down each morning, choose and drag, and that ritual is exactly what slips on a scattered day.

The free plan is narrower than it first looks. Unlimited tasks and Brain Dump are free, but Ellie's pricing page lists Timebox mode, Google and Apple Calendar, recurring tasks, labels, subtasks, reminders and the AI Assistant under Pro (Ellie pricing).

A planned block is also not the same as starting. Knowing that "write report" sits at 2 PM does not get you over the first minute of it; that gap is task initiation, explained in our guide to task initiation deficit.

Finally, there is no Android app. Ellie's FAQ says it runs on Mac, Windows, the web, iPhone and iPad.

How much does Ellie Planner cost?

Ellie is free to start, and Pro is $9.99 a month. On the US App Store the yearly plan is $99.99, and a one-time Lifetime purchase is $299.99.

Ellie's pricing page offers a Pro free trial with no credit card required, and says students and teachers can get 50% off by email. Prices outside the US may differ, according to the App Store listing.

Does Ellie connect to my other apps?

Yes, it connects to more than most planners. Its site lists Google Calendar, Outlook and Apple Calendar, two-way sync with Notion databases, a Todoist import with ongoing sync, Slack and Zapier.

It also says you can plan with ChatGPT or connect Claude through MCP. Calendar connections are listed as a Pro feature, so check which integrations you need before you rely on the free plan.

Who should try Ellie Planner?

Try Ellie if:

  • You like time blocking and want tasks next to your real calendar.
  • You plan on a Mac or Windows computer as well as your iPhone.

Look elsewhere if:

FAQ

Is Ellie Planner good for ADHD?

It can be, especially if brain dumping and time blocking already work for you. It is a general planner, not an ADHD app, and it relies on a daily planning habit.

Is Ellie Planner free?

Yes, the free plan includes unlimited tasks and Brain Dump on iPhone, iPad and web. Timebox mode, calendar connections, recurring tasks and the AI Assistant are Pro.

How much is Ellie Pro?

Ellie Pro is $9.99 a month or $99.99 a year on the US App Store. A one-time Lifetime purchase is $299.99.

Is Ellie Planner on Android?

No. Ellie runs on iPhone, iPad, Mac, Windows and the web, and its FAQ says there is no Android app right now.

Who makes Ellie Planner?

Ellie is built by developer Chris Raroque and published on the App Store by AloaLabs, LLC.

Does Ellie Planner sync with Google Calendar?

Yes. Ellie connects to Google Calendar, Outlook and Apple Calendar, and its pricing page lists calendar connections as a Pro feature.
