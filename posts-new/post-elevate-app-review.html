Elevate App Review: Is Brain Training Worth It for ADHD?

Elevate is a brain training app with 40+ short games for reading, writing, speaking, memory and math, on iPhone, iPad and Android. The free version gives you one 3-game workout a day, and Premium costs US$39.99 a year with a 7-day free trial, or US$9.99 a month.

It is a polished, award-winning app, but research finds brain games mostly make you better at the games, not at everyday focus.

  • Try Elevate if you enjoy word and math puzzles and want a few minutes of daily practice that feels like a game.
  • Skip it if you are hoping it will treat ADHD symptoms or fix focus at work.
  • Pick FOCO, the app we make, if your goal is starting real tasks, and you want them split into small timed steps.

Does brain training work?

Mostly for the games themselves. A 2016 review of 132 studies in Psychological Science in the Public Interest found people improve on the tasks they practice, but "there is little evidence to date of real-world benefits from brain training," as the University of Illinois summary puts it.

In other words, getting faster at Elevate's tipping game likely makes you faster at that game. It is much less clear that it makes you sharper at work or at home.

Elevate's own App Store listing says "90%+ report improved vocabulary, math skills, and overall mental sharpness" with frequent use. That is a self-reported claim from the company, not an independent study.

Is Elevate good for ADHD?

Not as a treatment. A 2015 meta-analysis by Cortese and colleagues of 16 trials in children with ADHD found that cognitive training improved working memory tests but "had limited effects on ADHD symptoms" when raters were probably blinded.

Elevate was not one of the programs studied, and it does not claim to treat ADHD. Its games train reading, writing and math skills, not the executive function skills behind starting and finishing tasks.

That does not make it useless. Short, adaptive games can be a pleasant break, and some adults with ADHD like having one small daily habit they can finish.

For questions about ADHD itself, start with the National Institute of Mental Health or CHADD.

Where might Elevate fall short?

Limited proof of real-world gains: As above, brain games mostly improve brain games.

The free version is small: One 3-game workout a day, so most of the 40+ games sit behind Premium.

Monthly is expensive: US$9.99 a month adds up to US$119.88 over a year, three times the yearly plan, and the monthly plan has no trial.

Streaks and leagues: They keep some people coming back, and make others feel behind after a missed day.

Tracking: Its App Store privacy label says usage data may be used to track you across other companies' apps and websites.

How is Elevate different from Lumosity and Peak?

All three are brain training apps from the mid-2010s, with short adaptive games and daily workouts. Elevate leans toward everyday language and math skills, such as writing concisely or splitting a bill.

On the US App Store, Elevate has 4.8 stars from about 547,000 ratings, Lumosity 4.7 from about 131,000, and Peak 4.7 from about 146,000. The research caveats above apply to all of them.

Is Elevate worth it?

It is worth it if you enjoy the games and want word and math practice. If you want better focus or fewer ADHD symptoms, research on brain training gives little reason to expect that.

You will likely get better at its games. A major 2016 review found little evidence that brain training games improve everyday thinking.
