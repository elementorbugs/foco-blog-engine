What does Llama Life look like in use?

The screenshots below are Llama Life's official App Store screenshots, not our own. We did not test Llama Life hands-on, so we describe what the listing shows and what Llama Life says the app does.

You write your list, and every task gets a time: wake up and get dressed 5 minutes, drink water 1 minute, make breakfast 10 minutes. The top bar adds them up into a total list time and an estimated end time.

Press play and the current task fills the screen as a countdown, with -5 and +5 buttons when you need more or less time. A visual dial shows the time left as a shrinking wedge of color.

Finishing a task sets off confetti, and you can give each task an emoji or color. Recent updates added soundscapes that keep playing as you switch tabs and stronger alarms when the app is closed.

Is Llama Life built for ADHD?

Mostly, yes. Its App Store name starts with "ADHD Organizer," and many of the user quotes on its listing come from people with ADHD, though the description itself focuses on focus and timeboxing in general.

The idea behind it is timeboxing: giving a task a fixed amount of time and full attention until the timer ends. Llama Life applies that to every item on a list; one user quoted on its listing calls it "pomodoro on steroids."

What does Llama Life get right for ADHD brains?

It makes time visible. A countdown for each task, plus a running end time for the whole list, gives an outside clock to people who struggle with time blindness, the trouble sensing how long things take (see our time blindness guide).

It keeps attention on one thing. Only the current task is big on screen, which cuts the pull of the other 14 items on the list.

It rewards finishing. Confetti sounds trivial, but quick, frequent rewards suit how many ADHD brains respond to motivation, which we cover in ADHD and dopamine.

It is also more flexible than a fixed 25-minute block. If classic sessions never fit your tasks, our guide to the Pomodoro method with ADHD explains why variable timers can work better.

Where does Llama Life fall short?

It assumes you already know the steps. A countdown helps once a task is clear and sized, but "sort out taxes" with a 60-minute timer can still freeze you before minute one.

That first-minute freeze is task initiation trouble, explained in our guide to task initiation deficit. Its founder's 2024 launch post described AI that breaks big tasks down, but its current store listings don't describe that feature, so check it in the trial.

There is no free plan, only a 7-day trial. And its Google Play rating, 3.8, is noticeably lower than its 4.5 on the US App Store, though both are based on fairly few ratings.

How much does Llama Life cost?

Llama Life is free for 7 days, then a subscription. The US App Store lists $6 a month and $39 a year, plus a $4 monthly price point and a $29 six-month plan.

Its founder's launch post says one subscription covers every platform, so you can use the web app and your phone without paying twice. Check the paywall in the app for your current offer.

Is Llama Life good for ADHD?

It can be, especially if you lose track of time inside tasks. It works best when you already know your steps and need help moving through them.

No. Llama Life has a 7-day free trial, then a monthly, six-month or annual subscription.

The US App Store lists $6 a month and $39 a year, plus other price points. Check the in-app paywall for your current offer.

Is Llama Life on Android?

Yes. Llama Life is on the web, iPhone and Android, and one subscription covers all of them.

Who makes Llama Life?

Llama Life is made by Llama Life Pty Ltd and its founder, Marie, who signs the app's release notes.

What is the difference between Llama Life and FOCO?

Llama Life puts a countdown on each task in a list you write. FOCO uses AI to split one stuck task into timed steps and keeps a focus companion on screen while you do them.
