Why might women with ADHD need a different kind of app?

Because ADHD often looks different in women, and the load they carry tends to be heavier. CHADD notes that girls more often show the predominantly inattentive presentation of ADHD, and that a woman may be diagnosed with depression or anxiety before her underlying ADHD is identified (CHADD).

The same page describes women who "feel even less able to keep up with the daily tasks of meals, laundry, and life or family management," and who feel "overwhelmed and exhausted." That is where many app lists miss: they assume more structure is the answer, when what often helps is making it easier to begin.

So we sorted this list by problem, not by feature count. Each pick solves one part of the day well.

Which app fits the way your day breaks down?

Start with the part that costs you the most each week. Pick one app for that, use it for two weeks, and only then add another.

  • "I know what to do, I just can't start." That is a task initiation problem, so pick FOCO, or Goblin Tools if you only need the steps.
  • "The day disappears and I'm always late." That is time blindness, so pick Tiimo.
  • "The house never stops." Pick Sweepy for chores, and see our family calendar apps for ADHD moms for the schedule side.
  • "I can't do anything alone." Pick Dubbii, or try our free body doubling room.
  • "I'm running on empty." Pick Finch for small wins, or Inflow if you want to learn coping skills.

The 7 best ADHD apps for women

1. FOCO, best ADHD app for starting tasks

FOCO is the ADHD planner we make, built for the moment you stare at a task and can't move. You say it, type it or snap a photo of a written list, and one optional tap breaks the task into small steps, each with a time estimate.

The first step opens a focus timer with calm music and an AI companion on screen, so you're not starting alone. Everything lands in a built-in calendar, and missed tasks move on to the next day instead of piling up as a red overdue wall.

Good to know: FOCO doesn't sync with Google or Outlook calendars yet, and there's no free version. It's $59 a year with a 7-day free trial, or $9 a month, on iPhone and Android.

2. Tiimo, best visual planner for time blindness

Tiimo turns your day into a calm, colorful timeline, which helps when time feels invisible. It was built with ADHD and autism in mind and was Apple's iPhone App of the Year for 2025.

Good to know: the free version is a basic planner with limited AI chats, and Pro unlocks full features and the web app, according to its App Store listing. Our Tiimo app review found that calendar sync is also a Pro feature, and it lists the prices.

3. Sweepy, best app for the household load

Sweepy splits the home into rooms and shows which one needs attention, so "clean the house" stops being one huge task you freeze in front of. You can invite the rest of the household, so the list isn't only in your head.

Good to know: the room tracker is free, while the daily schedule and family leaderboard need a subscription. Our Sweepy app review has the details, and our free ADHD cleaning planner does the same on paper.

4. Finch, best for low-energy self-care days

Finch gives a small pet bird energy every time you finish a tiny goal, like getting out of bed or drinking water. On days when the bar is low, it counts the small things instead of judging what you didn't do.

Good to know: Finch is free to download, with an optional Finch Plus subscription sold as an in-app purchase on the App Store.

It is a self-care app, not a planner, so pair it with something else for work. See our Finch app review.

5. Dubbii, best for body doubling with real people

Body doubling means doing a task with someone else present, which many people with ADHD find makes starting easier. Dubbii offers live sessions with hosts and follow-along videos for chores like laundry or making the bed.

Good to know: it's free to download, but full access needs a subscription with a 3-day free trial. See our Dubbii review, or read how body doubling works for ADHD.

6. Inflow, best for learning ADHD skills after a diagnosis

Inflow is a program of short lessons based on CBT, the type of talk therapy used to change unhelpful habits and thoughts. It suits you if you were diagnosed late and want to understand your ADHD before you build a system around it.

Good to know: there's no free version, only a free trial, and it costs more than the other apps here. Our Inflow review lists the plans.

7. Goblin Tools, best free way to break a task into steps

Goblin Tools' Magic ToDo takes one task and splits it into smaller steps, with a "spiciness" slider for how small you need them. It's free on the web and $0.99 as an iPhone app.

Good to know: it's mainly for getting the steps, with no calendar and no focus companion to help you start the first step. Our Goblin Tools alternatives guide covers what to use next.

Is there an ADHD app that adjusts to your menstrual cycle?

Not among the apps on this list. None of their store listings mentions adjusting your plan to your cycle.

If your symptoms seem to shift during the month, the most useful step is to track them for a few cycles and bring that record to your doctor. In the meantime, an app that lets you plan less on harder days, like Finch, or simply a shorter task list, fits better than one that expects the same output every day.

Which free ADHD apps are good for women?

Finch, Tiimo and Sweepy all have real free versions, and Goblin Tools is free on the web. You can cover a lot of ground without paying.

FOCO and Inflow don't have free versions, but both have a free trial. To clear your head for free first, try an ADHD brain dump.

What's the best ADHD app for a working mom?

Most working moms with ADHD need two things: help starting their own work in short windows, and a way to carry the family schedule. No single app does both well.

FOCO fits the first, because it turns a work task into steps you can start in a 20-minute gap. For the second, a shared family calendar works better, and our guide to the ADHD mom periscope explains how to keep the whole week in view.

FAQ

What is the best ADHD app for women?

It depends on your main struggle. FOCO is best for starting tasks, Tiimo for time blindness, Sweepy for household chores, Finch for self-care and Inflow for learning ADHD skills.

Is there a free ADHD app for women?

Yes. Finch, Tiimo and Sweepy have free versions, and Goblin Tools is free on the web.

Are there ADHD apps made only for women?

Few apps are made only for women, and none on this list is. What matters more is whether the app fits your problem, such as starting tasks or managing the household.

Should I use more than one ADHD app?

Start with one for at least two weeks. Starting several apps at once usually gives you more to manage, which is the opposite of what you need.

What ADHD app helps with the mental load?

No app removes the mental load, but you can share part of it. Sweepy lets the household share chores, and a brain dump gets the rest out of your head.

Can an app replace ADHD treatment?

No. Apps can help with daily tasks, but diagnosis and treatment belong with a clinician.
