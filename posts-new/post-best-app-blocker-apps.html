What are the best app blocker apps for ADHD?

An app blocker is an app that stops you from opening the apps or websites you choose, either completely or after a pause. Here are the eight worth trying, starting with the best free one.

1. ScreenZen: the best free app blocker

ScreenZen puts a short wait between you and a distracting app, and the wait can grow each time you open it. It also asks "Is this important?", limits how often you open an app, and can fully block apps by time of day or after a set number of opens.

It is free and funded by optional tips from $5 to $40, with a 4.9 rating from over 52,000 reviews. A lock on its own settings stops you from switching it off in a weak moment.

Good to know: the delay approach is gentler than a hard block, so turn on its strict block for your worst hours. More in our ScreenZen review.

2. Opal: best for schedules and focus stats

Opal blocks apps and websites on a schedule, like 9 to 5 or after 11pm, and rewards you with a focus score and gems. Its paid Hard Mode won't let you cancel a session once it starts.

The free plan gives you one rule forever, and Opal Pro costs $99.99 a year, $19.99 a month or $399 once. More in our Opal app review.

Good to know: price is the top complaint, and some users report bugs after updates.

3. Brick: the best physical app blocker

Brick is a small magnetic puck plus a free app. You tap your phone on it to lock the apps you chose, and the only normal way to unlock is to tap it again, so leaving it in another room adds real distance.

It costs $59 once with no subscription. Our Brick app review covers the emergency unbricks and what happens if you lose it.

Good to know: it only works if the Brick stays somewhere inconvenient. For a cheaper card version, see our Bloom app blocker review.

4. one sec: best for a gentle pause

one sec doesn't block by default; it delays. When you open Instagram, you get a short breathing pause and a question about why you're opening it, with a button to close the app instead.

A 2023 study in PNAS followed 280 people for six weeks: they gave up on about one in three opening attempts and tried to open the target apps 37% less often. One of its authors is one sec's developer.

Good to know: one app is free; Pro covers more apps for $6.99 a month, $19.99 a year or $99.99 once.

5. Freedom: best for blocking on phone and computer

Freedom runs one blocking session on your phone, tablet and computer at the same time, so you can't just switch screens. It works on iPhone, Android, Mac, Windows and Chromebook, and its Locked Mode gives you one minute to undo before the session can't be ended.

Basic blocking is free; Premium is $8.99 a month or about $3.33 a month billed yearly, with a 7-day trial on the yearly plan, per the official pricing page.

Good to know: it is the best pick for studying or desk work, where the laptop is the bigger problem.

6. Brainrot: the most playful app blocker

Brainrot gives you a cartoon brain whose health drops while you doomscroll, next to blocks, schedules and a focus timer. Before TikTok opens, the brain asks if you really need it right now.

It is iPhone only and every feature needs a subscription; the App Store lists plans from $3.99 a month or $19.99 a year. Blocking runs on your phone through Apple's Screen Time; more in our Brainrot app review.

Good to know: if guilt-tinged nudges make you spiral, a neutral pause like ScreenZen may sit better.

7. Forest: best focus timer that blocks apps

Forest grows a tree while you focus and kills it if you leave early. On iOS 16 or later it can also block apps during a session with an Allow List, and schedule blocks with Time Guard.

It is free on iPhone and Android, with an optional Forest Plus subscription. Read our Forest review for ADHD for the details.

Good to know: it works for one session at a time, not all-day schedules.

8. Focus Friend: best for company while you focus

Focus Friend, from Hank Green, gives you a little Bean that knits while you work and gets sad if you quit early. Its Deep Focus Mode locks the apps you choose during a session.

It is free, with Pro at $3.99 a month, $19.99 a year or $39.99 lifetime. See our Focus Friend review.

Good to know: its FAQ says it can clash with other Screen Time apps like Opal and Forest, so run one blocker at a time.

Some all-in-one apps bundle a blocker too. Wisey pairs one with courses and a focus timer, but at about $48.50 every 28 days; see our Wisey app review.

If friends and streaks motivate you more than locks, BePresent turns blocking into a game with leaderboards and rewards; see our BePresent app review.

Which type of app blocker works best for ADHD?

The one that matches how you get pulled in. Many people with ADHD open an app on impulse before they've decided to, which is a problem with executive function, the brain skills that pause an urge and weigh it against a goal (NIMH).

You open apps on autopilot

A pause app (ScreenZen, one sec) catches the reflex and hands the choice back to you.

You lose whole evenings

Scheduled blocks (Opal, Freedom) decide in advance, when you're calm.

You always find a loophole

A physical lock (Brick) or a mode you can't cancel (Opal Pro, Freedom Locked Mode).

You need a reason to stay

Instant rewards (Forest, Focus Friend, Brainrot); more in ADHD and dopamine.

If your worst distractions are on a laptop, our Cold Turkey Blocker review covers a strict blocker for Windows and Mac.

Whichever you pick, set it up in a calm moment, not mid-scroll. A 2018 JAMA study of 2,587 teens found a modest link between frequent digital media use and later ADHD symptoms, though it can't say one causes the other.

How do you block apps on iPhone for free?

Use Screen Time, which is built into every iPhone. It's free, but by default it shows an "Ignore Limit" button, so it's the easiest block to skip.

STEP 1

Open Screen Time

Settings, then Screen Time.

STEP 2

Add a limit

App Limits, then Add Limit (on iOS 27 it's called Time Allowances). Pick the apps and a daily time.

STEP 3

Make it harder to skip

Set a Screen Time passcode and give it to someone you trust.

You can also schedule Downtime, when only the apps you allow will open. Apple's Screen Time guide has the steps for each iOS version.

How do you block apps on Android for free?

Use Digital Wellbeing: open Settings, tap Digital Wellbeing, then set an app timer for each distracting app. When time runs out, the app closes and its icon turns grey until midnight.

Its Focus mode pauses chosen apps on a schedule and hides their notifications, per Google's Android help. "Take a break" lifts it in one tap, so use ScreenZen or Freedom if that's too easy.

What should you do once your apps are blocked?

Have one small, specific task ready, because a blocked phone and a blank hour is when you start hunting for loopholes. The scroll is often an escape from a task that feels too big to start, what we call ADHD task paralysis.

FAQ

What is the best free app blocker?

ScreenZen is the strongest free app blocker on iPhone and Android, with growing delays, open limits and strict blocks, funded by optional tips. Built-in Screen Time and Digital Wellbeing are free too, but easier to skip.

What is the best app blocker for iPhone?

ScreenZen if you want it free, Opal if you want schedules and a mode you can't cancel, and Brick if you need a physical lock. All three run on Apple's Screen Time framework.

What is the best app blocker for Android?

ScreenZen, Opal, one sec, Freedom and Brick all work on Android. Freedom is the best pick if you also want to block the same sites on your computer.

Can you get around an app blocker?

Usually, yes, on the gentler settings. Strict modes like Opal's Hard Mode, Freedom's Locked Mode and Brick's physical lock are built to make that much harder.

Do app blockers help with ADHD?

Many people with ADHD find they help with impulsive scrolling, and a 2023 study found a pause app cut opening attempts by 37%. They don't treat ADHD, so use them alongside the care a clinician recommends.

Is FOCO an app blocker?

No. FOCO is an ADHD planner that breaks a stuck task into small steps, puts it in your calendar and stays with you while you focus, so it works well next to any blocker on this list.
