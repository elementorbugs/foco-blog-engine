Finch App Review: Is the Self-Care Pet Good for ADHD?

Updated October 19, 2026 · Prices and ratings checked today · Not tested hands-on, based on official listings · We make FOCO, mentioned in the verdict

Short answer: Finch is a gentle self-care app where small daily goals, like getting out of bed or drinking water, give energy to a virtual pet bird. It is not an ADHD app, but its no-penalty rewards and tiny goals suit many ADHD adults on low days, and its core is free with an optional Finch Plus subscription.

  • Try Finch if basic self-care slips on hard days, and a cute pet and zero guilt would keep you coming back.
  • Skip it if you need help with work tasks, deadlines or focus sessions.
  • Pick FOCO if a specific task won't start, and you want AI to split it into timed steps with a focus companion on screen.

What does Finch look like in use?

The screenshots below are Finch's official App Store screenshots, not our own. We did not test Finch hands-on, so we describe what the listing shows and what Finch says the app does.

Each day starts with a short list of goals, many of them tiny: get out of bed, brush teeth, wash your face. Each one you tick gives your bird energy, and with enough energy it heads off on an adventure and comes back with a story.

You can also pair up with friends as Goal Buddies, see each other's shared goals, and send each other small notes of support.

Around the goals sit mood check-ins in the morning and evening, guided journaling, breathing exercises, short mental-health quizzes and insights that combine them.

Is Finch good for ADHD?

It can be, even though it isn't built for ADHD. Its listing talks about stress, anxiety and depression, not ADHD, but many ADHD adults use it for the parts of the day that fall apart first: meals, water, hygiene, sleep.

Two design choices matter most. Goals can be as small as you like, and missing one costs nothing, which avoids the shame spiral that apps with streak losses or penalties can cause (we compare that approach in our Habitica alternatives).

What does Finch get right for ADHD brains?

It rewards right away. Every tick gives your pet energy on the spot, the kind of immediate reward that many ADHD brains respond to better than a distant goal (more in ADHD and dopamine).

It turns care into caring for something else. Its own reviews describe journaling "in order to help Finch" on days they wouldn't do it for themselves.

It checks in on feelings. Morning and evening mood checks and journaling prompts give a gentle routine for emotional regulation, which is often harder with ADHD (see our emotional regulation guide).

Where does Finch fall short?

It is not a productivity tool. There is no task breakdown, no focus timer and no calendar, so a work deadline or a project you're avoiding gets no direct help.

A goal on the list is also not the same as starting it. If "do the report" sits there day after day, the problem is task initiation, explained in our guide to task initiation deficit.

And it is not care. Finch is a self-care companion, not therapy or treatment for ADHD, anxiety or depression.

How much does Finch cost?

Finch is free to download and use, with an optional Finch Plus subscription. The US App Store lists several Finch Plus prices, from $5.99 to $69.99, so check the price shown in the app.

According to Internet Matters, a UK online safety charity, Plus mainly adds extra customization and a deeper library of goals and exercises.

FAQ

Is the Finch app good for ADHD?

It can help with daily self-care, thanks to tiny goals and no penalties. It doesn't help with work tasks or focus, so many people pair it with a planner.

Is Finch free?

Yes, Finch is free to use. Finch Plus is an optional subscription with extra content and customization.

How much is Finch Plus?

The US App Store lists Finch Plus prices from $5.99 to $69.99. Check the price shown in the app for your plan.

Is Finch on Android?

Yes. Finch is on Google Play as well as the App Store.

Who makes Finch?

Finch is made by Finch Care, a public benefit corporation.

What is the difference between Finch and FOCO?

Finch rewards small self-care goals with a virtual pet. FOCO breaks one stuck task into timed steps and keeps a focus companion on screen while you do them.
