What does Aligned look like in use?

The screenshots below are Aligned's official App Store screenshots, not our own. We did not test Aligned hands-on, so we describe what the listing shows and what Aligned says the app does.

You open a conversation and say what's going on, in text or on a voice call. The coach answers with a plan sized to the problem, then tracks the goals and routines you agree on.

You also pick a coach style: calmer or more direct, practical or encouraging. The screenshots show three archetypes, The Challenger, The Strategist and The Empath.

After the chat, the coach suggests tasks and routines and follows up with check-ins. "Added to your 6:45 list, with a nudge the night before" is the kind of follow-up the listing promises.

Recent update notes add sending photos and files to the coach, creating goals during a voice call, and saving transcripts as PDFs. A PDF is handy if you want to bring a conversation to a therapist or a human ADHD coach.

Is Aligned built for ADHD?

Partly. The Google Play description now calls it "ADHD-friendly by design," with short steps and no streaks to break, while the App Store description talks about everyday life in general and does not mention ADHD.

In practice it is a general life coach covering home, work, family and health. An app designed around executive function, the brain's self-management system for planning, starting and switching tasks, makes narrower choices.

Inflow, for example, is built on ADHD-specific CBT lessons (see our Inflow review). Aligned is a flexible conversation partner that happens to fit a lot of ADHD friction.

What does Aligned get right for ADHD brains?

It lowers the cost of asking for help. You don't fill in a planner or build a system first, you just say what's wrong.

Thoughts often arrive as a jumble, half a sentence or a photo of a room you can't face. Aligned accepts all three, which works a lot like a guided brain dump.

The listing's own example is a pantry photo, answered with "the floor, twenty minutes, nothing else." Naming one first move is close to how small a first step should be for an overwhelmed brain.

Goals, routines and nudges are tracked for you. That offloads some of the working memory load that makes "I'll remember later" fail so often (more in our guide to working memory and ADHD).

Where does Aligned fall short?

Its limits come from being a conversation, not a doing tool. On a day of real task paralysis, opening the app and explaining what's wrong can be as hard as the task itself.

A good plan also isn't the same as starting. Knowing the first step and doing it are separate problems, and the second one is the core of task initiation trouble, explained in our guide to task initiation deficit.

Psychologist Russell Barkley describes ADHD as a problem of self-regulation more than knowledge (Barkley, executive function fact sheet). Once the chat ends, no timer runs beside you and nothing keeps you on the step you agreed to.

Is Aligned coaching or therapy?

Coaching. Both store listings say "Aligned is not a medical service and does not provide diagnosis or treatment."

That is worth knowing because Aligned's own website (tryalignedapp.com) is titled "AI Coaching & Therapy," and some Google Play reviewers call it therapy. The store disclaimer is the one that counts.

That lines up with how CHADD describes ADHD coaching: practical support with daily life, not treatment for ADHD, anxiety or depression. No AI coach is a safe place for a mental health emergency; in the US, call or text 988.

How much does Aligned cost?

Aligned is free to download, with nine in-app purchase options on the US App Store. Weekly is $9.99, monthly is $19.99 or $29.99, quarterly is $39.99, and yearly plans run $49.99, $59.99, $79.99, $99.99 or $149.99 (App Store listing).

The listing does not say which offer you will see, or whether a free trial applies. At $9.99 a week, the weekly plan works out to about $519 a year, more than three times the top yearly price.

Is Aligned safe to use?

It is a real, actively updated app (last updated October 3, 2026) with a clear disclaimer. Some negative Google Play reviews describe unexpected subscription charges, so keep your receipt and renewal date handy.

Its App Store privacy label lists data linked to you, including user content, contact info, location and usage data, and lists purchases, location and other data as used to track you. Think about what you type and photograph.

Who should try Aligned?

Try Aligned if:

  • You think best out loud and want something to talk to at 11pm.
  • Your stuck points are big and fuzzy rather than one specific task.
  • You want gentle accountability across many areas of life, not just work.

Skip it if:

  • You need evidence-based treatment. That is a job for a clinician, as the NIMH ADHD overview outlines.
  • Weekly billing is likely to slip past you. Forgotten renewals are a classic ADHD tax.

FAQ

Is the Aligned app good for ADHD?

It can help with ADHD overwhelm, especially if talking things through helps you think. It is a general life coach, though, and it won't stay with you while you do the task.

Is Aligned free?

It is free to download, but coaching is sold as in-app subscriptions. The App Store listing does not state a free trial.

How much does Aligned cost per month?

The US App Store lists monthly plans at $19.99 and $29.99. There are also weekly ($9.99), quarterly ($39.99) and yearly ($49.99 to $149.99) options.

Is Aligned a therapy app?

No. Its store listings say it is not a medical service and does not provide diagnosis or treatment.

Does Aligned work on Android?

Yes. It is on Google Play, rated 4.1, as well as iPhone, iPad and Apple silicon Macs.

Who makes the Aligned app?

The US App Store lists the seller as GM LAP LIMITED, and Google Play lists Mate academy as the developer.
