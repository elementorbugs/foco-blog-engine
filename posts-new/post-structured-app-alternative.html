Why do people look for a Structured alternative?

Usually not because Structured is bad. It has a 4.8 rating from 167,268 ratings on the US App Store, and its help center says you can use "almost every feature of Structured for free" (What is Structured Pro?).

It is also no longer Apple-only. Structured launched on Android in November 2023 and on the web in August 2024, though it says some iOS features are still missing there (Structured platform availability).

The friction is the timeline itself. It asks you to guess in the morning how long everything will take, and with time blindness those guesses often miss, so the plan stops matching the day.

Others plan fine and still freeze at the first block. That is a task initiation problem, and a cleaner schedule does not touch it.

What are the best Structured alternatives for ADHD?

1. FOCO, best for starting a task you keep avoiding

We make FOCO, and it is first here only because this is its job. You add a stuck task, AI breaks it into small steps with a time estimate for each, and a focus timer with calm music and an on-screen AI body doubling companion stays with you through step one.

It also has a simple day view (morning, afternoon, anytime), voice capture, and missed tasks move on without a red overdue wall. It is not an hour-by-hour timeline like Structured, so if you love that view, keep it for planning.

Price: 7-day free trial, then $9/month or $59/year. No free tier; iPhone and Android, no web app (checked Oct 9, 2026).

2. Tiimo, best for a visual planner built for neurodivergent users

Tiimo is the closest like-for-like switch: a color-coded timeline with icons, plus routines, reminders and a focus timer. It is designed for ADHD and autistic users first, where Structured is a general planner that many ADHD users like.

The trade-off is cost: most of the app sits behind Pro. Its Android app was rebuilt in 2026 and is still catching up to iOS (Tiimo's Android relaunch).

Price: free version with some tools; Pro monthly or yearly, price varies by country, 7-day trial on yearly only (Tiimo pricing, checked Oct 9, 2026).

3. Sunsama, best for a guided daily planning ritual

Sunsama walks you through planning each morning, pulls in tasks from other tools, and helps you set a realistic workload for the day.

If you skipped Structured's quick setup, a longer ritual may be harder, not easier. There is also no free plan, and Sunsama says it does not plan to add one.

Price: $17/month billed yearly or $22/month billed monthly, after a 14-day trial with no card. Its FAQ lists slightly different figures, so confirm on the Sunsama pricing page (checked Oct 9, 2026).

4. Motion, best for letting AI build your schedule

You enter tasks with deadlines and durations, and Motion arranges them around your calendar, then reshuffles when something moves. For ADHD, that removes the daily job of rebuilding a broken plan.

It still depends on time estimates you enter, and there is no free plan to fall back on. Its App Store rating (4.1) is also the lowest on this list.

Price: free trial, no free plan. For one person, Pro AI is $29 a month billed yearly or $49 billed monthly; the $19 often quoted is the per-seat team price (Motion pricing, checked Oct 9, 2026).

5. TickTick, best for tasks, calendar and habits in one

TickTick combines a to-do list, calendar views, habit tracking and a Pomodoro timer. You can use only the list and ignore the rest, which is gentler than an app that needs full setup.

The free plan allows 9 lists with up to 99 tasks each. The trade-off is many settings, which can turn into a tinkering trap.

Price: Premium is $49.99/year (TickTick upgrade page, checked Oct 9, 2026).

6. Todoist, best when the timeline itself is too much

If a visual day overwhelms you, a plain list may help. Todoist is fast for capturing tasks the moment they pop into your head.

It has no time-blocking view by default, so it will not show you the shape of the day. Keep one Today list and skip elaborate projects.

Price: free Beginner plan with 5 personal projects; Pro is $7 a month or $60 a year since a December 2025 price change (Todoist help center) (checked Oct 9, 2026).

7. Reclaim.ai, best for protecting focus time on a busy calendar

Reclaim sits on top of Google Calendar or Outlook and books focus time, habits and breaks around your meetings. It keeps a normal calendar at the center, so there is no separate timeline to maintain.

It suits meeting-heavy work days more than a personal life plan. We found no Reclaim app on the US App Store, so treat it as a web and calendar tool.

Price: free Lite plan; Starter $10/month billed yearly or $12/month monthly (Reclaim pricing, checked Oct 9, 2026).

Who should stay with Structured?

  • You like one clean line for one day, and your days mostly run close to plan.
  • You want most features free, or a one-time lifetime Pro option.
  • You tend to overbook yourself: its opt-in Energy Monitor warns when a day goes over your limit (Structured help).

FAQ

Is there a free alternative to Structured?

Yes. TickTick, Todoist and Reclaim.ai have free plans, and Tiimo has a free version with some tools, though Structured itself is mostly free.

What is the best Structured alternative for ADHD?

Tiimo, if you want a visual timeline built for neurodivergent users. If you plan fine but can't start, FOCO is built for that moment.

Is Structured only on iPhone?

No. Structured runs on iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch and Mac, and launched on Android in November 2023 and the web in August 2024.

Is Structured free?

Mostly. Structured says almost every feature is free, and Pro adds Structured AI, recurring tasks, calendar import and custom notifications.

Which Structured alternative plans my day for me?

Motion builds and reshuffles your schedule automatically. Reclaim.ai books focus time and habits around an existing Google or Outlook calendar.

Is FOCO free?

No. FOCO has a 7-day free trial, then costs $9 a month or $59 a year.
