Flow Club Review: Body Doubling Sessions for ADHD

Flow Club is a paid virtual coworking service where small groups work together on video. A volunteer host runs the timer and the goal check-ins, and it costs US$40 a month or US$400 a year after a 7-day free trial that needs no credit card.

  • Try Flow Club if working next to other people gets you started, and a host plus a group feels easier than a one-on-one call.
  • Skip it if US$40 a month is a stretch, or a camera on you makes it harder to focus.
  • Pick FOCO, the app we make, if you want a body-doubling companion on your phone with no camera and no schedule, plus help splitting the task into small steps.

What does a Flow Club session look like?

We did not test Flow Club hands-on. The images below come from its official website, so they show the service as Flow Club presents it.

A 60-minute session follows a set script: about 5 minutes to share goals, 50 minutes of muted work with optional music, and 5 minutes to share how it went. Your goals and the timer stay on screen next to the video tiles.

You book sessions from a schedule, and each one shows its host, length and format. A host directory lets you filter by badges such as "Neurodivergent" or by time zone.

Is Flow Club good for ADHD?

It can be, because it is built on body doubling: working while another person is present. In a survey of 220 neurodivergent adults by Eagle and colleagues, 85% said they were more likely to finish a task with someone else there.

Flow Club adds structure on top of that. Booking a time turns "later" into an appointment, and saying your goal out loud gives you a reason to start when the timer does.

Flow Club's own member survey says 62% of 245 respondents identify as having ADHD. Among them, 97% said it became easier to start difficult tasks, but that is the company's own survey of its members, not an independent study.

The evidence on body doubling is still early, and our guide to body doubling for ADHD walks through what the studies do and do not show. For ADHD itself, the National Institute of Mental Health is a better place to start.

Where might Flow Club fall short?

The price: At US$40 a month it costs more than most ADHD apps. Flow Club compares it to a fitness class or two, for unlimited sessions.

No free plan: After the 7-day trial you pay or stop. Because no card is needed, there is no surprise charge.

The camera: Flow Club asks you to keep video on as much as you can. Chat-only sessions exist, but camera-on is the default.

Computer only: Its FAQ tells you to join from your computer. That suits desk work, but not a sink full of dishes.

You still pick the task: The host asks what you will work on. If the task feels too big to name a first step, you may stall before the timer starts.

How much does Flow Club cost?

Flow Club costs US$40 a month or US$400 a year, which works out to US$33.33 a month. You can cancel anytime and keep access until the end of the billing period.

Full-time students and nonprofit workers can get 50% off, and hosts can lower their membership by up to 50% by hosting sessions. You can become a host after 10 sessions.

The 7-day free trial needs no payment method. Flow Club's help center says there is "nothing to cancel" when it ends.

Flow Club also says it can count as a business expense or an ADHD workplace accommodation, and it has a guide for asking your employer to pay.

How is Flow Club different from Focusmate?

Flow Club is a hosted group of up to 9 people. Focusmate pairs you with one other person, with no host.

When we reviewed Focusmate, it had a free plan with 3 sessions a week and paid plans from $8 a month billed yearly. Flow Club has no free plan and costs more, but adds a host, music and a group.

If a one-on-one call feels intense, a group may feel easier. If price matters most, start with Focusmate's free sessions.

FAQ

How much is Flow Club?

Flow Club is US$40 a month or US$400 a year, which is US$33.33 a month. Full-time students and nonprofit workers can get 50% off.

Is Flow Club free?

No, but it has a 7-day free trial with no credit card. There is no free plan after the trial.

Is Flow Club good for ADHD?

It can help with starting and staying on task, because it is built on body doubling with a host and a timer. Its own member survey reports strong results, but it is not an independent study.

Do you have to keep your camera on in Flow Club?

Flow Club asks members to keep video on as much as they can, but says turning it off is okay. It also offers chat-only sessions where you do not talk.

How long are Flow Club sessions?

Sessions run from 30 minutes to 3 hours, and 60 minutes is the most popular. A 60-minute session has about 5 minutes for goals, 50 minutes of muted work and 5 minutes to share progress.

Is Flow Club better than Focusmate?

It depends on what helps you start. Flow Club is a hosted group with music and costs more, while Focusmate pairs you with one person and has a free plan.
