Why do people look for a Tiimo alternative?

Usually because of price or fit, not because Tiimo is a bad app. It is a well-liked visual planner built for neurodivergent people, rated 4.6 (20,673 ratings) on the US App Store.

The free version includes "a selection of planning tools," and the full app, including the web version, needs Pro. Tiimo says the Pro price varies by country, and its 7-day free trial applies to the yearly plan only (Tiimo pricing).

Android users have another reason to look around. Tiimo rebuilt its Android app in 2026 and says it is still adding features to match iOS (Tiimo's Android relaunch post).

The third reason is quieter. A beautiful plan of your day does not always get you to begin the first item on it, and that gap is a different problem from planning.

What are the best Tiimo alternatives for ADHD?

We ordered these by the job they do best, not by a score. Pick the one that matches the reason you are leaving Tiimo.

1. FOCO, best for starting a task you keep avoiding

We make FOCO, so weigh this entry with that in mind. It is first only because it targets a specific gap: task initiation, the moment where you know what to do and still can't begin.

You type or say a stuck task, and FOCO's AI breaks it into small steps with a time estimate on each. Then a focus timer opens on the first step, with calm music and an on-screen companion, a light form of body doubling.

FOCO also has a day view with morning, afternoon and anytime slots, and missed tasks move forward without a red overdue wall. The trade-off: it is not an hour-by-hour visual timeline like Tiimo, it is phone-only, and there is no free tier.

Price: 7-day free trial, then $9 a month or $59 a year (checked Oct 9, 2026).

2. Structured, best free visual timeline

Structured is the closest one-to-one swap for Tiimo. Each task is a colored block on a single-day timeline, sized to how long it takes, which makes the shape of the day easy to see.

Its help center says you can use "almost every feature of Structured for free," with Pro adding Structured AI, recurring tasks, calendar import and custom notifications (What is Structured Pro?). It now runs on Android and the web too, though Structured says some iOS features are still missing there (Structured platform availability).

The trade-off: it is a general planner rather than one designed around neurodivergent users, so it offers less of the ADHD-specific scaffolding Tiimo is built for.

Price: Free version with most features; Pro is monthly, yearly or a one-time lifetime purchase, priced by region (checked Oct 9, 2026).

3. Goblin Tools, best free task breakdown

If what you used most in Tiimo was splitting big tasks into steps, Goblin Tools does that one job for free. Type a task into Magic ToDo, press a button, and you get a short list of concrete steps, with a "spiciness" control for how small they get.

We have used the website ourselves, and it needs no account or setup. Its makers say existing features will never move behind a paywall (goblin.tools About).

The trade-off: there is no calendar, timeline or reminders, and no time estimate on each step. It is a list, and the rest is up to you.

Price: Free website; iPhone app $1.99 one-time on the US App Store; optional Pro for advanced features (checked Oct 9, 2026).

4. TickTick, best all-in-one app on a budget

TickTick puts a to-do list, calendar views, a Pomo timer and a habit tracker in one app. If Tiimo felt like one more app on top of your to-do list, TickTick can replace both.

The free plan covers 9 lists with up to 99 tasks each, reminders, and sync across devices, with only a basic calendar view (TickTick plans). It runs almost everywhere, including Windows and Linux.

The trade-off: it is a general productivity app with a lot of options. If setting up a system tends to become its own way of avoiding the work, that depth can work against you.

Price: Free plan; Premium listed at US$49.99 a year on TickTick's site, and store prices may differ by region (checked Oct 9, 2026).

5. Amazing Marvin, best for building your own system

Amazing Marvin is built with ADHD in mind and lets you switch features on and off until the app fits how you work. It includes a Procrastination Wizard for stuck tasks, Pomodoro timers and a focus mode.

It runs on web, Mac, Windows, iPhone and Android. Students get 50% off, and Marvin offers pay-what-you-can by email (Amazing Marvin pricing).

The trade-off: it has no free version, and all that choice takes setup time.

Price: $12 a month, or $96 a year; 14-day free trial, no credit card (checked Oct 9, 2026).

Who should stay with Tiimo?

  • You use the visual timeline daily and seeing the day is what keeps you on track, especially with time blindness.
  • You want an app designed for neurodivergent users, with routines, a focus timer and reminders in one place.
  • You are on iPhone, iPad or Apple Watch, where Tiimo is most complete.

FAQ

Is there a free alternative to Tiimo?

Yes. Structured offers almost every feature for free, Goblin Tools is free on the web, and TickTick has a free plan.

What is the best Tiimo alternative for ADHD?

It depends on why you are leaving. Structured is the closest visual planner, while FOCO fits people whose plan is fine but who can't start the first task.

Is Structured better than Tiimo?

Structured is cheaper and simpler, with most features free. Tiimo is designed specifically for neurodivergent users and adds routines and a focus timer.

Is Tiimo free?

Tiimo has a free version with a selection of planning tools on iOS and Android. The full app, including the web version, needs Pro, with a 7-day trial on the yearly plan.

Is there a Tiimo alternative for Windows?

Yes. TickTick and Amazing Marvin both have Windows apps, and Structured and Goblin Tools work in a web browser.

Can an app help me start tasks, not just plan them?

Some can. FOCO breaks a stuck task into timed steps and opens a focus session on the first one, and Amazing Marvin has a Procrastination Wizard.

How did we compare them?

We checked each app's pricing page, help center and US App Store listing on October 9, 2026. Ratings are from the US App Store on that date, rounded to one decimal.

We did not test Tiimo, Structured, TickTick or Amazing Marvin hands-on for this update. We have used FOCO, which we make, and the Goblin Tools website, as described in our Goblin Tools vs Tiimo test.

We judged each app on what tends to trip up people with ADHD: setup effort, seeing time, and getting started. Psychologist Russell Barkley describes ADHD as a problem of self-regulation more than knowledge (Barkley, executive function fact sheet), which is why we weigh starting as heavily as planning.

For background on ADHD itself, see the National Institute of Mental Health and CHADD. Our guide to task initiation deficit explains the planning-versus-starting gap in more depth.
