What happened when we gave Goblin Tools a real task?

We typed "Clean the kitchen" into Magic ToDo on goblin.tools and pressed the breakdown button. No account, no setup, and the steps appeared in a few seconds.

The six steps were sensible and concrete. You can break any step down again, and the "spiciness" control sets how small the steps get.

Honestly, for a lot of people that is enough. If seeing "clear countertops" as step one gets you off the couch, you are done, and it cost nothing.

What the list does not tell you is how long each step takes, and it does not stay with you once you start. Magic ToDo has been the core of the site for a while, as this earlier screenshot shows.

What does FOCO do with the same kind of task?

FOCO also breaks a stuck task into small steps with AI, but each step gets a time estimate. Here is a real task from the app, "Finish my presentation."

The first step is small on purpose, three minutes. From there the task opens into a focus session with one step on screen, a soundscape and the companion.

Which is better when you can't start a task?

It depends on where you get stuck. If the task feels like a fog with no edges, Goblin Tools clears it fastest; if you can see the steps and still freeze, FOCO is built for that second gap.

Task initiation is the gap between knowing what to do and doing it. Psychologist Russell Barkley describes ADHD as a problem of self-regulation rather than knowledge (Barkley, executive function fact sheet), so lowering the effort to begin can matter more than adding features.

Goblin Tools wins on that effort: one box, one button, no install. FOCO asks you to install an app first, which is a real cost if setup is the thing you avoid.

Where FOCO earns that cost is after the list exists. A tiny first step with a time on it, plus something on screen while you do it, is aimed at the freeze our guide to task initiation deficit describes.

Which is better for planning your day?

FOCO, because Goblin Tools has no calendar. Goblin Tools is a set of single-purpose helpers, and planning the day is not one of its jobs.

In FOCO, tasks land in a built-in calendar sorted into morning, afternoon or anytime, with reminders. Missed tasks move on to the next day instead of piling into a red overdue wall.

You can also add tasks by voice or photograph a handwritten list. If your plans live on sticky notes, that skips the retyping step.

Goblin Tools does let you keep lists: an optional account syncs them across devices, according to its About page. That is list storage, not a day plan, and for many people that is plenty.

What does each one cost?

Goblin Tools: the website is free, and its makers say existing features will never move behind a paywall. The phone apps are a one-time purchase ($1.99 on the US App Store, Android price varies), and a new optional Pro subscription covers only advanced features that are costly to run.

FOCO: a 7-day free trial, then $9 a month or $59 a year. There is no free tier.

If cost decides it, Goblin Tools wins outright. Prices change, so check the store or the official site before you pay.

Who should pick which?

Choose Goblin Tools if:

  • You need a breakdown in the next minute and nothing else.
  • You don't want another subscription, or you want to use it in a browser on any computer.
  • You have quit other apps during setup.
  • You would also use its other tools, like Formalizer for email tone or Estimator for time guesses.

Choose FOCO if:

  • Breaking the task down is not the problem; starting step one is.
  • You want a time on every step and a focus timer with a companion while you work.
  • You want tasks in a calendar with reminders, added by voice or photo.
  • You are fine with a paid app after a 7-day free trial ($9 a month or $59 a year), on iPhone or Android.

FAQ

Is Goblin Tools still free?

Yes, the website is free and needs no account. The phone apps cost a small one-time fee, and an optional Pro subscription adds advanced features only.

Is FOCO free?

No. FOCO has a 7-day free trial, then costs $9 a month or $59 a year.

Does Goblin Tools save my lists?

Yes, if you want it to. An optional account syncs your lists across devices.

Does Goblin Tools have a focus timer?

No countdown timer. Its Taskmaster tool keeps one task on screen, which helps some people focus.

Can I use FOCO and Goblin Tools together?

Yes. You can use Goblin Tools for a quick free breakdown, then move a bigger task into FOCO when you want a timer and a plan.

Is there a Goblin Tools app for iPhone?

Yes. It is a one-time purchase, $1.99 on the US App Store as of October 9, 2026.
