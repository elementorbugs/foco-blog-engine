What is a visual timer?

A visual timer shows time as a shape that shrinks, so you can see how much is left at a glance without reading numbers. This one is a coloured wedge that gets smaller as the minutes pass, like the classic red-disc timers used in classrooms.

It runs in your browser, on a phone, tablet, laptop or a classroom screen, and keeps counting when you switch tabs.

Why does a visual timer help with ADHD?

Because it puts time outside your head, where you can see it. Many people with ADHD describe time blindness: ten minutes and an hour can feel the same until suddenly it's late.

Research backs the feeling. A 2022 meta-analysis of 55 studies in the Journal of the American Academy of Child and Adolescent Psychiatry found that people with ADHD, on average, estimate and produce time intervals less accurately than people without it.

A 2024 meta-analysis covering children and adults found the same kind of gap across the lifespan (Developmental Neuropsychology). A timer doesn't change that, but it lets you check time by looking instead of guessing.

ADHD researcher Russell Barkley has long recommended making time external and visible for exactly this reason. Read more in our guide to his executive function model.

How long should I set the timer to start a task?

Start smaller than feels useful: five minutes is a good default. The hard part of a stuck task is usually the start, not the work, what we call ADHD task paralysis.

Tell yourself you only owe it five minutes, then use Keep going +5 if you're rolling. More on why it works in our 5-minute timer guide, or try 25 minutes with breaks using the Pomodoro method.

Is this the same as a Time Timer?

It works the same way: a coloured disc that shrinks over a 60-minute face. Time Timer is a physical clock you buy; this one is free in your browser and adds things a desk clock can't, like a countdown in your tab, halfway cues and focus sounds.

For kids and classrooms, use full screen with the numbers hidden. Children often read the size of the colour long before they can read a clock.
