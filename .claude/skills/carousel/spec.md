# FOCO Carousel Engine — Master Content & Performance Specification V2

(Adi, 2026-11-17. MASTER document: it wins over SKILL.md wherever they differ. Saved verbatim.)

## 1. Mission

Create authentic, emotionally recognizable, high-performing Instagram and TikTok photo carousels that turn everyday ADHD struggles into relatable stories and motivate viewers to try FOCO.

**Primary business objective:** Generate qualified app installs and paid subscribers.

**Secondary objectives:**
- Increase shares and saves.
- Generate profile visits and link clicks.
- Build familiarity with recurring characters.
- Improve organic discovery through relevant search language.
- Learn which stories, hooks, characters and situations lead to conversions.

**Core principle:** The audience should feel like they discovered a real moment from someone's life, not watched an app advertisement.

FOCO is not the hero of the story. The person making progress is the hero. FOCO is simply the tool that helps them begin.

---

## 2. Audience Strategy

### Primary audience

English-speaking women aged approximately 25–45 who:
- Have ADHD or strongly identify with executive-function difficulties.
- Are mothers, freelancers, remote workers, business owners or working parents.
- Struggle with task initiation, overwhelm, procrastination and time blindness.
- Have responsibilities they genuinely want to fulfill but struggle to start.
- Have tried conventional productivity methods that do not consistently work for them.

Do not assume every person with ADHD experiences the same difficulties.

### Secondary audiences for future testing

- College students with ADHD.
- Self-employed adults without children.
- Corporate employees working from home.
- People who experience task paralysis without identifying as having ADHD.

**Initial campaign focus:** Mothers who work from home or run small businesses.

Each carousel must speak to one recognizable audience and one specific moment.

## 3. Character Strategy

### Main recurring character: Jess

Jess is the central character of the first content series.

Use one consistent visual identity for Jess across the series.

Preserve:
- Facial features.
- Approximate age.
- Hair, skin tone and physical proportions.
- General clothing style.
- Apartment layout and recognizable household objects.
- Family context and recurring environmental details.

Clothing, expressions, room lighting and everyday mess may naturally change.

Do not make the character look artificially perfect, excessively glamorous or visibly staged.

### Alternative character testing

A different mother may be introduced for a deliberate creative test.

Before producing the first carousel with a new character:

1. Define her age, appearance, family situation, work and apartment.
2. Generate one base portrait in her home.
3. Show the base image for approval.
4. After approval, save her reference image and character profile.
5. Use that approved visual identity throughout her carousel.

Never mix character identities within one carousel.

A test character must receive her own story, context and visual identity rather than automatically inheriting Jess's biography.

Do not claim that a character is a real customer or that the story is a genuine personal testimonial unless that is verified.

## 4. Research-Driven Storytelling

Research real conversations about ADHD, task initiation and productivity from communities such as Reddit.

Extract repeated patterns, not isolated dramatic comments.

For each proposed story, identify:
- The specific problem people repeatedly describe.
- The words they naturally use.
- The emotional consequence.
- The situation or trigger.
- What they have already tried.
- What kind of small action might actually help.

Prioritize patterns mentioned by several independent people.

**Research quality rules:**
- Distinguish a recurring theme from a single anecdote.
- Never fabricate quotations, users, studies or evidence.
- Keep direct quotations exact, attributed and linked to their original source.
- Prefer paraphrasing recurring language naturally in the character's voice.
- Never present a fictional character's experience as a documented Reddit story.
- Do not manufacture medical, psychological or product-effectiveness claims.

Maintain a research file containing sources, recurring themes, frequency indicators and potential story ideas.

Research should inform the writing, not appear as awkward citations inside every carousel.

## 5. Story Angles and Creative Variety

Avoid producing the same narrative repeatedly.

Rotate between distinct emotional angles.

### A. The deadline

A client needs something by 3pm. Jess has two free hours but cannot begin.

### B. The invisible work

Jess has been busy all morning, yet the one important task remains untouched.

### C. Waiting mode

A meeting is scheduled later. Jess feels unable to do anything beforehand.

### D. The interrupted workday

A child needs something. Jess loses her train of thought and struggles to return.

### E. The tiny unfinished task

An email that might take five minutes has been sitting in her inbox for six days.

### F. The endless planning loop

Jess spends more time rewriting her to-do list than acting on it.

### G. The unexpected small win

Jess completes the first step without finishing the entire task. That small action is the story.

### H. The imperfect day

Jess still has unfinished work by the end of the day. The progress was real, but the story does not end in a miraculous transformation.

### I. The social consequence

Jess avoids opening a message because someone is waiting for her response.

### J. The task that looks too big

"Update the website" feels impossible until it becomes one very small, specific action.

No single story category should dominate the series.

Use a variety of stakes: clients, family logistics, personal admin, creative work, unpaid invoices, household responsibilities and business tasks.

## 6. Carousel Structure: Flexible, Not Formulaic

Default length: **6–8 slides**.

Use fewer or more only when the story genuinely benefits.

Every slide must accomplish a specific narrative function.

### Slide 1: The hook

Show a recognizable moment, tension or contradiction.

The viewer should immediately think: "I do this."

Examples:

"I had two hours to work. I spent 40 minutes opening my laptop."

"My client needed one tiny change. I couldn't even open the file."

"I wasn't relaxing. I was waiting for myself to start."

Avoid generic hooks such as:
- "5 ADHD tips you need."
- "This amazing app changed everything."
- "Are you struggling with productivity?"

### Slide 2: The situation

Establish the real-life context.

Who needs something? What task is waiting? When does it matter?

Use concrete details rather than broad descriptions.

### Slide 3: The internal struggle

Show the recognizable behavior.

This could be:
- Switching between tabs.
- Rewriting a list.
- Checking the time.
- Cleaning an unrelated surface.
- Avoiding a client message.
- Looking at the task without starting.

Do not describe the character as lazy or broken.

### Slide 4: The escalation

Introduce a believable complication.

A deadline approaches, a child wakes up, a client follows up or the available work window shrinks.

Avoid artificially dramatic emergencies.

### Slide 5: The turning point

Show the first meaningful shift.

FOCO may appear here if the story naturally calls for it.

Example:

"I opened FOCO and said, 'Finish the client proposal.'"

"Then I tapped to break it into smaller steps."

"The first one was just two minutes."

The depicted interaction must match an actual, tested FOCO feature and interface.

### Slide 6: The action

Show the character doing a tangible first step.

Examples:
- Opening the document.
- Writing the subject line.
- Finding the client brief.
- Editing the first paragraph.
- Replying to one message.

The outcome should be realistic, not exaggerated.

### Slide 7: The outcome

Show what changed.

Sometimes the task is finished. Sometimes only the first step is finished.

Both can be meaningful.

Use an authentic timestamp when it makes the story more concrete.

### Slide 8: The ending and CTA

Close with a memorable, emotionally grounded thought.

Possible examples:

"I didn't need another plan. I needed somewhere to start."

"The whole proposal wasn't easy. Opening it finally was."

"Maybe the first step was the part I needed help with."

Include a clear but natural invitation to try FOCO when appropriate.

Not every ending must use the same line or CTA.

## 7. Hook and Retention Rules

Every carousel must have one primary hook.

The hook should achieve at least one of the following:

1. Reveal a frustrating contradiction.
2. Describe an unusually specific everyday moment.
3. Create curiosity about what happened next.
4. Express a thought people recognize but rarely say aloud.
5. Show an immediate consequence of avoidance.

Prefer concrete moments to abstract declarations.

**Strong:**
"It's 12:40pm. My baby's still asleep. The proposal is still blank."

**Weak:**
"ADHD can make productivity challenging."

Do not automatically make hooks dramatic, negative or clickbait-driven.

The first slide should be understandable without reading the caption.

Slides should create narrative momentum, with each one giving the viewer a reason to swipe.

Do not use artificial cliffhangers such as "You won't believe what happened next."

## 8. Writing Rules

Write in natural, conversational American English.

The voice should feel like a private journal, a message to a friend or an unpolished personal thought.

### Required

- First-person storytelling for diary entries.
- Short, natural sentences with varied rhythm.
- Everyday vocabulary.
- Specific situations.
- One dominant idea per slide.
- Believable emotional reactions.
- Occasional imperfect phrasing when natural.
- Clear cause and effect.
- Realistic work and family details.
- Consistent timeline.

### Avoid

- Motivational speeches.
- Corporate marketing language.
- Therapy clichés.
- Excessive explanation of ADHD.
- Repeating the same formula.
- Artificially inspirational endings.
- Shame, blame or guilt-based persuasion.
- Overuse of "overwhelmed," "chaos," "game changer" and "finally."
- Em dashes.
- Invented statistics.
- Unsupported medical claims.
- Invented customer experiences.
- Promises that FOCO will eliminate ADHD symptoms.

Do not force every slide to contain an ADHD keyword.

Write for people first and search discovery second.

## 9. FOCO Product Integration

FOCO should appear naturally rather than dominate the narrative.

### Standard rule

Show FOCO once at the meaningful turning point.

A second small visual reference may appear in the ending if it helps establish the next action.

### Approved functionality

Only depict existing, verified features such as:
- Speaking a task.
- Typing a task.
- Capturing a task from an image.
- Breaking a task into smaller steps.
- Choosing the first action.
- Starting a focus session.

Do not imply FOCO automatically performs client work, sends emails, manages Google Calendar or guarantees task completion.

Do not invent buttons, interactions or UI states.

Use authentic app screenshots or UI representations that faithfully match the current version.

### Product visibility test

Test two approaches across separate carousels:

**Soft integration:** FOCO appears only as part of the story.

**Clear integration:** FOCO appears at the turning point and again with a concise CTA at the end.

The objective is to measure whether stronger product clarity improves qualified traffic and installs without reducing engagement.

## 10. Visual Storytelling Principles

The visual style will be defined in a separate design specification.

For now, follow these creative principles:

- Each slide must feel like a moment from the character's real day.
- Maintain strong continuity between the character, environment and story.
- Vary compositions, camera angles and objects.
- Use objects to communicate emotion and context.
- Prioritize immediate readability on mobile.
- Do not make the carousel resemble a conventional software advertisement.
- Avoid repetitive visual layouts.
- Do not invent unrelated props just to fill space.

Visual authenticity matters more than decorative complexity.

A crowded slide is not automatically more engaging.

## 11. Conversion and CTA Strategy

Use different CTA approaches to learn what drives action.

### Emotional CTA

"For the next task you can't start."

### Situational CTA

"Try it during the next nap."

### Product-led CTA

"Turn your next overwhelming task into a first step with FOCO."

### Curiosity CTA

"See what your first step could look like."

Test CTAs individually.

Never promise a free experience that does not exist.

Avoid discount claims, fabricated reviews, fake urgency and misleading guarantees.

For Instagram and TikTok, use platform-appropriate directions such as "link in bio" only when the link is actually available.

Do not claim that viewers can click a non-clickable carousel image.

---

## 12. Caption and Discovery Strategy

Create a separate caption for Instagram and TikTok.

Each caption should:
- Complement the story rather than repeat every slide.
- Include one natural search phrase when relevant.
- Stay conversational.
- Include a clear next action when appropriate.
- Use approximately 3–5 relevant hashtags.

Potential keywords:
- ADHD task paralysis
- ADHD planner
- ADHD procrastination
- Getting started with ADHD
- ADHD working mom
- Executive dysfunction
- ADHD productivity

Do not keyword-stuff.

Prioritize the terms most relevant to the specific story.

Never describe FOCO as clinically proven or medically effective without supporting evidence.

## 13. Creative Experimentation

Treat every carousel as a test with one primary hypothesis.

Examples:

**Test A: Hook type**
- Time-specific hook vs emotional confession.

**Test B: Story**
- Deadline pressure vs an everyday unfinished task.

**Test C: Character**
- Jess vs a different mother.

**Test D: Product visibility**
- Soft FOCO introduction vs clearer product demonstration.

**Test E: Ending**
- Emotional ending vs direct action CTA.

Change one major variable at a time whenever feasible.

Do not attribute performance differences to creative alone when posting time, account audience, distribution or other factors differ materially.

Maintain a record of each creative and its hypothesis.

## 14. Performance Measurement

### Primary business metrics

When tracking is available:
- App store visits attributable to the carousel.
- Installs attributable to the carousel.
- Signups.
- Trial starts.
- Paid subscriptions.
- Revenue.
- Install-to-trial and trial-to-paid conversion rates.

Use tracked links, platform attribution tools or campaign-specific reporting where possible.

Do not claim exact carousel attribution when it cannot be verified.

### Secondary content metrics

- Reach and impressions.
- Saves.
- Shares.
- Comments.
- Profile visits.
- Link clicks.
- Swipes or completion metrics when the platform provides them.

Interpret engagement in the context of reach.

A carousel with fewer views but more attributable installs may be more valuable than a viral carousel with no business impact.

### Learning log

For each carousel, record:
- Date and platform.
- Character.
- Story category.
- Hook.
- Number of slides.
- CTA.
- Test hypothesis.
- Performance results.
- Confidence and attribution limitations.
- Recommendation for the next test.

Do not identify a winning format from one isolated post.

Look for repeatable patterns across multiple posts and comparable conditions.

## 15. Production Workflow

### Stage 1: Research and concept

Select a recurring audience problem.

Write:
- Audience.
- Problem.
- Story angle.
- Character.
- Work task.
- Time window.
- Stakes.
- Intended emotional takeaway.
- Why FOCO fits the moment.

### Stage 2: Story approval

Generate the slide-by-slide written story before producing the full carousel.

Check:
- Authenticity.
- Narrative flow.
- Distinctiveness from previous posts.
- Realistic timeline.
- Natural FOCO integration.
- Potential conversion motivation.

### Stage 3: Visual production

Use the approved character reference.

Create all slides using the approved design system.

### Stage 4: Quality assurance

Run automated checks where available:
- Overlapping elements.
- Text outside safe margins.
- Missing assets.
- Incorrect image dimensions.
- Inconsistent slide counts.
- Visual continuity.

Then manually review every slide at actual mobile viewing size.

Check:
- Readability.
- Character consistency.
- Chronological consistency.
- Text accuracy.
- Product accuracy.
- Emotional coherence.
- Platform interface safe zones.
- Caption and CTA alignment.

### Stage 5: Delivery

Deliver:
- Complete carousel.
- Instagram caption.
- TikTok caption.
- Relevant hashtags.
- Test hypothesis.
- Suggested publishing considerations.
- Tracking identifier.

Email the deliverables only through an authorized, connected delivery workflow.

Commit and push to `carousel-tool` only when repository access is configured and authorized.

Never report delivery, saving, committing or pushing as successful without confirmation.

### Stage 6: Performance review

After publication, request available performance data.

Record results in the relevant creative log, including `jess.md` if that is the configured tracking file.

Use results to refine future stories and tests.

## 16. Quality Scoring

Before finalizing, evaluate each carousel on a 1–5 scale:

| Criterion | What to evaluate |
|---|---|
| Hook strength | Would the target audience stop and read? |
| Authenticity | Does it sound and feel like a believable experience? |
| Specificity | Is there a concrete task, situation and consequence? |
| Narrative momentum | Does each slide earn the next swipe? |
| Emotional recognition | Will the audience recognize themselves? |
| Product relevance | Does FOCO solve a specific initiation problem in the story? |
| Product accuracy | Are capabilities and UI represented truthfully? |
| Visual readability | Can each slide be understood quickly on mobile? |
| Conversion clarity | Is the next action understandable and compelling? |
| Originality | Is it meaningfully different from recent carousels? |

Maximum score: 50.

Target: 42 or higher.

Scores are editorial judgments, not guarantees of commercial performance.

Any critical failure involving fabricated evidence, incorrect app functionality, misleading claims or unreadable text must be fixed regardless of score.

## 17. Non-Negotiable Rules

1. Never prioritize aesthetics over story clarity.
2. Never use fabricated user testimonials.
3. Never invent Reddit quotes.
4. Never present speculation as proven performance.
5. Never make medical promises.
6. Never promise unavailable FOCO features.
7. Never force FOCO into an unrelated story.
8. Never recycle the same narrative with only different words.
9. Never mix visual identities within a carousel.
10. Never use fake reviews, pricing or urgency.
11. Never claim an experiment succeeded without relevant results.
12. Never report external actions as completed unless confirmed.
13. Always preserve approved character continuity.
14. Always make mobile readability a priority.
15. Always optimize for qualified users and measurable business outcomes, not vanity metrics alone.

---

## 18. Success Definition

A successful FOCO carousel accomplishes three things:

**Recognition:** "That's exactly what happens to me."

**Belief:** "That first step actually feels doable."

**Action:** "I want to try this with my own task."

The long-term goal is to build a recognizable series of human stories that earns attention, establishes familiarity and consistently introduces qualified people to FOCO.

The winning format is determined by repeated, measurable audience behavior, not by how impressive the creative looks internally.
