What is the Aligned app?

Aligned is an AI coach in an app. Its App Store listing is "Aligned: AI Coach & Planner," published by GM LAP LIMITED, and it is also on Google Play.

The pitch is simple: whatever is on your mind, you ask the coach. As the listing puts it:

"Sometimes you'll get a plan. Sometimes one small thing. Sometimes just company."

It covers home, work, family and health, not just productivity. That breadth is the point, and also the trade-off, which we'll get to.

As of October 2026 it holds a 4.3 rating from 944 reviews on the US App Store and 4.1 stars on Google Play.

How does Aligned work day to day?

You open a conversation and say what's going on. The coach replies with a plan sized to the problem, then tracks the goals and routines you agree on.

There are four ways in:

  • Text chat. Type a messy thought, get a structured answer back.
  • Voice calls. A recent update lets the coach create goals, tasks and routines during the call, so the plan updates while you talk.
  • Photos. The listing's own example: send a photo of your pantry and the coach tells you the floor is the real problem, start there, twenty minutes.
  • Check-ins and reminders. "Added to your 6:45 list, with a nudge the night before" is the kind of follow-up it promises.

You also choose a coach personality: calmer or more direct, practical or encouraging. Whoever you pick shows up the same way every day.

Transcripts can now be saved as PDFs, which is useful if you want to bring a conversation to a real therapist or ADHD coach.

Is Aligned actually built for ADHD?

Not specifically. Aligned's current listing and website describe a general life coach and don't mention ADHD at all.

ADHD adults still find it because the problems it targets are ADHD-shaped: overwhelm, not knowing where to start, forgetting the small daily things, and drifting from goals.

That matters for expectations. An app designed around executive function, the brain's self-management system for planning, starting and switching tasks, makes different choices than a general coach does.

Inflow, for example, is built on ADHD-specific CBT lessons (see our Inflow review). Aligned is a flexible conversation partner that happens to help with a lot of ADHD friction.

What does Aligned get right for ADHD brains?

Its biggest strength is that it lowers the cost of asking for help. You don't fill in a planner, pick categories or build a system first.

It accepts input the way ADHD thoughts arrive

Thoughts show up as a jumble, half a sentence, or a photo of a room you can't face. Aligned takes all three, which works a lot like a guided brain dump.

It answers with one place to start

The good replies don't hand you a 12-step plan. They name the first move ("the floor, twenty minutes, nothing else"), which is exactly how small a first step should be for an overwhelmed brain.

It gives permission to stop

One example in the listing: "Honestly, I'm just done today." The coach replies, "Then nothing today," and offers to move tomorrow back a day.

For people who carry a lot of shame about unfinished lists, a tool that doesn't punish a bad day is worth a lot.

It remembers so you don't have to

Goals, routines and nudges are tracked for you. That offloads some of the working memory load that makes "I'll remember later" fail so often with ADHD.

Where does Aligned fall short?

Its limits mostly come from being a conversation, not a doing tool.

You still have to start the conversation

Opening the app and explaining what's wrong is itself a task. On a day of real task paralysis, that first message can be as hard as the thing you're avoiding.

A good plan isn't the same as starting

Based on its listing and user reviews, Aligned's strength is naming where to begin. But knowing the first step and doing it are separate problems, and the second one is the core of a task initiation deficit.

Once the chat ends, you are on your own with the plan. There is no timer running beside you and nothing that keeps you on the step you agreed to.

Pricing is hard to read

The App Store lists weekly, monthly, quarterly and annual plans at several price points. The App Store shows several prices for the same plan length, so check the exact plan and renewal date before you confirm.

It is coaching, not therapy

Aligned says plainly that it "is not a medical service and does not provide diagnosis or treatment." That is the honest position, and it lines up with how CHADD describes ADHD coaching: practical daily-living support, not treatment of anxiety, depression or other conditions.

The American Psychological Association's health advisory goes further, warning that AI chatbots and wellness apps currently lack the scientific evidence and regulation to be relied on for mental health care.

Is Aligned legit and safe to use?

Yes, it's a real, actively updated app with 944 App Store reviews and a clear "not a medical service" disclaimer. Positive reviews mostly describe practical help getting unstuck:

"I have gotten insights and tactical recommendations that completely break through blockers." (5-star App Store review)
"This App has helped me to process my hangups with cleaning / tidying / organizing." (5-star App Store review)

The complaints that do appear focus on customer service, so keep your receipt and renewal date handy.

Two things to weigh before you share a lot with it:

  • Privacy. Its App Store privacy label lists data linked to you, including user content, contact info, location and usage data. Think about what you type and photograph.
  • Crisis support. No AI coach is a safe place for a mental health emergency. If you are in crisis in the US, call or text 988.

Evidence on ADHD apps in general is thin. A 2017 study in JMIR mHealth and uHealth found popular ADHD apps may not meet the complex needs of young people with ADHD, so treat any app as a support tool alongside care, not a substitute for it.

Aligned vs FOCO: which one fits your problem?

They solve neighboring problems: Aligned helps you figure out what to do, and FOCO helps you start doing it.

The two can work together. Use Aligned when you need to untangle a week or a feeling, then put the concrete task into FOCO when it's time to actually begin.

FOCO is built around the moment after the plan: the task sits in your calendar, one tap breaks it into steps small enough to start, and focus mode runs a timer with ambient music and an AI companion so it feels like someone is working alongside you.

Who should try Aligned, and who should skip it?

Try Aligned if:

  • You think best out loud and want something to talk to at 11pm.
  • Your stuck points are big and fuzzy: a messy house, a hard conversation, a week with no shape.
  • You want gentle accountability across many areas of life, not just work tasks.

Skip it, or pair it with something else, if:

  • You already know what to do and the problem is starting. See our task initiation strategies and the best apps for task paralysis.
  • You need evidence-based treatment for ADHD, anxiety or depression. That is a job for a clinician, as NIMH's ADHD overview outlines.
  • Recurring subscriptions are a known ADHD tax for you. Weekly billing adds up fast.

FAQ

Is the Aligned app good for ADHD?

It can help with ADHD overwhelm, especially if talking things through helps you think. It isn't ADHD-specific, though, and it won't stay with you while you do the task.

How much does Aligned cost per month?

The US App Store lists monthly plans at $19.99 to $29.99. There are also weekly ($9.99), quarterly ($39.99) and annual ($49.99 to $149.99) options.

Is Aligned free?

It's free to download, but full coaching requires a subscription. Check which plan you're signing up for before confirming.

Does Aligned work on Android?

Yes. Aligned is on both the App Store and Google Play.

What's the difference between Aligned and FOCO?

Aligned is a conversation that ends in a plan. FOCO is a task app built for the moment after the plan, when the first step still won't start.

The bottom line

Aligned is a legit, actively updated AI coach that's built for turning a jumble of worry into a first step. For many ADHD adults, that alone is worth trying.

Just know what it is: a coach you talk to, not a tool that sits with you while you work. If your hardest moment is the one after you know what to do, pair the plan with something built for starting.
