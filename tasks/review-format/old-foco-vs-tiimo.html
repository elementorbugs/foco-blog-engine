What is Tiimo?

Tiimo is a visual day-planner app built specifically for neurodivergent brains, with real roots in executive function research.

The core feature is a visual timeline. You schedule tasks onto a day-long view with color-coded blocks, icons, and time estimates, so the day has a shape you can see instead of a list you have to hold in your head.

Pro unlocks AI task breakdown (splitting a task into steps directly on the timeline), custom home-screen widgets, and more advanced scheduling. Pro runs around $6/month after a 7-day free trial.

Tiimo also includes a focus timer, mood tracking, and streak systems for finished blocks. It's built as an all-day companion, not a single-purpose tool.

What is FOCO?

FOCO skips the day-level view entirely and starts at the task you're currently stuck on. You enter one task, and FOCO breaks it into small first steps with time estimates.

Saved tasks also get reminders, so a step you're not ready for yet doesn't just vanish once you close the app.

There's no timeline to build, no color to pick, no icon to choose. The app stays with you through the steps with a timer, ambient focus music, and an AI companion on screen, closer to working alongside someone than checking off a planner.

The trade-off is real. FOCO won't show you your week as a visual timeline, and has no mood tracking. It solves one specific gap, not the whole day.

Which is better for starting a task right now?

For pure task initiation, FOCO wins, mostly because it removes a step Tiimo requires.

Before Tiimo's AI breakdown can help you, you first build or open a schedule, which for someone new to the app means choosing colors, icons, and a layout. The Russell Barkley executive function model frames ADHD as weaker self-regulation, and asking that same system to design a visual schedule before it can help you start spends exactly the resource the app is meant to save.

FOCO has no design step. You open it, enter the task, and the breakdown starts immediately.

Once Tiimo is set up and you're used to it, its own AI breakdown works well and puts steps directly on your timeline with time slots attached. The gap is entirely in the setup, not the breakdown quality.

Which is better for seeing the shape of your day?

This isn't close. Tiimo wins outright, and FOCO isn't built to compete here at all.

Color-coded blocks, icons, and a scrollable timeline give you a spatial sense of the day that a plain list cannot. For people who struggle with working memory leaks, seeing the day laid out visually can prevent tasks from silently falling off a mental list.

FOCO shows exactly one task at a time and nothing else. If day-level structure is your actual problem, FOCO won't help with that part.

How do the free options compare?

Both are paid subscriptions. FOCO starts with a 7-day free trial, then $9/month or $59/year.

Tiimo's free tier covers the basic planner, to-do list, and focus timer on iOS/Android. Its AI task breakdown and web app both require Pro, at roughly $7.99/month or $79.99/year.

According to the National Institute of Mental Health, ADHD is a chronic condition, so the cost comparison matters over years, not weeks. Neither app's core AI feature is free long-term; the real question is which day-to-day format fits, not which is cheaper.

When should you pick Tiimo?

Pick Tiimo when any of these are true.

You're a visual thinker who has tried text-based to-do lists and they don't stick. The timeline format is the actual differentiator here.

You have a routine you want to maintain, not just an emergency to solve. Tiimo is built for daily rhythm: mornings, work blocks, evenings.

You'll open the app daily and the ~$8/month is trivial against that use. If you'll use it twice a month, the subscription isn't worth it yet.

When should you pick FOCO?

Pick FOCO when any of these are true.

The setup itself is where you get stuck. If choosing colors and building a schedule feels like its own task you'll never finish, FOCO skips that step.

Your problem is one specific task, not the shape of your whole day. A single stuck task doesn't need a visual timeline to solve it.

You want the session to feel like company, not a planner. Tiimo is built to show you a plan; FOCO is built to feel like someone working alongside you while you do the thing.

You want to try manual task tracking at no cost first, before deciding whether the AI breakdown is worth $9/month to you.

FAQ

Is Tiimo worth the ~$8/month?

It depends on usage. If you open Tiimo daily and reschedule blocks through the week, that's strong value.

If you set it up and forget it, you're paying for an unused tool. The 7-day trial is the best way to predict your own usage before subscribing.

Does Tiimo have a free plan?

Yes, but it's limited to the basic planner, to-do list, and focus timer. The AI task breakdown, custom widgets, and advanced scheduling require Pro.

Is FOCO a replacement for Tiimo?

No. FOCO doesn't do visual day planning, mood tracking, or a timeline view. It solves a narrower problem, starting a specific stuck task, that Tiimo's day-level design doesn't directly target.

Does Tiimo work for non-ADHD users?

Yes. Tiimo was built for neurodivergent users, but the visual planner format works for anyone who prefers spatial scheduling over text lists.

Can I use FOCO and Tiimo together?

Yes. Tiimo for the shape of the week, FOCO for the specific task that's stuck right now. They don't integrate directly, but they solve different layers of the same day and don't conflict.

The bottom line

Tiimo and FOCO answer different questions. Tiimo answers "what does my day look like." FOCO answers "how do I start this one thing."

If you've never had a plan stick because you couldn't see the shape of your time, Tiimo's visual approach may be the one that finally works, and it's worth the setup and the subscription.

If the plan is already clear and you still can't start, no timeline fixes that. That's what FOCO is built for, and you can try it free for 7 days.
