Structured is a general time-blocking planner, Apple-only, cheaper (~$3/mo vs ~$6/mo), and adds AI dictation plus an Energy Monitor. What is Tiimo?

Tiimo is a visual day-planner built specifically for neurodivergent users, with design choices rooted in executive function research.

The core screen is a scrollable timeline. Tasks appear as colored blocks with icons, so your day has a visible shape instead of a bare list.

Pro unlocks AI task breakdown, custom home-screen widgets, and deeper scheduling options. Pro costs around $6 per month with a 7-day free trial.

Tiimo also includes a built-in focus timer and mood tracking, both active on the free tier in a limited form.

What is Structured?

Structured is a time-blocking planner built around a single clean timeline of your day.

You drag tasks into blocks of time rather than just listing them. The visual result looks similar to Tiimo's timeline, but the app was not built with a neurodivergent audience as its primary design target.

Structured has become popular with ADHD and student communities anyway, largely through word of mouth on social platforms, because the visual format happens to work well for time-blind brains.

Pro adds AI voice dictation (speak a task instead of typing it), calendar sync, and an Energy Monitor that flags when your schedule looks likely to cause burnout.

Structured runs on iOS, iPadOS, macOS, and Apple Watch. There is no Android or Windows version, which rules it out entirely for mixed-device households.

Which one is actually built for ADHD?

Tiimo, without much debate.

Tiimo's marketing, onboarding, and feature choices are explicitly aimed at neurodivergent brains. Mood tracking exists because emotional regulation is a real part of the ADHD and autism experience, not a generic wellness add-on.

Structured was not designed around ADHD specifically. Its popularity in ADHD circles is a side effect of good visual design, not an intentional feature set.

That distinction matters less than it sounds. A tool built with you in mind is not automatically better than one that just happens to fit.

Plenty of ADHD adults get more consistent use out of Structured's simpler screen than Tiimo's fuller one, because executive function struggles often respond better to fewer choices, not more features.

Which handles time blindness better?

Both apps exist to solve time blindness, the difficulty ADHD brains have sensing how much time has actually passed or how long something will take.

Structured's single-timeline view is arguably the more direct answer. One line, one day, blocks sized to actual duration. There is no toggling between views.

Tiimo's timeline does the same job but surrounds it with more: a to-do list view, a focus timer, mood check-ins. For some people that context helps. For others it's one more screen to get lost in.

If time blindness is your single biggest struggle and you want the least visual noise possible, Structured's simplicity wins. If you want time-awareness paired with in-the-moment focus support, Tiimo's extra layers earn their place.

Which is cheaper, and does it matter?

Structured's Pro tier runs roughly half the price of Tiimo's, around $3 a month against Tiimo's $6.

Over a year that gap is small in absolute terms, roughly $36. Over several years it adds up, and pricing on both apps changes periodically, so verify current numbers on Structured's site and Tiimo's site before subscribing.

Price should not be the deciding factor if the two apps' feature sets point clearly toward one or the other. A cheaper tool you abandon in a month costs more than a pricier one you use daily.

Which one works on my devices?

This is the fastest way to eliminate one option.

Structured is Apple-only: iOS, iPadOS, macOS, and Apple Watch. If anyone in your household, or you yourself, uses Android or Windows for anything related to your planning, Structured cannot follow you there.

Tiimo covers iOS, Android, watchOS, and a web app. It is the only one of the two that works across a mixed-device life.

If you're fully inside Apple's ecosystem, this difference disappears. If you're not, it decides the comparison before any other feature matters.

Is there a better option for getting started, not just planning?

Both Tiimo and Structured are excellent at showing you the shape of a day you've already decided to do.

Neither is built for the specific moment before that: staring at a task you know you need to do and not being able to make yourself begin. That's task initiation deficit, and it's a different problem from time blindness or scheduling.

A gorgeous timeline does not help if you cannot get past the first two minutes of a task on it.

FOCO is built around that narrower moment. It breaks one stuck task into small steps, the way task paralysis usually needs, and stays with you through the steps instead of just displaying them on a timeline.

If your problem is seeing your day, Tiimo or Structured will serve you well. If your problem is starting what's already on the page, that's a narrower need worth solving separately.

FAQ

Is Structured really cheaper than Tiimo?

Yes, in most current pricing. Structured Pro runs close to half of Tiimo Pro's monthly cost. Both companies adjust pricing occasionally, so check each app's official site for the current numbers before subscribing.

Does Structured work on Android?

No. Structured is Apple-only across iOS, iPadOS, macOS, and Apple Watch. There is no Android or Windows app, which is the single biggest limitation for mixed-device households.

Which app is better for autistic adults?

Tiimo, since it is explicitly designed with neurodivergent users including autistic adults in mind. Structured was not built with that audience as a primary design target, even though many autistic and ADHD users use it successfully.

Do either of these apps break down tasks into steps?

Tiimo's Pro tier includes AI task breakdown integrated into the schedule. Structured focuses on time-blocking existing tasks rather than breaking a large task into smaller ones.

What is Structured's Energy Monitor?

It's a feature that reviews your planned day and flags patterns that look likely to lead to burnout, such as back-to-back blocks with no rest. Tiimo does not have an equivalent feature.

Can I try both before committing?

Yes. Both apps offer free tiers or trials. Since the two are built around a similar visual concept, a week with each on the same real week of tasks is the most reliable way to see which interface your brain actually keeps opening.

Is a visual timeline actually better for ADHD than a to-do list?

For many ADHD adults, yes, because working memory struggles make it hard to hold an abstract list in mind. Seeing time as physical blocks gives the brain something concrete to track instead. This is not universal, and some ADHD adults do better with simple text lists.

Do I need Tiimo or Structured if I already use FOCO?

They solve different problems. FOCO focuses on breaking down and starting a specific stuck task. Tiimo and Structured focus on laying out your whole day visually. Some people use FOCO for the moment of starting and a timeline app for the rest of the day.

The Bottom Line

Pick Tiimo if you want a tool built explicitly for a neurodivergent brain, need Android support, or want mood tracking and a focus timer built in.

Pick Structured if you're fully in the Apple ecosystem, want the cleanest possible timeline, and want to pay less for it.

If your real problem is starting the task that's already sitting on either timeline, that's a narrower problem than either app solves, and worth addressing separately.
