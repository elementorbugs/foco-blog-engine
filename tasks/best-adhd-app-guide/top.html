The best ADHD app depends on where your day breaks down. Pick FOCO, the app we make, if you know what to do and still can't start. Pick Tiimo or Structured to see your day, Opal or ScreenZen to stop phone pull, and Focusmate to work next to someone. Every pick below comes from our 40 app reviews.

Which ADHD app should you pick?

There is no single best ADHD app, because ADHD shows up differently from person to person. Match the app to the part of your day that breaks down most often.

  • You know what to do and can't start: FOCO, the app we make. It breaks a stuck task into small timed steps, and focus mode keeps one step on screen with a timer and an AI companion. It costs $59/year with a 7-day free trial, or $9/month (more apps for task paralysis).
  • Breaking the task down is enough: Goblin Tools splits a task into steps for free on the web, and the iPhone app is a $1.99 one-time purchase (FOCO vs Goblin Tools).
  • You lose track of your day: Tiimo shows a gentle visual timeline with a countdown, and Structured lays your day out as time blocks. Both have free versions (Tiimo review, Structured review).
  • Your head is too full to plan: Ellie turns a brain dump into a time-blocked day, and Recordo turns spoken brain dumps into tasks and reminders (Ellie review, Recordo review).
  • Your phone keeps pulling you away: Opal blocks apps on a schedule and has a free plan, and ScreenZen is free and makes you wait a moment before an app you picked opens. If you override every software limit, Brick is a $59 device: your apps stay locked until you tap your phone on it again (best app blockers).
  • Your computer is the problem: Cold Turkey Blocker blocks websites for free on Windows and Mac, and Pro is $45 once (Cold Turkey review).
  • You work better with someone there: Focusmate pairs you with one other person by video, with 3 free sessions a week. Flow Club runs hosted group sessions for $40 a month (Focusmate review, Flow Club review).
  • You need a timer, not a system: Pomofocus is a free Pomodoro timer in your browser with no setup. Llama Life puts a countdown on every task, at $39/year after a 7-day trial (Pomofocus review, Llama Life review).
  • Your routines fall apart: Routinery runs a routine one timed step at a time, with voice guidance. The free version covers 2 routines (Routinery review).
  • Streaks make you quit: Finch tracks small self-care goals with a pet and no penalty for missed days. Habitica turns habits into a game, and the full app works for free (Finch review, Habitica review).
  • Silence makes it harder to focus: Endel has a free edition with fewer soundscapes. Brain.fm has no free plan and costs $14.99/month or $99.99/year (Endel review, Brain.fm review).
  • Your work calendar runs your day: Reclaim AI protects focus time on Google Calendar or Outlook and has a free plan. Sunsama guides a daily plan built from your work tools for $22 a month (Reclaim review, Sunsama review).
  • You want to learn coping skills: Inflow is a CBT-based program. Without coaching, the App Store lists it at $22.49/month or $95.99/year (Inflow review).
  • You run a household: RemindHer is a shared family calendar where you speak a task and it lands on everyone's schedule (family calendar apps).
  • Your child has ADHD: Joon turns a child's daily routines into a game, and families can use a free version (Joon review).
  • You want a calm to-do list with no subscription: Things 3 is a one-time purchase, $9.99 on iPhone and $49.99 on Mac, for Apple devices only (Things 3 review).
