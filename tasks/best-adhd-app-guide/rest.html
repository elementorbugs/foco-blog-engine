What makes an ADHD app actually work?

Five patterns separate ADHD-friendly apps from generic productivity tools with "ADHD" added to the marketing.

Low setup: If an app asks for a long setup before you can add your first task, the setup itself can become the thing you put off. The best ADHD apps work on the first open.

External structure, not more decisions: The Russell Barkley executive function model frames ADHD as a problem of self-regulation. Apps that supply structure from the outside help, and apps that hand you more decisions tend to get in the way.

Help with time: Many adults with ADHD find it hard to sense how long things take, often called time blindness. Countdowns, time estimates and visual timelines put the clock where you can see it.

Kindness on bad days: ADHD symptoms vary from day to day. Apps that punish a missed day with broken streaks or guilt notifications make bad days worse.

One job done well: An app that does one thing well is easier to keep using than one that tries to run your whole life.

Are free ADHD apps any good?

Yes, several are. ScreenZen is free, Habitica's full app works for free, Pomofocus needs no account, and Structured, Finch and Tiimo all have free versions.

Watch for apps that are free to download but need a subscription to do anything. Our reviews found this with Clarify, Brainway and Wisey.

Which ADHD apps should you be careful with?

Systems you build yourself: Tools like Notion can do almost anything, which is the trap. Building the perfect system can turn into the task you do instead of the real one.

Apps with harsh streaks: If missing a day costs you a streak or sends a guilt notification, the app hurts most on the days you need it.

Vague "AI": If the marketing leans on AI without saying what it does, look for a concrete feature list before you pay.

Paywalls after onboarding: Some apps walk you through a long quiz before showing the price. Check the price in the store listing first.

Kids and teens

Most apps on this page, FOCO included, are built for adults. For children under 6, the CDC notes that the American Academy of Pediatrics recommends parent training in behavior management as the first line of treatment, so an app is a support at most.

Teens can use the same simple setups adults do: a calendar with reminders, a timer, and one place to break big assignments into steps. A parent helping with setup often matters more than which app you choose.

Can an ADHD app replace medication or therapy?

No. ADHD apps help you organize and start what you've decided to do, on top of whatever else you're doing.

According to the National Institute of Mental Health, ADHD treatment for adults can include medication, psychotherapy such as cognitive behavioral therapy, or both. Apps don't replace any of that.

If you've tried apps for months and still struggle every day, that is a reason to talk to a clinician, not to look for a better app. CHADD has background on how common ADHD is in adults.
